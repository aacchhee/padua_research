## Exact values, zero mask and the removable exception

At c=(-1,-1) every reflected kernel copy agrees. Set X=-a_j,Y=-b_k.
The corrected quotient on the odd translated board gives, away from a
collision, `G=2*tau_j*(1-b_k)/(b_k-a_j)`. Coprimality of n and n+1
allows equal a_j,b_k only at00 and(n,m); the latter has odd checkerboard
parity and is not a node. Thus the only permitted collision is00.

The numerator vanishes exactly when k=0. Except for00, even parity then
forces j positive even. Every k>0 value is nonzero. The sign of its
denominator is exactly the sign of `m*j-n*k`; on the permitted index
set this is positive for j>=k and negative for j<k. No floating zero
or sign threshold is needed.

At00, the corrected x-mode matters. Summation over second modes leaves
first-mode totals `tau` at a=0,n and `2*tau` at1<=a<n. Hence
`G(pi,pi)=2n*tau`, so the weight1/(2nm) gives `L_00=tau/m`, not zero.
The second first-angle derivative is
`-tau*[2 sum_(a=1)^(n-1)a^2+n^2]`, and the second second-angle
derivative is `-2*tau*sum_(b=1)^n b^2`. Their weighted values are
exactly the two removable gradients in part1.

## Inward linearity and exact zero contribution

For a finite sum of absolute polynomial values the one-sided first
variation is the signed gradient sum on nonzero values plus the
absolute directional gradients on zero values. Every zero gradient here
has equal components and a common sign depending only on n. Therefore
on s,t>=0 its contribution is `|L_x|*(s+t)`. The full variation is
exactly `s*A_n+t*B_n`, stronger than the generic convexity observation.

Endpoint weighting is retained in the zero contribution. Writing j=2r
gives `(1/m)sum epsilon_(2r)csc^2(r*pi/n)`. Pairing r with n-r,
including the half weight at r=n/2 for even n, yields
`(n^2-1)/(6m)=(n-1)/6`. The finite csc-squared sum follows by
differentiating the roots-of-unity sine product and taking its regular
limit, as stated. Combining this with the sign-adjusted00 derivatives
gives exactly `-(n^2+2)/(6m)` and `-(n+2)/6` in the two slope sums.
On I, `sign(L)*L_axis=|L|*(L_axis/L)`, which verifies the q_x,q_y
normalization in equation (2).

The other nonnode corner is transported by x reflection for even n

physical inward interpretation is preserved under that transport.

## Aggregate endpoint derivative bound

For the univariate degree-n Lobatto Lagrange polynomial ell_i, factor
its roots relative to x=-1. For i<n it contains x+1 and every remaining
root lies strictly to the right. Its nonzero Taylor coefficients
therefore alternate, with denominator sign(-1)^i. This gives
`sign ell_i^(r)(-1)=(-1)^(n+r+i)` for1<=r<=n. For i=n the same
formula reduces to(-1)^r. Differentiating the exact Lagrange expansion
of T_n proves the endpoint functional norm equality in equation (5).

The Chebyshev differential equation yields the written recurrence
`(2r-1)T_n^(r)(1)=[n^2-(r-1)^2]T_n^(r-1)(1)` and hence the
upper bound n^(2r). The tensor argument may use two copies of this
degree-n univariate grid: it applies to any polynomial of separate
degree at most n and does not require the Padua second mesh of degree m.
Thus the displayed n^(2r+2s) constant is valid in both coordinates.

For each fixed mixed derivative choose one signed cardinal polynomial
whose coefficient signs match those derivatives at c. Its derivative
there is the sum of their absolute values, and its supremum norm is
at most Lambda_n. Applying the tensor functional bound proves (6).
The signed polynomial may depend on the derivative order; the proof
does not need one simultaneous sign choice for every order. Orders
above the polynomial degree vanish. No external endpoint/Markov theorem
is required by this argument.

## Taylor bound, sign changes and the conditional radius

The finite Taylor remainder, summed using (6), is at most
`Lambda_n*[exp(n^2 H)-1-n^2 H]`. Terms of order0 and1 are removed
exactly; the exponential only enlarges the remaining finite sum.

For a nonzero corner value l, the algebraic inequality
`|z|<=(z^2+l^2)/(2|l|)` with z=l+g.h gives the quadratic correction
`(g.h)^2/(2|l|)`. This is valid for either sign of z, including
after a nonzero cardinal crosses zero. Zero corner values contribute
`|g.h|` directly. Consequently the use of a frozen nonzero sign in
the linear term does not assume sign stability. Cauchy-Schwarz gives
`(g.h)^2<=H^2*(g_x^2+g_y^2)`, proving precisely equation (3).

This resolves the main adverse case identified before the final source
was read: a Hessian estimate for one fixed signed polynomial alone would
not control changing cardinal signs. The actual aggregate inequality
does control them through the weighted energy W_n.

Under the explicit hypothetical bounds in (8), put z=n^2 H. For
0<=z<=1 the excess is at most
`Lambda_c*[-a*z+(b+e*K)*z^2/2]`. Thus
`kappa=min(1,a/(b+e*K))` gives descent at least
`(a/2)*n^2*Lambda_c*H` for0<H<=kappa/n^2. Every factor in the
conditional implication is correct. The argument supplies no proof of
its uniform slope and energy premises.

The direction1 global estimate and quadratic-logarithmic corner growth
give an eventual bounded Lambda_n/Lambda_c. Actual-corner phase
