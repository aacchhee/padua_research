## Horizontal primitive and compact envelope

For every real s, including collision parameters,

`F_jr(s,0)=[(-1)^j D_n(u,v)-cos s]/2`,
`Fh_jr(s)=[(-1)^r D_n(u,v)+cos s]/2`.

The periodic board is odd, so `(-1)^r=-(-1)^j`. Thus the half kernels
are exact negatives term by term and `Sh_n(s)=S_n(s,0)`. This is a
finite polynomial identity retaining the corrected x mode. It neither
interchanges n and m nor assumes horizontal symmetry of the defect.
The exact horizontal decomposition uses precisely this same primitive.

For the lower comparison on an edge, a continuous g_h has a minimizer
t_* in the fixed nondegenerate interval I. The complementary centers
on that edge have spacing2pi/n. A center can be chosen **inside I**
within2pi/n of t_*, including when t_* is an endpoint of I. At zero
phase Sh=Lambda_c, so the compact-uniform defect theorem supplies the
required lower envelope on each edge separately, uniformly in parity.

For an arbitrary maximizing sequence on either edge, the nearest
complementary center is at phase distance |s|<=pi. It may lie outside I,
but only by pi/n and within one fixed compact subinterval of(0,pi).
The just established lower comparison, nonnegative defect and global
bound give

`sin^2(s/2)*Lambda_c<=C log(n+1)+min_I g_h+o(1)`.

The explicit corner lower bound forces s to zero uniformly for these
maximizers. Applying the local primitive gain and the horizontal defect
limit to exactly the same phase gives `Sh<=Lambda_c+o(1)` and
`Delta_h=g_h(center)+o(1)`. Compact uniform continuity implies
`g_h(center)>=min_I g_h-o(1)`, even for the outside-I centers.
This proves the matching upper envelope on each edge, hence also for
their maximum. No unique maximizing branch or differentiability is used.

The positive compact gap follows from the prior g_h lower bound. The
