## Independent mathematical reconstruction

### Pairwise projection

The corrected-mode normalization is consistent. Writing
`G = 2 (-1)^j H`, each original four-copy pair defect is twice the
corresponding H defect. Thus Qv starts with `w/2` times the sum over the two
first-coordinate signs. Since `w=2 e_j f_k/(nm)`, periodic reflection of
the first mesh yields exactly the coefficient `1/(nm)` in (B), not twice
or half that coefficient. Translating by J changes the parity condition
from `j+k even` to `j+k = J mod 2`. The shifted kernel board remains odd
because `J+K` is odd. Common signs disappear inside each triangle defect.
The identical accounting with the second mesh gives Qh.

The divided-difference numerator (A) is correct, including the minus sign
on `sin(alpha) sin(u)` and plus sign on `sin(beta) sin(v)`. Clearing the
denominator before invoking a collision is essential and is done correctly.
The original polynomial supplies all collision values. Grouping four real
numbers into pairs directly proves `Delta >= Qv,Qh >= 0`.

For Qv, the separation of the two denominator targets is
`2 sin(phi) sin(b)`. A regular separated half therefore bounds the point
defect even when the other half is a polynomial collision. On compact phi
intervals the near-endpoint numerators stay positive for all sufficiently
small phases, uniformly in u. Support then has periodic angular length at
most `4 min(b,pi-b)`, giving the stated endpoint-tail sum
`C_delta [eta+(1+log m)/n]`.

For Qh, positivity of both numerators is unavailable. Their difference is
exactly `-sin(alpha) cos(theta) sin(a_j)`. Outside the closed cosine band,
positive defect requires opposite numerator signs, so each numerator is
bounded by the absolute difference. The separated regular denominator
then gives `C_delta |alpha|`. Inside the band, the periodic angular length
and mesh count give the other two tail terms. This argument retains exact
zeros and collisions and is uniform in the unused macroscopic phi.

On either bulk strip the defect is uniformly bounded. Moving pole tubes
have mesh mass `O(t+1/n+1/m)` for either parity; away from them the rational
expressions have uniform denominator bounds and are equicontinuous. The
periodic/physical rectangle has Riemann density `1/(2 pi^2)`. Its limiting
point defect is `2h`; symmetry of the periodic angle supplies precisely
the inherited `g_v` or `g_h` normalization. The endpoint bounds allow the
successive limits in the stated order. All estimates depend on the compact
coordinate and the small phases, and not on the unused macroscopic position.
The claimed compact-uniform projection limits are supported by this proof.

### Two-phase primitive A and B

The full periodic odd-board normalization and the endpoint-weighted
four-reflection formula (2) agree. The factor
`a=cos((alpha+beta)/2) cos((alpha-beta)/2)` is nonnegative on the full phase
diamond, as required by the global comparison.

For (A), the fixed diagonal strip contains only a bounded number of second
indices per first index. The polynomial derivative bound there sums to
`O(log(n+1))` after normalization, including collisions. Away from the
strip, both denominator sine factors retain constant-factor comparability
under each coordinate shift. Exact subtraction uses numerator `1+cos(v)`
for the first shift and `1+cos(u)` for the second; the two cotangent sums
therefore give the asserted logarithmic cost. The endpoint rows have
normalized cost O(1). No nonzero-alpha folded primitive is substituted.

For (B), the removed family is exactly the potentially anomalous upper
near-diagonal family. Every retained odd pair has L,d at least one and
the bounds `t >= c L/m`, `b >= c d/m` along all small phase paths. For each
fixed signed difference there are at most two pairs per d. In particular,
the Hessian terms sum as

`sum b^(-1)t^(-3) = O(m^4 log m)` and
`sum b^(-2)t^(-2) = O(m^4)`.

Dividing by `nm n^2` gives (8). The cotangent expressions for both sine
channels give the claimed derivative sums. Simultaneous-negation Taylor
pairing cancels the first signed variation; it does not assume absolute
values are differentiable at a zero numerator.

The new sine-alpha channel genuinely changes the sign-flip geometry.
From (9), a negative numerator implies
`|Y| <= C(rho+sqrt(rho x))`; an O(rho)-width assertion alone would be
incorrect. The proof retains the square-root width. If `x <= K rho`,
the normalized harmonic row cost is
`C(rho^2+rho/n) log(n rho+2)`. If `x > K rho`, a sufficiently large fixed K
gives `|Y| <= x/2`, `|X| >= 3x/4`, hence a denominator of order x squared
and cost at most `C rho/x` per contributing term. The row counts give
`C rho^(3/2) + C rho^2 log(2+1/rho) + C rho log(m)/m`, which is O(rho).
The lower harmonic cutoff `max(K rho,pi/n)` covers arbitrarily small rho.
Thus the new sign flips do not invalidate (B).

For the removed family, reflection about pi turns the two-product formula
for D into the difference in (13). The identity
`U_(n+1)-U_n = cos((2n+1)z/2)/cos(z/2)` then proves its displayed
factorization. Finite Fourier sums retain every collision, including

normalizing the O(n) terms, proving the O(rho) excess. Together these
estimates establish (B) and its `O(log^(-2) n)` optimized excess under the
explicit inherited corner lower bound.
