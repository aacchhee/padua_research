The computable-criterion subproblem has an analytic answer in a limited sense. The answer is a finite exact-data criterion — a definition, decidable for represented rational/real-algebraic inputs — with a sufficient first-order theorem. It does not show that the criterion accepts any selection, or that it is efficient or online. The unrestricted theorem that some staircase (or other admissible) selection keeps drift polynomially bounded on a non-product family remains **OPEN**. A sufficient criterion does not prove that any candidate passes it.

The new step is the polynomial surrogate: whatever the computed coefficients are, the recurrence polynomials remain triangular in the monomial order and hence span Π_s. The difference between that surrogate and the stored basis is the forced node recurrence. Full column rank and σ_min(Qhat_s) then convert its residual bound to a **span** bound (2). Near-orthogonality is used only for the simplified form (3). This differs from PD-T032's representation alone and PD-T037/PD-T041's amplification measurements/certificates, which do not by themselves identify the surrogate space or certify drift.

The proposed rule is exact-data and finite: enumerate the 2^(s−1) (C)-staircases, discard χ>χ_0, and minimize the maximum node inverse norm of the **row-normalized** triangular matrix. Accept only below a declared K_0(s). A missing accepted selection is an informative failure to certify. The staircase restriction is not used in proving the first-order bound (6). It serves only the (C)-based implicit-section interpretation, and it excludes the pivot-adaptive rule (PD-T051's best measured one-relation rule). The same bound holds over all 2^(s(s−1)/2) admissible selections, without that interpretation. Rational/algebraic data admit exact computation in principle; unrestricted real data need representation/oracle assumptions. No efficient global or online algorithm is claimed.

For an accepted selection and the declared aggregate local-residual model, the theorem gives

  θ_s(u)≤C_loc u sqrt(N_s−1) χ_0 K_0(s)+O(u²).

This is O(u poly(s)χ) only if those exact-data quantities have independently justified polynomial bounds. The O(u²) remainder and the first-order neighborhood may depend strongly on the measure, the degree, the selection and **the particular perturbation family** (its second-order terms and coefficient-path smoothness). No remainder uniform over all (Hres)-families is asserted; uniformity holds only over a compact, jointly smooth set of families. The exact computed-data certificate is non-asymptotic but requires an evaluated Khat and residual bound. Neither score predicts the sign or occurrence of actual IEEE rounding.

Failures are retained. (C) guarantees acyclic implicit sections, not small inverses; PD-N015 already kills dev, mirror and alt in its measured scopes. PD-T041's large unnormalized entries cannot be silently substituted into a normalized score or promoted into a span lower bound. Minimizing K alone can neglect χ; the χ filter and explicit normalization repair that route without claiming the original idea was novel.
