## Drift and input/output norm

S_s is the exact polynomial evaluation space, P_s its Euclidean orthogonal projector; P_hat_s is the projector onto the computed/perturbed span of the same dimension. The drift is

    theta_s=||(I-P_s)P_hat_s||_2,

the sine of the largest principal angle. This is theta_s itself, not theta_LB(n), which bounds the maximum over stages.

Use the rational monic orthogonal gauge b_0=1, b_k=A_k b_p-sum_(l<k)alpha_kl b_l, d_k=||b_k||^2. Inject epsilon f_k immediately before exact full projection. Define sigma_k^2=||A_k b_p||^2 and

    ||f||_src^2=sum_k ||f_k||_2^2/sigma_k^2.

The span condition kappa_s is the operator norm from this Hilbert source norm to the **spectral** norm of the normal first-order graph. The precise map and proof are [PD-T?drift-codex-01](../../../tracks/bivariate-lanczos/theory/drift-codex-e1-01-span-derivative.md). No irrational basis is computed: the exact rational responses g_kh use frozen recurrence coefficients. The certified bounds from [PD-T?drift-codex-02](../../../tracks/bivariate-lanczos/theory/drift-codex-e1-02-rational-span-bounds.md) are

    Knode^2=max_(h,i) sigma_h^2(1-P_s,ii)sum_k g_kh(z_i)^2/d_k,
    KHS^2=sum_(h,i) sigma_h^2(1-P_s,ii)sum_k g_kh(z_i)^2/d_k,
    Urow^2=max_i sum_(h,k) sigma_h^2 g_kh(z_i)^2/d_k,
    Knode <= kappa_s <= min(KHS,Urow).

Knode is attained for a particular single-node/source impulse; its output is rank one, so it is a lower bound for spectral output. KHS and Urow are upper bounds for the full spectral-output map, not measured estimates of its norm. Urow is an analytic tightening sent to the code developer before receiving comparison results; it changes no target, threshold or verdict rule.

The degree-two-only source set is a cheaper screen. Its upper endpoints cannot upper-bound the full map. The main test uses **all** source columns 1,...,N_s-1. All quantities used for decisions are rational squares and compared exactly. Decimal logarithms are presentation only.

**Clarification before verdict computation:** the reversal clause alone only refutes an assertion that BJ exceeds *each* comparator, not BJ divided by the *best* comparator. Consequently the E1 best-comparator target uses the simultaneous-both reversal (or the second clause) for DEAD; an isolated reversal is reported per-comparator and never closes the best-comparator target. The original interim messages used "a good rule" loosely; this logical distinction is corrected before interpreting numerical output. The unambiguous best-comparator DEAD criterion is U_BJ^2 <100 min(L_alt^2,L_pivot^2), which subsumes simultaneous reversal. Keep isolated reversals as descriptive evidence, not a kill of the best-comparator mechanism.

Intermediate degrees are descriptive. Lower-bound ratios alone cannot kill or validate the condition comparison. Overlapping intervals mean INCONCLUSIVE. Degree-8 fallback does not enable E2. Even SURVIVES would certify only this aggregate-residual, finite-grid comparison; no all-degree or actual-rounding theorem follows.
