\section{Finite certificates and the verification boundary}
\label{sec:certificates}

The analytic argument leaves a finite interval of degrees.  Its
completion requires a cover of a continuum for each degree, together
with a local argument at the intended equality points.  Comparing a
grid maximum with a corner value would not supply either requirement.
The certificates used here have two forms, both based on nonnegative
interpolation weights.

\subsection{Polynomial and phase covers}

For the smaller degrees, the cardinals are enclosed in tensor Bernstein
form on a finite binary subdivision of the physical square.  If
$c_{\ell,ij}$ are their coefficients on a leaf, positivity and partition
of unity give
\[
 \sum_\ell |L_\ell(z)|
 \leq\sum_{i,j}\left(\sum_\ell|c_{\ell,ij}|\right)
                 B_i^d(s)B_j^d(t).
\]
A bound leaf has every coefficient sum strictly below a validated lower
bound for $\Lambda_c(n)$.  A corner leaf permits equality only in the
coefficient at its designated corner and has a strict inequality at
every other coefficient.  The corresponding corner Bernstein weight
equals one only at that vertex.  Thus this second predicate proves
strict inequality throughout the punctured corner leaf.  Reconstructing
the complete closed subdivision proves the global assertion, including
its equality set.  Exact rational coefficient conversion, outward
trigonometric enclosures, and midpoint de Casteljau subdivision preserve
these implications.

For $41\leq n\leq319$ we use the common phase triangle
\[
 \mathcal T=\{(a,b):a,b\geq0,\ a+b\leq1\},
 \qquad S_n^t(a,b)=S_n(\pi a,\pi b).
\]
The following proposition explains what the retained proof must certify.
Its proof does not assume that the certificate producer succeeds.

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of a phase certificate]
\label{prop:certificate-phase-soundness}
Fix $n\geq41$.  Suppose a finite family of closed rational rectangles
covers $\mathcal T$ after intersection with $\mathcal T$.  Suppose also
that it supplies validated same-degree point enclosures, a global norm
bound for the smooth signed kernel sums, and a positive lower bound
$L_c\leq\Lambda_c(n)$.  On the actual far part $\rho\geq1/2$ of every
retained rectangle, require a strict upper comparison $S_n^t<L_c$.
On its actual near part $\rho\leq1/2$, require either $S_n^t<L_c$ or
$S_n^t-\delta<L_c$ with a proved defect floor $\Delta_{JK}\geq\delta>0$
for every admissible noncorner center.  For the actual corner centers
on the near part, instead require strict descent off the origin on the
closed inward diamond $A+B\leq1/2$.  An independently proved analytic
far comparison against $\Lambda_c(n)$ may replace the numerical far
comparison on its entire guarded leaf.  Then
\eqref{eq:argmax-exact-set} holds at degree $n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The signed kernel sums are smooth trigonometric polynomials with
coordinate frequencies at most $1,n/(n+1)$ in $\alpha,\beta$, hence
$\pi,\pi n/(n+1)$ in $a,b$.  Apply a nonnegative Peano
kernel and the trigonometric Bernstein inequality to each complete
signed sum before taking its maximum over signs.  This bounds $S_n^t$
on a rectangle by its vertex bounds plus a quadratic remainder.  The
result is valid even when $S_n$ has a cusp.  Lemma~\ref{lem:argmax-physical-cover}
then transfers a strict primitive comparison to every physical center.
On an actual near point at a noncorner center, the applicable projected
defect supplies the stipulated improvement.  A corner receives the
separate direct descent estimate.  These closed alternatives cover all
points; the reflection argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:argmax-analytic-tail}
identifies and equates the two remaining origins.  Explicit rational
leaf predicates and norm witnesses are in
Appendix~\ref{app:argmax-details}.
\end{proof}

The corner witness below degree $256$ uses three outward evaluations of
the actual Lebesgue function and an interpolation bound in $A^2,B^2$.
It gives a positive multiple of $A^2+B^2$ as the loss, so equality is
allowed at the origin and excluded arbitrarily close to it.  From
degree $256$, \eqref{eq:argmax-corner-loss} supplies this witness.
Some certificates also use an analytic far leaf, valid from degree
$192$, when its entire closed phase rectangle lies in
$\pi/2\leq\rho\leq\pi$.  Such a leaf proves
$S_n<\Lambda_c(n)-3/100$ against the exact corner value.

\subsection{Coverage, arithmetic, and independent checks}

The finite degree inventory is exhaustive:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\hline
Degrees & Proof route\\
\hline
$2$--$7$ & exact polynomial cases and their strict equality checks\\
$8$--$32$ & complete exact or outward Bernstein covers\\
$33$--$40$ & reconstructed Bernstein covers, with two corner leaves\\
$41$--$319$ & complete common-phase certificates\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The checkers reconstruct complete partitions and strict predicates;
they do not accept a producer flag.  Every retained phase proof was
reconstructed locally with exact rational arithmetic, including its
norm witness, defect guards, corner predicate, and source and degree
identities.  Native point evaluation supplies outward endpoints through
the frozen Arb/FLINT/MPFR/GMP implementation.  The trust boundary
includes this implementation, the cardinal normalization and collision
formulas, and the compiler and native runtime.  The analytic tail also
retains the quantitative constant enclosure identified as PD-T271.

All returned degrees underwent separate primary and fresh verification
on the production machine.  Locally, every retained proof was checked;
the full histories and new independent native replays were checked for
the two prescribed sampled degrees $199$ and $238$.  This supplies no
independent local native replay of every residual degree.  The theorem
uses the complete collection of covers under the stated arithmetic and
execution trust, rather than a statistical inference from those samples.
Essential proofs are retained, while full unsampled histories remain on
the production machine under the authorized selective-retention policy.
External retrieval of every original historical byte has not been
verified; pinned source and commands permit regeneration.  The exact
coverage, sample scope, source identities, and retention record are in
Appendix~\ref{app:argmax-verification-record} and the companion
reproducibility notes.

Generic polynomial meshes provide useful norming and validation tools
in related settings; see \cite{Mesh2024}.  Here the global norm follows
from an explicit Peano absorption argument, and the equality
classification requires the additional defect and punctured-corner
predicates.  None of these predicates is replaced by an observed
maximum on a mesh.
