## Shared vertices, phase order and collision removal

On the literal domain P=B intersect {alpha+beta<=pi}, A is nonnegative:
`A=2*cos((alpha+beta)/2)*cos((alpha-beta)/2)`. Thus the stated global
ranges A in[0,2], p,q in[0,1] are valid on P, even when B straddles
the diagonal. A box enclosing only the actual coefficient image on P
is sufficient; the denominator/grid enclosure may still use B.

N2's normalization gives exactly

    F=[A(1+cos v)+q sin v-p sin u]/[2(cos u-cos v)].

The exceptional mode and odd-board parity are unchanged. The four
retained rows include every possible sin(v)=0. On all other rows the
two closed actual pole-index bands have exact width
`(w_alpha+(n/m)*w_beta)/(2pi)<1`. The literal outward-width guard is
still necessary. Each one-unit buffered band contains at most three
integers; adding both seam indices gives at most eight actual samples.
For surviving k, the central and periodic pole images are at least one
grid unit away. In particular no artificial numerator is substituted
at an actual removable collision.

At fixed physical phase every surviving absolute summand is convex in
the same (A,p,q). One common convex decomposition of the actual triple
therefore applies to the entire ordinary-row sum, proving S<=G. The
four-coordinate seed depends on the two cosine coordinates only through
their sum: its affine image is an interval in A times the p,q intervals.
Hence at most eight effective vertices suffice; redundant mixed cosine
endpoints are not additional extrema of this convex problem.

For finite Z, `sup_phi max_z F_z = max_z sup_phi F_z`. Separating the
common D supremum then gives (8). The maximum must remain outside the
row sum. Adding per-row interval uppers in each fixed z accumulator is
valid, but may add phase-dependence overestimation beyond the analytic
relaxation. The text correctly makes no bound on that extra numerical
loss from (13) or (15).

## Thin freezing estimate and constants

For each fixed z, c(v)=A0(1+cos v)+q0 sin v has derivative
`-A0 sin v+q0 cos v`, with absolute value at most
`sqrt(A0^2+q0^2)<=sqrt(5)`. Since the row angle changes by at most
`w_beta/(2m)` from the beta midpoint, adding the full dyadic center
calibration error gives exactly

    epsilon_r,z=L_z*w_beta/(2m)+rho_r,z.

The ideal square-root constant is bounded by sqrt(5); a computed upward
bound must use its actual value, as the text explicitly requires.
The simple A0+q0 choice is valid and at most3. Projection of a chosen
dyadic center to[-5,5] cannot increase its error because the true center
lies in that interval. No transcendental center is assumed exact.

Independently reconstructing the pole-weight bound gives

    W_r <= 2n*H_n/(pi*abs(sin v_r)).

The shifted-grid cotangent distances in each half start at least1 and
are separated by1, giving two harmonic sums per channel. The
constant-numerator cotangent identity supplies the factor
`1/(2*abs(sin v_r))`. Across ordinary rows the sine chord bound gives
`sup_B 1/abs(sin v_r)<=m/(2d_r)`, and each positive integer d_r occurs
at most four times. Thus the sum of separate row suprema is at most
`2m*H_n`, not merely a bound on a single-phase row sum. Consequently
`sum_r W_r<=4N*H_n^2/pi`, also for any successful-row subset.

Reverse triangle inequality then yields

    abs(Ftilde_z-F_z)/N <= (2/pi)*max_r epsilon_r,z*H_n^2
      <= [L_z*w_beta/(pi*m)+2*rho_z/pi]*H_n^2 = E_z.

With the definition (14), each Ftilde_z/N+E_z lies between F_z/N
and F_z/N+2E_z. Taking finite maxima proves exactly

    S <= G <= M <= G+2*max_z E_z.

There is no factor8, no inverse phase-distance factor and no tangent-root
factor in this value estimate. It is not a derivative estimate. A
nonzero arithmetic center radius is a separate term even for a phase
box of zero width.

For the endpoint-pole-only weight, the distance of v_r/2 from pi*Z is
at least `pi*min(r,2m-r-1)/(2m)`. Each positive distance integer occurs
at most twice, giving `sum_r sup_B abs(cot(v_r/2))<=4m*H_n/pi`.
This verifies the constant used for the A term in (20).

## Fixed-root backend, tails and bounded work

With one fixed exact dyadic ctilde and p0 per row/vertex, the numerator
roots are fixed angles. Constant p0=0, exact zero and tangent cases are
covered. For p0>0 the three asin alternatives cover all roots in
[0,2pi], including negative thresholds and seam representatives. Their
exact index width is at most w_alpha/(2pi)<=1/2. The actual outward
band must have width<1, so each band contributes at most one regular
artificial sample. Closed equality points and empty-band cuts remain
necessary. No unbounded root-refinement loop belongs to the count.

The two buffered pole bands, two seam singletons and three numerator
bands give at most eight cores; twelve per channel is conservative.
Their constant signs and wraps follow from continuity on each connected
real-index times phase domain after all zero/pole bands are excluded.
Unknown arithmetic signs or wraps cause failure. Whole-phase offset
balls, and both literal strict pole guards on those balls, remain
mandatory even when exact geometry supplies a stronger margin.

Independent cotangent algebra verifies

    C=(p0-ctilde/sin v)/4, D=(p0+ctilde/sin v)/4.

The actual signed-row T identity is unavailable for these artificial
coefficients. Every signed core and every regular artificial term must
use its own rational/cotangent formula, exactly as specified. C11's
source accepts full offset balls, propagates their radius in its moment
construction, retains both harmonic errors, the separate w' error and
the J16 smooth remainder, and returns failure on invalid strict guards.
Those are the relevant inherited backend properties.

For one complete vertex/channel, the disjoint cores contain at most n
samples and twelve blocks. Thus the smooth error has sample factor n,
the two harmonic tails per block give24 eta/h, and the w' endpoint
tails give h*tau. No block multiplier is added to the smooth sample
count. The artificial coefficient identity

    abs(C)+abs(D)=max(p0,abs(ctilde/sin v))/2
                  <=(1+5/abs(sin v))/2

gives total row weight at most m(1+5H_n). Division by N yields precisely
(21). Signed all-core assembly needs no extra negative-part factor2.
The global maximum uses the largest common vertex allowance, not the
sum over eight allowances. Returned-ball tails are already included;
the audit budget is not added again. In particular J16 uses M_16, not
the smaller J48/J96 remainder, and fixed orders do not imply arbitrary
accuracy at unbounded degree.

Per ordinary row the count is at most8 actual samples,8 vertices,
24 regular artificial samples and192 partial calls. The four retained
rows add4n bounded-work actual primitive evaluations, available through
the C01 finite-ratio contract with finite caps. The total is O(n) scalar
work at fixed orders/precision, with bounded attempts and fallbacks.
Integer bit lengths, elementary argument reduction, coefficient/moment
precision, retries and useful tightness are separate costs.

The unchanged C11 partial backend requires LP64 and n<=2147483647;

Neither source becomes a broader C14 implementation through this proof.

## Common fallback and comparison with C09

Successful rows form one fixed subset for the whole phase box. If any
vertex fails on a row, discarding that row from every vertex accumulator
and replacing its entire actual contribution by a common cap preserves
the common convex decomposition. Its D_r contribution must be replaced
as well. The remaining-row pole and freezing estimates still hold.
An actual polynomial cap is not a cap for an isolated artificial
rational vertex, so replacing just one failed vertex would be unsound;
the frozen text expressly prohibits that operation.

The actual finite polynomial has nonnegative cosine coefficients with
sum2N before the factor1/2, giving abs(F)<=N, row<=nN and S<=2N.
These caps justify finite fallback for valid mathematical inputs, with
any coarse excess additional to the freezing and coefficient budgets.
Invalid/nonfinite or unsupported native input still requires rejection.

Algebraically max_z sum_r H_r(z)<=sum_r max_z H_r(z). If each C09
row rectangle contains every image of the same global coefficient box
through c=A(1+cos v)+q sin v, its convex row maximum dominates each
H_r(z). Matching natural interval constructions satisfy this inclusion;
an independently tightened correlated row rectangle may not. With the
same common direct/fallback rows, this proves

    M <= C09_exact_row_majorant+2*max_z E_z.

Different executable interval dependence, rounding or fallback patterns
are not ordered by this inequality. This limitation is explicit and
necessary, rather than an omitted implementation guarantee.

## Separate global coefficient loss and scope

At fixed actual phase a vertex differs by at most the full box widths.
The numerator difference is bounded by
`Delta_A*(1+cos v)+Delta_q*abs(sin v)+Delta_p`. Dividing by2N and
using the independently checked weights gives

    0 <= G-S <= (4*Delta_A/pi^2+2*Delta_p/pi)*H_n^2
                  +2*Delta_q*H_n/pi.

The A coefficient uses the endpoint cotangent sum, the p coefficient
uses the full W sum, and the q coefficient cancels abs(sin v) before
summing at most2m rows. The common maximum adds no factor8. The
ideal width bounds follow from unit Lipschitz sine/cosine; actual
outward widths must be charged. Near the origin the quadratic cosine
width is correctly isolated from the sine-coordinate widths.

This bound is generally O(w*H_n^2), with no1/n factor. The separate
freeze estimate does not improve it to O((w/n)*H_n^2). The exact
example cos(alpha)+sin(alpha) on[pi/4-h,pi/4+h] has true maximum
sqrt(2) while its coefficient rectangle has maximum
sqrt(2)*(cos h+sin h), with a positive linear term. It refutes the
generic inference that a shared coefficient rectangle preserves every
physical stationary cancellation. It supplies no Padua-specific lower
bound or negative theorem. Both that limitation and the OPEN sharper
Padua loss/tightness question are correctly retained.
