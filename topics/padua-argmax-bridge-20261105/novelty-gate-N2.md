## Exact comparison and verdict before the declared checks

The inherited numerator is already a two-direction edge decomposition in
another notation. Merely rewriting it with csc/cot or CRT is an
**equivalent algebraic form**, and is not pursued as a new mechanism.
The useful narrower continuation is to extend PD-T226's absolute-row
reduction from beta=0, |alpha|<=2/5 to the entire two-phase diamond:

1. Compute every signed row including its beta-dependent term and the
   exceptional x mode.
2. Express all regular negative indices by the symmetric difference of
   two explicit arcs; account separately for polynomial collisions,
   whose values need not be positive away from beta=0.
3. Match PD-T365 on every closed diamond edge, including vertices.

This is a **variant with difference in domain and explicit sign data**
of IDEA-329, serving IDEA-459's same lower-order-profile obligation.
It is not a B,C profile, an error bound, a new polygonal method, or a
claim of external novelty. The falsifiable distinction disappears if
PD-T226 already contains the full beta-dependent signed rows and negative
collision terms; it does not. A wrong exceptional-mode coefficient,
missed collision or failure to reduce to T365 kills the proposed identity.

Cheapest declared check: elementary numerator/finite Fourier algebra,
then a small direct finite-polynomial comparison over both parities,
generic phases, exact collision phases, all four edges and vertices.
No fit or expensive degree scan is authorized by this gate. Signed-row
identities alone do not pass IDEA-329's quantitative repair gate.

The remaining useful test is an explicit uniform approximation to the
resulting moving-endpoint cotangent partial sums, with a proved summed
remainder. B1's conservative half-budget is approximately 2.2e-5 at
n48 through 5.2e-5 at n256, and its measured values do not certify a
continuous margin. A fit without the analytic remainder is disallowed.
