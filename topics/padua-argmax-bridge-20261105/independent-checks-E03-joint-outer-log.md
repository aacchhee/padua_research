## Midpoint error versus omitted endpoint cells

The derivative of g is zero before z_*, smooth after it, and has
one finite jump at z_*. Its total variation is finite for the fixed
alpha. On each centered cell the linear Taylor term integrates to
zero; the remaining integral is bounded by a constant timesh^2
times the derivative variation on that cell, after normalization.
Summing over disjoint cells on each side proves an O_alpha(m^-2)
midpoint error, including the cell containing z_*. An O(m^-1)
midpoint estimate would not determine the leading coefficient;
the supplied BV-derivative argument provides the needed extra order.

At t=0 the left cell union is[3h/2,pi-3h/2] and the right is
[5h/2,pi-h/2]. For a Lipschitz function each omitted endpoint

sin alpha/pi and its two omitted lengths total2h. Subtracting
the normalized missing integrals therefore gives

    (Q_0+Q_1)[g]
       =-(1/pi)(2h)(sin alpha/pi)+O_alpha(h^2)

folded indices work for both parities and all integer n>=32.

## Quantifier order and exact negative conclusion

Assume the proposed absolute bound holds on one fixed nontrivial
near diamond for all degrees beyond some fixed N. Fix any alpha
in that diamond BEFORE passing to n tending to infinity. Multiplying
by n/log n makes the fixed-C term vanish and H/log n tend to1.
The proved expansion then forces

    2sin alpha/pi<=K alpha^2.

Now choose the fixed positive alpha smaller than BOTH r and
1/[2pi(K+1)]. This choice is made after the proposed K is known,
and does not depend on n. The elementary sin alpha>=alpha/2 gives
2sin alpha/pi>=alpha/pi, whereas K alpha^2<alpha/(2pi).
These strict incompatible bounds prove a counterexample degree
exists beyond every prescribed N, even if the O_alpha constant is
large. No uniformity of that constant in alpha is used. Finite C
does not affect the contradiction, and K=0 is included literally.

The result is strictly weaker in scope than rejecting D13: it
concerns ONLY the outer-plus-log component and a proposed linear
term with NO log n factor. D13's original absolute target permits
rho(1+log n)/n and a larger quadratic logarithmic term, and applies
to the COMPLETE sum of row, retained, outer and mesh increments.
The present asymptotic is consistent with accepted E02's absolute
linear-logarithmic allowance and does not invalidate it.

Finally T_n is eventually negative at each fixed positive alpha.
An upper bound without the absolute value can retain that favorable
sign; this proof does not refute it. Its uniform finite-phase
remainder, the regime where alpha is comparable to a mesh step,
and any cancellation against separately studied row increments
remain open obligations. A fixed-degree cusp and this fixed-phase
degree limit have different quantifier orders, so their separate
