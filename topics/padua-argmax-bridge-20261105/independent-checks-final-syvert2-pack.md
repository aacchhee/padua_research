### FOPS-07 — MAJOR: unrelated old unreadable process prevents admission

Root's traceback locates the failure at `assert_no_writers`, reading
`/proc/1/environ`; receipt records `PermissionError(13, 'Permission denied')`.
The scanner currently attempts every same-UID environment regardless of process
age or package instance. The container's older same-UID nondumpable init is

unreadable *possible writer* is correct; treating every preexisting process as
such is unnecessarily broad. The focused source verdict above is now qualified
by this observed operational finding; branch remains WORK.

Recommended bounded repair, subject to source review before another execution:

1. Under the campaign lock, before any worker admission, persist a writer-domain
   record containing the canonical absolute package-instance path, immutable
   runtime-manifest identity, boot identifier and the initiating supervisor's
   `/proc/self/stat` start-time tick. Bind the inherited writer token to the
   manifest and package instance. Never infer authority from process names.
2. Retain the **earliest** such floor across full/small/large runs, resume and
   collection. Never replace it with the current collector/resumer's birth
   tick. For existing output, missing/corrupt/inconsistent records must refuse;
   a boot mismatch must be handled explicitly and fail closed rather than
   compare ticks from different boots. Creating a fresh floor after old workers
   may exist would be unsound.
3. Within the same boot, a process with a readable birth tick **strictly less**
   than that earliest floor is excluded. Equal ticks remain candidates because
   Linux ticks are coarse. All later/equal same-UID candidates retain the
   environment-marker check; unreadable candidate metadata/environment remains
   a refusal. Do not blanket-ignore `PermissionError`.

The exclusion argument is causal: a legitimately admitted pack worker is born
after its initiating supervisor, whose birth is no earlier than the stored
earliest supervisor floor. Descendants cannot be born earlier than their
ancestors. `setsid`, double fork and reparenting change neither their original
birth ticks nor inherited writer marker. Therefore an older process cannot be
a worker admitted under this registered domain. This argument assumes the
pre-admission record is created and retained as above, and uses the same boot's
monotonic tick coordinate. It does not identify arbitrary unregistered programs
that manually write into the package tree.

A `/proc` walk is not an atomic global quiescence proof. Retain exclusive
campaign locking, invalidation of stale quiescent summaries before admission,
completed ownership-scoped cleanup/ECHILD, and refusal of interrupted output;
do not replace these safeguards with the age filter or environment marker.
Verify stable process identity when metadata is read across PID reuse, and
treat disappearance separately from inaccessible possible writers.
