## Convexity, coefficients and upper assembly

At one fixed actual phase, each surviving term is the absolute value of
an affine function of(c0,p0), multiplied by a fixed positive denominator
weight. The row H_r is therefore convex in these two coefficients.
Expressing an actual pair as a convex combination of the rectangle's
four vertices proves H_actual<=max_z H_z. The denominator and grid stay
at that same physical phase. Their subsequent interval enclosure is a
separate step and needs no convexity in v.

The valid global coefficient ranges [-5,5] and [0,1] justify clipping
before extracting exact endpoints. An interval ball must not itself be
treated as a point vertex. Degenerate rectangles and duplicate vertices
cause no change to the argument.

Independent use of the sum/difference cotangent identities gives

    (c0-p0*sin u)/(2(cos u-cos v))
      = (p0-c0/sin v)/4 * cot((u-v)/2)
        +(p0+c0/sin v)/4 * cot((u+v)/2).

Both signs and the factor1/4 are correct. Ordinary rows avoid sin(v)=0
on B. The original correlated signed row T is unavailable for an
artificial vertex, so summing every positive and negative core with its
own sign is essential and is what (12) specifies. E_(r,z) also uses the
relaxed function; only D_r uses the actual finite polynomial.

Each successful C06 ball encloses its full input-offset image, as its
exact rational midpoint moments include an input-radius derivative
bound. Summing signed products and regular direct vertex terms therefore
encloses H_z on the whole phase set. Taking the maximum of their upper
endpoints, adding actual direct uppers, summing all rows and dividing by
positive N gives the stated S upper. A failed vertex may not be omitted.
The coarse actual-row cap nN is a valid replacement for the complete
row, including when a special-row primitive guard fails.

## Intrinsic relaxation loss

Define M explicitly as the exact retained-row contributions plus, on each
ordinary row, its exact actual D_r contribution and max_z H_r(z;phi),
all divided by N. At fixed phi, the reverse triangle inequality gives

    0 <= max_z H_z-H_actual
       <= (Delta_c,r+Delta_p)*A_r/2,
    A_r=sum_(k in K_r) 1/|cos u_k-cos v|.

On either half of a shifted grid with clearance at least1, successive
distances from its pole are separated by one. The inequality
|cot(pi*y/n)|<=n/[pi*min(y,n-y)] therefore yields a total at most
2n*H_n/pi for each channel. The constant-numerator identity gives

    A_r <= 2n*H_n/(pi*|sin v|).

For the ordinary rows the integer lower distances in (16) are positive.
The chord bound sin(t)>=2t/pi on[0,pi/2] gives the reciprocal bound
m/(2d_r). Each distance integer occurs at most four times across both

    0<=M(phi)-S(phi)<=(2/pi)*Delta_* H_n^2.

Differentiation of the actual coefficients gives

## Artificial-vertex tail budget

For real p0,c0/sin v, the identity
|a-b|+|a+b|=2max(|a|,|b|) gives

    |C_z|+|D_z|=max(|p0|,|c0/sin v|)/2
               <=(1+5/|sin v|)/2.

This differs from the correlated C02 coefficient estimate and is the
appropriate budget for artificial vertices.

For one vertex/channel the disjoint cores contain at most n samples and
12 blocks. C06's J48 smooth error, both harmonic tails per block and
the WD96 endpoint-derivative tail consequently sum to

    e=7n*h^4*M48/5760+24*eta32,16/h+h*tau.

The signed all-core formula has no negative-part factor2. Its tail

the largest uniform per-vertex allowance, not the sum of four allowances.
Summing the row weights and dividing by nm produces precisely

    [7*pi^4*M48/(5760*n^4)+24*eta32,16/pi+pi*tau/n^2]

## Work, totality and implementation boundary

At fixed orders each ordinary row requires at most8 actual samples,
12 regular vertex samples and96 C06 calls, plus bounded-size coefficient,
root, sign, cut and wrap operations. Four retained rows require4n
bounded-work finite evaluations. The C01 finite-ratio contract supplies
such interval evaluations, including a global-cap fallback; enumerating
the original Fourier polynomial at each sample is not the intended
implementation. C06's prefix length is capped at32 and its moment degree
is fixed, so there is no block-length loop in the production call.

Thus the scalar-operation count is O(n) for a bounded attempt at fixed
precision followed by coarse row replacement on failure. The cap follows
from the nonnegative cosine coefficient sum2N of G: |F|<=N, row<=nN,
and S<=2N. The fallback has bounded arithmetic cost and can be extremely
loose. Unbounded precision escalation, retry/subdivision loops, useful
acceptance, input bit lengths, exact-rational bit complexity and elementary
argument reduction are outside the O(n) claim. In particular the recent
C07 cost-only pilot supplies no speed conclusion for this C09 construction.

For unchanged C06 adoption the LP64 bound2<=n<=2147483647 is mandatory.
Products N,nN and global caps must use checked arbitrary precision or
outward arithmetic; nN need not fit a signed64 integer at that ceiling.
The domain requires finite valid rectangle inputs, contained in the
stated closed quadrant, with nonempty P. Invalid inputs are not rescued
by the scientific fallback. Outward pi endpoints slightly beyond pi
must be intersected with the mathematical domain or separately justified;
the present theorem's B subset[0,pi]^2 assumption remains literal.

No positive lower bound for p over a box is needed. Constant p0=0
vertices, exact tangencies, seams, alpha0/pi, beta0/pi and the closed
diagonal boundary are covered by the stated branches. No mesh exchange
symmetry or artificial zero-times-infinity evaluation is used.
