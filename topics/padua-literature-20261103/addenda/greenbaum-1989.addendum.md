## Extracted published statements

### LIT?padua-literature-claude-57 — Setting and the Paige theorem used by Greenbaum

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: A slightly perturbed Lanczos recurrence (3) is v^j = Aq^j − α_j q^j − β_{j−1}q^{j−1} − f^j, with α_j from the usual formula, ‖f^j‖ ≤ ε‖A‖, ε ≪ 1, q^{j+1} = v^j/β_j, β_j = ‖v^j‖; in matrix form AQ_J = Q_J T_J + v^J e_Jᵀ + F_J (3.1). For rounding errors ε is a small multiple of the unit roundoff, and coefficient deviations are absorbed into the f^j (pp. 13–14). Greenbaum quotes Paige's theorem in this form: if a perturbed recurrence of form (4.1) reaches β_{N+m} = 0 at step N + m with all perturbation terms bounded by ξ‖A‖, every eigenvalue of T_{N+m} lies within σ(N + m)³ξ‖A‖ of an eigenvalue of A, σ = O(1) independent of ξ, N, m, ‖A‖ (p. 22, (4.5)–(4.6)).
- Page / theorem / equation: pp. 13–14, eqs. (3), (3.1), (4); p. 22, (4.2)–(4.6).
- Reading evidence and limitations: read on page images.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): the one-operator template for any "Greenbaum-type" statement; the hypothesis is a perturbed three-term recurrence for one symmetric A with one start vector.

### LIT?padua-literature-claude-58 — Theorem 1 and Theorem 1′: the backward model and its interval width (two branches)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Theorem 1 (p. 23): the tridiagonal T_J generated at any step J of a perturbed recurrence (3) equals that generated by exact Lanczos on a matrix whose eigenvalues lie within σ(N + m)³·max{ε‖A‖, ‖f^J‖, …, ‖f^{N+m}‖} of eigenvalues of A, where f^J, …, f^{N+m} are the smallest perturbations that make the recurrence produce β = 0 at or before step N + m (m = number of converged Ritz vectors). The construction (§4, p. 21; §7) continues the recurrence with small extra perturbations that orthogonalise new vectors against each other and against the unconverged Ritz vectors of the original run. Theorem 1′ (p. 51, (7.31)) quantifies the extra perturbations: the eigenvalue intervals have width σ(N + m)³O(J²γ√ε‖A‖) when converged Ritz vectors are defined with threshold δ = √ε‖A‖ (7.9) and the unconverged Ritz values are then well separated (so c_max = 1; p. 43), and otherwise σ(N + m)³O(c_max^{3/2} J ε^{1/4}‖A‖) with the more liberal threshold δ = ε^{1/4}‖A‖ (7.10, p. 44).
- Hypotheses and notation: N = order of A; J = step; γ and c_max are quantities defined in §7 (c_max is the maximal number of Ritz vectors in a cluster); ε small enough that the bounds of Lemmas 1–2 are below one (p. 43).
- Page / theorem / equation: p. 23 Theorem 1, (4.7); p. 43 (7.9); p. 44 (7.10); p. 50 (7.29)–(7.30); p. 51 Theorem 1′, (7.31).
- Reading evidence and limitations: statements read on page images; proofs not checked.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): resolves the interval-width conflict recorded in LIT-213: both widths are in the paper as the two branches of Theorem 1′. Šimonová–Tichý quote the √ε branch; T. Chen quotes the ε^{1/4} branch citing (7.10); Meurant–Strakoš quote Theorem 1 with the unquantified f^j. None of the three accounts is wrong; each describes one form.

### LIT?padua-literature-claude-59 — Author's caveat on the size of ε (conclusions)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: The theorems require excessively small ε (a condition of the form (N + m)³J²γ√ε below a constant, as printed on p. 61), but in practice the conclusions seem to hold for much larger ε; a precise bound on the ε needed is not known. The conclusions also name the frequency of multiple copies of eigenvalues as an open question, reduced by the analysis to exact Lanczos on clustered spectra.
- Page / theorem / equation: pp. 60–61.
- Reading evidence and limitations: read; the displayed condition on p. 61 is partly garbled in OCR and is paraphrased only.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): the 1D model is existence-plus-size with a large polynomial factor; supports wording that even in 1D the sharp width is not proved in the original.
