## Extracted published statements

### LIT?padua-literature-claude-66 — Theorem 5.7 (v2): Greenbaum-type backward model on I_{k+1} ⊗ A with O_k(ε‖A‖) perturbation

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: For symmetric Lanczos without reorthogonalisation under the paper's inexact-arithmetic model, there is a symmetric Ã = I_{k+1} ⊗ A + Δ of dimension n(k + 1) with ‖Δ‖ ≲_k ε‖A‖ and an orthonormal V̂_{k+1} with first column (v̄₁, 0, …, 0) such that ÃV̂_k = V̂_{k+1}T_k: k exact Lanczos steps on (Ã, ṽ) reproduce the computed T_k and β_k exactly. Remark 5.8: every eigenvalue of Ã is within ‖Δ‖ of an eigenvalue of A, and the spectral measures of (A, v̄₁) and (Ã, ṽ) are close in Wasserstein distance (the start-vector weight on each eigenvalue of A is redistributed over a cluster of k + 1 model eigenvalues). §5 (pp. 14–15) contrasts this with Greenbaum, whose bound depends on the fourth root of the accuracy parameter (citing Greenbaum eq. (7.10)), and places the construction closest to Paige 2010, iterating Paige's augmentation until the T_k block disappears. Theorem 5.11: every computed Ritz value lies in [λ_min(A), λ_max(A)] up to ≲_k ε‖A‖.
- Hypotheses and notation: one real symmetric A, one start vector; ≲_k hides polynomial factors in k (abstract, p. 1); local error bounds of Theorem 3.2 (p. 10) feed the construction.
- Page / theorem / equation: §5 pp. 14–16; Theorem 5.7 and proof pp. 20–21, (5.10); Remark 5.8 p. 21; Theorem 5.11 p. 22; Theorem 4.1 (Paige loss-of-orthogonality bound) p. 14.
- Reading evidence and limitations: statements read; Theorems 5.3–5.6 used in the proof not read.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): the sharpest one-operator Greenbaum-type statement read here: δ = O_k(ε) with any number of model eigenvalues per cluster. It is the 1D counterpart against which the 2D square-case lower bound δ ≳ √ε (PD-T059(c), PD-T060) and the Padua obstruction in reading (E) (PD-T069 and N303) must be worded. Different setting: one operator, no commutation constraint, exact representation of the computed T_k by a model of dimension n(k + 1).
