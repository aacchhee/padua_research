## Extracted published statements

### LIT?padua-literature-claude-54 — Commuting conditions and the finite-n Favard-type characterisation as used by the paper

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: For an orthonormal basis p_n of the degree-n orthogonal space, x_i p_n = B_{n+1,i} p_{n+1} + A_{n+1,i} p_n + B_{n,i}ᵀ p_{n−1} with rank conditions (5) (notation swapped relative to Xu: here A is the square symmetric block). Theorem 2.1 states three commuting conditions (6a)–(6c) on these blocks as necessary for recurrence matrices of an orthonormal sequence, citing zu Castell–Filbir–Forster (Inzell lectures, Theorem 2.4). Theorem 3.2 (citing Dunkl–Xu, Theorem 3.5.1): polynomials generated by the stacked recurrence (9) are orthonormal for some positive-definite bilinear functional iff the A blocks are symmetric, the rank condition holds and the commuting conditions hold; the authors add that further conditions are needed for the functional to be integration against a measure, which their finite-n algorithm does not need.
- Hypotheses and notation: non-degenerate measure (their assumption (1)); total-degree spaces.
- Page / theorem / equation: §2.2 p. 4, eqs. (4)–(7), Theorem 2.1; §3.3 p. 6, Theorem 3.2.

### LIT?padua-literature-claude-55 — Canonical form and the stacked evaluation recurrence (Def. 3.1, Thm 3.1, §3.2)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: A basis is canonical if Σ_i B_{n,i}ᵀB_{n,i} = Λ_n is diagonal (Definition 3.1). Then stacking the d relations gives Λ_{n+1}p_{n+1} = Σ_i x_i B_{n+1,i}ᵀ p_n − Σ_i B_{n+1,i}ᵀ A_{n+1,i} p_n − Σ_i B_{n+1,i}ᵀ B_{n,i}ᵀ p_{n−1}; the stacked system is overdetermined but consistent, and Λ_{n+1} is positive definite by the rank condition (Theorem 3.1). Any valid recurrence matrices are brought to canonical form by one symmetric eigendecomposition per degree (§3.2, eqs. (10)–(12)).
- Page / theorem / equation: §3.1 p. 5 (Definition 3.1, Theorem 3.1, eq. (9)); §3.2 p. 6.
- Reading evidence and limitations: read.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): the all-coordinate ("two-relation" in d = 2) block recurrence in exact arithmetic; compare PD-T051 (MEASURED: the two-relation block recurrence keeps the reference-free drift bound below √u on six non-product benchmarks).

### LIT?padua-literature-claude-56 — The multivariate Stieltjes procedure and where non-convexity enters; stability claims are empirical

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: From moments of known polynomials, A_{n+1,i} = S_{n,i} (eq. (25)); the U and Σ factors of B_{n+1,i} come from an eigendecomposition of T_{n,i,i} = B_{n+1,i}B_{n+1,i}ᵀ (eq. (26)); the V̂ blocks come from mixed moments (eq. (28)). In d = 2 the remaining row is fixed (up to an irrelevant sign) by orthonormality, and the commuting conditions need not be imposed (§5.2.4, p. 11). For d > 2 the commuting condition (6c) restricts the remaining blocks to a kernel, and orthonormality plus the mixed moments leave unitary unknowns W_j satisfying (35), a non-convex generalisation of a weighted orthogonal Procrustes problem (§5.2.6, pp. 12–13); for d = 3 a direct solution is derived (§5.2.7, p. 13); for d > 3 numerical optimisation is needed and is left as a natural extension (p. 21). Stability: the abstract and conclusions say the MS algorithm is far more stable than direct Gram–Schmidt orthogonalisation of moments, supported by examples up to d = 3 and by condition numbers of the T_n moment matrices versus Gram matrices (§6.3, p. 19). No theorem bounds rounding errors in the pages read.
- Page / theorem / equation: §5.2 pp. 9–13, eqs. (20)–(36); §6.3 p. 19; §7 p. 21; abstract p. 1.
- Reading evidence and limitations: read; the d = 3 direct solution only partly read (p. 13).
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): RELATED to PD-T051 and to the selection question (PD-T143): MS uses all coordinates at every degree (no one-relation selection), works from moments (quadrature or Monte Carlo), and measures stability by recurrence-matrix and basis errors, not by span drift from the exact graded space. The paper cites Barrio–Peña–Sauer 2010 ([1]) and Xu SIAM J. Math. Anal. 1993 ([29]).
