## Scalar enclosure justification (developer proof; independent review needed)

For x>0 differentiation under the exponentially convergent integrals gives
F'=-G and G'=F-1/x. Thus S(x)=pi/2-cos(x)F(x)-sin(x)G(x) has derivative
sin(x)/x. F(0+)=pi/2 and sin(x)G(x)->0 at zero: split G at t=1 and
t=1/x to obtain G(x)=O(1+|log x|). Hence S=Si. Likewise
C(x)=sin(x)F(x)-cos(x)G(x) has derivative cos(x)/x and tends to zero
at infinity (F<=1/x,G<=1/x²), identifying C=Ci with its usual improper
integral definition. At x=n*pi these are exactly the protocol identities.

The finite geometric identity for 1/(1+t²) yields remainder sign (-1)^m;
its magnitude is bounded by the first omitted monomial integral because
0<1/(1+t²)<=1. Integrating t^r exp(-xt) gives r!/x^(r+1), by r
integrations by parts. Thus the 20-term bounds in the protocol are exact.

Integrating the entire sine/cosine power series on a finite interval gives
Si(x)=sum_(k>=0)(-1)^k*x^(2k+1)/[(2k+1)(2k+1)!] and
Cin(x)=sum_(k>=1)(-1)^(k+1)*x^(2k)/[(2k)(2k)!]. Successive absolute ratios
are respectively x²(2k+1)/[(2k+2)(2k+3)²] and
x²(2k)/[(2k+2)²(2k+1)]. They decrease with k in their stated ranges;
cross multiplication verifies this directly. Once <=1/2, twice the next
term bounds the entire unsigned remaining series. Outward interval
evaluation encloses each finite sum even under cancellation.

Since Cin(x)=gamma+log(x)-Ci(x), subtraction at 16*pi gives the anchor
identity; gamma cancels exactly. For 1<=q<=2, z=(q-1)/(q+1) is in [0,1/3]
and log(q)=2*sum_(k>=0)z^(2k+1)/(2k+1), by integrating the geometric
series for 1/(1-z²). After k terms the positive tail is at most
2*z^(2k+1)/[(2k+1)(1-z²)]. Power-of-two reduction extends this to every
positive rational in use. Machin's identity follows by applying the tangent
addition formula to 4*atan(1/5)-atan(1/239), which lies in (0,pi/2) and
has tangent one. Alternating arctangent terms enclose its finite evaluation.

Every arithmetic operation rounds the lower integer endpoint down and
upper endpoint up on a fixed dyadic grid. Addition/subtraction are exact
on endpoints; multiplication takes the extrema of four endpoint products;
division takes extrema after checking the denominator excludes zero.
Python integers are unbounded. Fraction-to-grid conversion uses exact
integer quotients. These statements describe the intended implementation,
which still requires code inspection and an independently executed rerun.
