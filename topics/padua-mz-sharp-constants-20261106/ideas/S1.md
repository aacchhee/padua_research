# IDEA?mz-sharp-codex-S1-01 — reciprocal cardinal Volterra model

Aliases: logarithmic primitive; Mellin primitive pole; two-edge antisymmetric
nodal envelope; half-wave logarithmic ground state.

- Question / scope: PD-T020, the sharp lower spectral constant of Lebesgue
  CC on degree N−1, for all N and each parity.
- Mechanism and hypotheses: remove the alternating nodal sign, use the
  reciprocal cardinal interpolation operator, and separate endpoint scales.
  Its far lower-scale kernel is a primitive in log k. Retain the Jacobian,
  actual CC weights and the average of sin²(N theta). Enforce the degree
  constraint by a_(N−k)=−a_k.
- Closest registry entries read in full: IDEA-065–071 and385/386; see the
  [gate](../novelty-gate-S1.md) and linked theory.
- Separation criterion: a variable logarithmic envelope with a sharp
  continuum norm and an o(log N) interpolation-operator error. A mere
  absolute Schur order bound or a constant harmonic envelope erases the
  distinction. A failure of the normalized model/finite transfer kills the
  proposed constant, not PD-T020.
- Literature baseline and limitations: bounded repository screen only;
  no external asymptotic theorem imported, no priority claim. The handoff's
  Wilf/Widom attribution is unverified and not a proof dependency.
- Applicable obstructions and exact hypotheses broken: antisymmetry
  cancels the top DCT coefficient exactly, escaping PD-N006; signed
  near-diagonal interpolation is retained, escaping PD-N005's required
  absolute-column estimate. No PSD inference from Hankel entry signs.
- Novelty gate verdict: variant with the specified variable-envelope and
  error obligation, continuing the existing sharp target.
- Cheapest decisive test: derive leading constants from the exact cardinal
  formula, first for smooth envelopes supported away from the logarithmic
  endpoints; verify the parity/degree constraint exactly. Then establish
  a norm bound for the residual by dyadic blocks and unweighted DCT
  stability before attempting any spectral fit.
- Kill criterion written before testing: failure of degree cancellation,
  weight/Jacobian factors, or o(log N) residual blocks the prediction.
  K2 separately kills the model at >5% discrepancy only when K1 passes.
- Test discriminability: exact algebra and operator estimates distinguish
  the claimed coefficient; finite fits are measured checks only.
- Actual results: model derivation and prediction in
  [S1-model](../theory/S1-model.md), registered before K1 final data.
  Finite-N sharpness is not yet proved at this preregistration.
