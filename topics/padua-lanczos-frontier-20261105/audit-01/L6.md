## 2. Independent reconstruction

**l6-01 (claimed PROVED, analytic).** For 0 < e ≤ 1/300, with the PD-T145 four-atom sections in the positive-leading orthonormal (1,x,y) gauge:
1. Both section norms are at most 1, and ‖[Hx,Hy]‖ ≥ e/6.
2. For any commuting X, Y with norms at most 1 and any invertible 3×3 Q, the complete residuals satisfy ‖Fx‖+‖Fy‖ ≥ e·σ_min(Q)/12.
3. If each residual is at most c·u·‖Q‖ and the Gram loss is at most 1/2, then e ≤ 24√3·c·u.

Each step was checked:
- M, Cx and Cy, built from the atoms with sympy, equal the displayed matrices and PD-T145's.
- The leading minors, M⁻¹, A, B and the commutator entry 3e(12e−1)/D² all match.
- The bound 7200/2809 holds on the stated interval.
- The Cholesky orientation is right: Hx = R⁻ᵀCxR⁻¹ = RAR⁻¹. At 40 digits the error is about 1e-41; the reversed orientation is off by about 2.4.
- The conditioning bounds λ_min ≥ 1/108 and κ(R) < 15 hold (actual λ_min ≈ 0.09). The final step is 480/2809 > 1/6.
- The compression argument gives ‖Hx‖ ≈ 0.991.
- The residual identity Q[Hx,Hy] = −FxHy + FyHx − XFy + YFx is exact and uses no orthogonality of Q. With κ(Q) ≤ √3 it gives (3). The gauge-change remark is correct.

Exact cross-check: [Hx,Hy] is a real skew 3×3 matrix and the trace is similarity-invariant, so ‖C‖₂² = −tr([A,B]²)/2 = 27e²(1−12e)²/(1+18e)³. The ratio ‖C‖₂/e is strictly decreasing on (0, 1/300].
