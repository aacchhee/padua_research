## Result

For the direct signed Lebesgue coefficient operator
`H_(m,n)f=sum Q_m(f phi_alpha)phi_alpha`, the valid norm control is

\[
\|H_{m,n}\|_{C\to L^2}
=\sup_{\|p\|_2=1}\sum_z|w_z||p(z)|
\le\sqrt{\tau_m\beta_{\rm abs}(m,n)},
\]

where `tau_m=sum|w|` and `beta_abs` is the largest eigenvalue of the
**absolute-weight** Legendre Gramian. This is
PD-T112, PROVED in development. Its negative-leverage corollary
gives `beta_abs<=lambda_+(G)+2 sum_(w<0)|w|K_n(z,z)`.

For `0<=r<=n` and `n+r<=m`, the operator reproduces `Pi_r`, giving

\[
\|f-H_{m,n}f\|_2
\le\left(2+\sqrt{\tau_m\beta_{\rm abs}(m,n)}\right)E_r(f).
\]

This is PD-T113, PROVED in development. A version with the
explicit polynomial aliasing term is recorded in the same file.

At `n=m-1`, `m>=3`, the maximal complete reproduced space is exactly
`Pi_1`. Odd-m quadratic x tests and even-m quadratic y tests give explicit
nonzero coefficient defects, so every E2-only bound, and in particular an
E_(m-1)-only bound, fails for all continuous inputs. This is the scoped
PD-N051, PROVED in development. It does not preclude convergence
for a fixed function or an estimate with an aliasing term.

## Invalid transfer retained

An upper eigenvalue bound for the signed Gramian alone gives no
continuous-input operator bound for arbitrary signed rules. The explicit
three-node degree-one-exact rule `(t,2-2t,t)` has scalar `G=1` on constants
while its operator norm is `(4t-2)/sqrt(2)`. This is
PD-N050, PROVED in its general signed-rule scope; it is not a
counterexample to a Padua-specific bound.
