## Kill criterion frozen before numerical testing

Derive the **complete** error functional on Chebyshev products, then form
the total-degree Gram defect. Stop D2/D3 proof attempts if this exact
defect has no structure controllable by a number, bounded independently
of `m`, of one-dimensional-type operators (Hankel, Toeplitz-plus-Hankel,
coordinate reflections, and clearly specified total-degree/triangular
compressions). A table indexed by two degrees or a relabeling of an
arbitrary matrix does not pass this test. A pass must exhibit explicit
operators and the bounded decomposition; it does not itself supply
their spectral bounds. If the criterion fails, keep the aliasing theorem
and finite spectra and record a scoped obstruction; do not reject the
universal Padua targets merely because this route fails.

Cheapest decisive test: use nodal cosine identities to classify each
mode by a finite alias group, then see whether its piecewise error
kernel factors into a bounded family of scalar moment kernels and
one-dimensional triangular masks. This analytic test precedes any
large spectral calculation.
