the total-degree Gram defect. Stop D2/D3 proof attempts if this exact
defect has no structure controllable by a number, bounded independently

their spectral bounds. If the criterion fails, keep the aliasing theorem

universal Padua targets merely because this route fails.

Cheapest decisive test: use nodal cosine identities to classify each
mode by a finite alias group, then see whether its piecewise error
kernel factors into a bounded family of scalar moment kernels and
one-dimensional triangular masks. This analytic test precedes any
large spectral calculation.

## Decisive analytic result and structural verdict

Write `h_even=2/(1-r^2), h_odd=0`. Fold the mode by
`A=f_m(a), B=f_(m+1)(b)` into the Lobatto rectangle. Its moment is
`h_A h_B` if `A+B<=m`; otherwise it is
`h_(m-A) h_(m+1-B)`. The latter is valid because the checkerboard signs
in the two complementary cosine modes cancel. This gives every mode,
including inexact modes below degree `2m`. In particular
`T_(m+1)(y)=T_m(x)` on nodes, so the rule's Lebesgue algebraic degree of
exactness is exactly `m` for every `m>=1`.

In the endpoint range `a+b<=2m-2`, the five inexact zones are the
central complement and the two alternatives after either coordinate
fold. Their complete inequalities are in the theory file. On each
zone the error is a difference of two separable scalar moment kernels;
the zone consists of coordinate intervals and one half-plane
`a+b<=c` or `a-b<=c`. Product-to-sum therefore gives at most 40 masked
tensor Hankel/Toeplitz terms. Splitting absolute differences into their
two signs gives at most 160 terms, independently of `m`.

Each coupled mask becomes a scalar block triangular truncation in
signed-degree row/column labels. The theory file gives its exact
one-dimensional Fourier integral, with diagonal unitary factors, and
the proved operator-norm multiplier bound `1+log(8n+1)`. Tensor factors
are genuine one-dimensional scalar-sequence Hankel or one-sided
Toeplitz operators, followed by coordinate compression to `Pi_n`.
This has substantially more content than bivariate indexing: an
arbitrary bivariate moment kernel has no bounded separable-rank
five-region decomposition.
