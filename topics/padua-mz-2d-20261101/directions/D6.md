## Exact reduction

Let `v_ab=T_a(x)T_b(y)`, in the fixed total-degree order, `a+b<=m-1`.
Let `H_uv=integral(v_u v_v)` and `Q_uv=Q_m(v_u v_v)`. The matrix H is positive
definite: a nonzero polynomial is nonzero on an open subset of the square,
so its squared Lebesgue integral is positive. The generalized eigenvalues
of `(Q,H)` equal the ordinary eigenvalues in the orthonormal Legendre basis:
a change of polynomial basis changes Q and H by the same invertible
congruence, and the Legendre mass matrix is the identity.

The complete exact D1 alias formula is used, not numerical weights. Write
`f_N(k)=min(k mod (2N),2N-(k mod (2N)))`, `A=f_m(r)`, `B=f_(m+1)(s)`.
Then `q_rs=h_A h_B` if `A+B<=m`, and otherwise
`q_rs=h_(m-A) h_(m+1-B)`, where `h_k=0` for odd k and
`h_k=2/(1-k^2)` for even k. The product-to-sum identity gives

`Q_(ab,cd)=(1/4) sum_(r in {a+c,|a-c|}) sum_(s in {b+d,|b-d|}) q_rs`,

with multiplicities, including equal zero modes. H uses `h_r h_s` instead
of `q_rs` in the same sum. Every entry is rational, formed with Python
`fractions.Fraction` before any numerical selection occurs.

For even m the x-degree parity is preserved: in the direct branch, a
nonzero moment needs r even; in the reflected branch it needs m-A even,
also r even. For odd m the corresponding y-degree parity is preserved,
since m+1 is even. The code splits the basis into these two parity blocks
and checks every off-block H and Q entry is exactly zero. It does not
assume separate parity in both coordinates.

For rational t and each block, clear a common positive denominator d to
obtain the exact symmetric integer matrix `A=d*(Q-tH)`. A numerical
generalized eigenbasis is rounded after multiplying by `10^12` to produce
a square **integer** matrix R. This numerical step only proposes a
congruence. Compute `B=R^T A R` with exact FLINT integer matrix arithmetic,
verify symmetry, and verify for every row i the strict integer inequality

`abs(B_ii) > sum_(j!=i) abs(B_ij)`.

A symmetric strictly diagonally dominant matrix with nonzero signed
diagonal has the same inertia as its diagonal matrix. Indeed, continuously
multiply all off-diagonal entries by s in `[0,1]`. Every matrix in this
path remains strictly diagonally dominant, and hence nonsingular: if
`Mx=0`, choose i with maximal nonzero `|x_i|`; its row contradicts strict
dominance. Eigenvalues vary continuously and cannot cross zero. At s=0
the inertia is exactly the count of negative and positive diagonal entries.

In particular B is nonsingular. Since R is square, B nonsingular implies
R invertible (`Rx=0` would imply `Bx=0`). Sylvester's law of inertia then
identifies the inertia of A with B. The positive scale d does not change
inertia. Thus every accepted count is exact regardless of numerical
accuracy or rounding in the candidate R. Insufficient numerical accuracy
causes certificate rejection rather than a false accepted count.

The generalized inertia of Q-tH counts generalized eigenvalues below t,
because H is positive definite. At the two minimum endpoints the checker
requires negative counts `(0,1)` and no zero eigenvalues; at maximum
endpoints it requires `(N-1,N)` and no zero eigenvalues. These give the
reported strict brackets. Strict dominance proves the absence of zeros.

For the witness, the integer vector z directly defines p in the same
Chebyshev basis. The code computes exact rationals `a=z^T Q z` and
`b=z^T H z`, checking `b>0` and `b-4a>0`. The Rayleigh principle gives
`lambda_min <= a/b < 1/4`, and hence `eta >= 1-lambda_min > 3/4`.
The coefficient list is explicit, so a checker can evaluate this final
inequality without any eigenvector calculation.
