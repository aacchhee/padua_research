## What fails in the old positive/sign argument

The continuous tensor mass already has the sign cycle `1,T2(x),T2(y)`:
the two constant-coordinate entries of `2H−R` are negative, while the
cross-coordinate entry is positive. Its positive cycle product survives
every sign switch. Thus the one-dimensional absolute-coefficient proof
cannot begin even where these entries are exactly integrated.
At `m=4`, `(H−R)[T2(x),T3(y)]=−4/9`, whereas
`(H−R)[T2(x),T2(x)T1(y)]=2/15`, so the other favorable defect sign also
fails. Finally the exact `m=3` cardinal polynomial at `(1,1)` has
integral `−1/18`: positive-weight Cauchy–Schwarz on arbitrary nodal
data is unavailable. The negative weight alone does not prove that the
form on `Pi_(m−1)` is indefinite.
