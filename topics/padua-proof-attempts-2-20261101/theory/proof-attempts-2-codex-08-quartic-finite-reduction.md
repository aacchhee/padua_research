## Statement

Let L_0,…,L_(N−1) be real bivariate polynomials of total degree at most4, and let λ=Σ|L_i| on S=[−1,1]². For s∈{−1,1}^N set P_s=Σs_iL_i. Every strict relative local maximum of λ belongs to the following finite candidate set, with all polynomials interpreted in the real coefficient field K generated by the L_i coefficients.

For each interior pattern, write A=(P_s)_x and B=(P_s)_y. If A or B is identically zero, P_s has no strict interior maximum and the pattern contributes no candidate. Otherwise let G=gcd(A,B), a=A/G and b=B/G. Include every common zero of a,b. If G is nonconstant, let F be its squarefree part and choose an integer t from {0,…,deg F} for which gcd(F,F_x+tF_y)=1. Include every common zero of F and F_x+tF_y. Both included systems have coprime nonzero polynomials and finitely many common zeros. Their two coordinate resultants provide finite supersets of their coordinate projections, so every Cartesian pair of complete real-resultant root intervals must receive a valid disposition.

For each open edge and every sign pattern of the non-identically-zero restricted cardinals, include every root in (−1,1) of q_s', where q_s is the restricted signed polynomial; a constant restriction contributes no candidate. Include the four corners.

All interior maxima on regular cardinal zero curves are excluded exactly: if L_i(z)=0 and ∇L_i(z)≠0, z is not a local maximum. On an open edge the analogous statement uses the nonzero tangential derivative of a vanishing restricted cardinal. Singular cardinal zeros, arbitrary crossings, singular Hessians and isolated-real zeros of a common gradient factor are retained by this reduction.

For degree-four Padua cardinals, exact arithmetic can be performed in K=Q(sqrt2,sqrt5). For any nonzero coordinate resultant R∈K[u], its field norm to Q is nonzero and contains all real roots of R in the chosen embedding. Complete real-root isolation of the squarefree norm is therefore a finite rational super-enumeration.

## Definitions

A sign pattern is active at z when s_iL_i(z)=|L_i(z)| for every i. A strict local maximum is relative to the physical closed square. Gradients, Hessians, tangent directions and roots use physical coordinates. Total degree is denoted deg. A polynomial gcd and its squarefree part are determined up to nonzero constants, which do not change their zero sets.

## Proof or evidence

### All active polynomials and the arrangement types

Pointwise, λ=max_sP_s. If s is active at z and λ has a strict relative local maximum there, then for every distinct sufficiently near feasible w,

P_s(w)≤λ(w)<λ(z)=P_s(z).

Thus **every** active P_s has a strict relative maximum. For an interior maximum all active gradients vanish. Flipping just the sign of a vanishing L_i gives another active polynomial, and subtracting the two gradient identities yields 2s_i∇L_i(z)=0. This proves the regular-crease exclusion, including crossings with any number of additional zeros. On an open edge the identical argument uses the two feasible tangent directions and the restricted tangent derivatives. This makes no assertion about the normal derivatives of an identically-zero edge cardinal.

Every point belongs to one of the finitely many polynomial sign conditions L_i>0, L_i<0, or L_i=0, and to the square interior, an open edge, or a corner. Thus the candidate treatment includes cell interiors, regular creases, arbitrary crossings and singular zero points without a tolerance-based geometric coincidence rule. It does not require a numerical drawing or a connected-component enumeration of those sign sets.

### The residual stationary system

An interior strict maximum of P_s is stationary. If one derivative is identically zero then P_s is independent of that coordinate, so a line of nearby points has the same value, contradicting strictness. Otherwise A=Ga and B=Gb. At a stationary point off G=0, a=b=0. The full gcd guarantees gcd(a,b)=1. If a or b is a nonzero constant there are no residual roots. Otherwise the coprime pair has a finite common zero set over an algebraic closure and nonzero coordinate resultants (possibly nonzero constants). A common root must zero both projected resultants, by the defining Sylvester identities. All roots of both resultants, followed by every Cartesian pair, therefore supply a finite superset. Leading coefficient degeneracies only add extraneous pairs; they remove no common zero.

### Common-gradient curves and their singular points

Suppose G(z)=0 and F is the squarefree part of G. If ∇F(z)≠0, the real implicit function theorem gives a nonconstant smooth curve through z contained in F=0. On that curve G=0 and hence ∇P_s=0. The chain rule makes P_s constant on a small connected curve segment. Points of that segment occur arbitrarily close to z, so z is not a strict interior maximum. It follows that every strict maximum on G=0 satisfies F=F_x=F_y=0. This includes an isolated-real zero of F: no smooth real curve has been asserted there.

It remains to enclose these singular points by a finite coprime system. Factor F over K into its distinct nonconstant irreducible factors f. For a fixed f, reduction modulo f gives

F_x+tF_y ≡ (F/f)(f_x+t f_y) mod f.

Since F is squarefree, f and F/f are coprime. Thus f divides F_x+tF_y only if f divides f_x+t f_y. The derivative has total degree below deg f, so divisibility is equivalent to the identity f_x+t f_y=0. Each nonconstant f has at most one such t: two different values would force f_y=0 and then f_x=0, impossible in characteristic zero. The number of irreducible factors is at most deg F. Consequently among deg F+1 distinct integers 0,…,deg F one has gcd(F,F_x+tF_y)=1. For that t, H=F_x+tF_y is nonzero; if H is a nonzero constant its common zero set with F is empty. Otherwise the same resultant argument as above gives a finite candidate set containing **every** singular point. Regular directional tangencies may be added as harmless extra candidates.

No sign of the Hessian was used to discard a common-factor zero. In particular P=−(x²+y²)² has G proportional to x²+y² and a strict maximum at its isolated-real zero; that zero is singular for F and is retained. P=−x²−y⁴ also shows why a singular Hessian cannot be discarded for degree4 even when G is constant.

### Edges, degenerate tangent critical points and corners

At an open-edge strict maximum every active restricted q_s has a strict one-dimensional maximum. A constant q_s is impossible; every other candidate satisfies q_s'=0 and a nonzero univariate polynomial has finitely many real roots. Signs of cardinals restricting identically to zero do not affect q_s and may be fixed arbitrarily during this tangent enumeration. A quartic strict maximum can have q_s''=0 (for example −t⁴), so all derivative roots are retained. At a stationary point of a nonconstant real quartic, the exact first nonzero Taylor coefficient has even order2 or4 and is negative precisely when it is a strict tangent maximum. The reduction does not assume that this tangent test alone decides normal maximality. Zero inward derivatives and singular vanishing edge cardinals require a separate exact local classification. Four explicit corner points finish the covering of S.

### Rational norm super-enumeration

For finite algebraic K/Q and nonzero R∈K[u], its field norm is the product of its conjugates, is a nonzero polynomial in Q[u], and vanishes at every real chosen-embedding root of R. Removing repeated factors changes no distinct root. Exact Sturm variation counts, together with disjoint intervals each containing one distinct real root and accounting for the total count on the line, certify a complete list. Taking all coordinate interval pairs covers each common root even though conjugate embeddings may introduce additional pairs. Every additional pair still needs a verified exclusion or inclusion. ∎
