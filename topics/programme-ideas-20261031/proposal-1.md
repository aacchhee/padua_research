## Central question

What is the second-order asymptotic expansion of the Lebesgue constant of the Padua points, where on the square is the Lebesgue function maximal, and how many local maxima does it have — each answer a proof or a certified computation, not a mesh observation.

## Template theory

Univariate Lebesgue-constant theory on [−1,1]: the Chebyshev expansion (2/π) log n + (2/π)(γ + log(8/π)) + O(1/n²) (Günttner; Shivakumar–Wong 1982; Smith 2006), the Ehlich–Zeller endpoint maximum, Brutman's local maxima. Missing on the square: an exact second term for Padua (Kolomoitsev–Lomako 2021 Thm 2.1 leaves O(log n)), a proof that the maximum sits at a non-node corner (an observation since De Marchi 2022), a certified count of local maxima (Białas-Cież–De Marchi–Suder 2026 publish numerical tables). A universal lower bound by the projection-constant route is excluded: that route is capped at (16/π⁴) log² n (Kolomoitsev–Lomako Thm 2.2, LITERATURE; `adversarial-gen4-2.md` (iii)); other mechanisms are not excluded.

## Sub-problems and payoffs

**Paper A — corner expansion and corner-argmax theorem.** The corner value agrees with an O(N) sum of closed-form kernel terms to 2.1e-13 for n = 3…64 (MEASURED; the identity is not yet written as a proof). Finite high-precision values to n = 8192 fit the conjectured form Λ_c = (4/π²) log² N + b log N + c − 1/N + …, N = n + ½ (PD-T?programme-ideas-claude-04, OPEN). Two uncertainties must be kept apart: the **pre-registered** fit family gives c ∈ [1.846095, 1.846161] (half-spread 3.3e-5), so T2's registered precision target (≥ 9 digits) is not met; a **post-registration, model-conditioned** refit in N = n + ½ is stable to ±2e-12 (b = 1.2255221257453, close to (8/π²)(γ + log(8/π)); c = 1.846160310424; κ = c − c₁² ≈ 0.91971). Fit stability is not a bound on the asymptotic remainder, and the failure to identify κ with small-height combinations of 1-D constants does not show that κ is genuinely two-dimensional. To prove: (i) the expansion, at least its b-term; (ii) the nature of κ; (iii) the −1/(n+½) term; (iv) the corner argmax (PD-T?programme-ideas-claude-05, OPEN) — no counterexample at 75 tested degrees up to n = 200 on finite meshes; the runner-up is an edge maximum near the midpoint of x = ±1 with fitted gap → ≈ 0.548, so a proof needs constant-order expansions at two points plus an a-priori bound elsewhere. Payoff, if (i) and (iv) are proved: a short classical-analysis paper.

**Paper B — certified counting of Lebesgue-function maxima.** The developer's quadtree search with local certificates returns candidate counts 15, 27, 25, 41, 50, 62 for n = 3…8 (published: 15, 27, 25, 39, 42, 56) and resolves crease pairs 1.7e-3–2.2e-2 apart that fixed meshes miss (PD-T?programme-ideas-claude-06, MEASURED). An independent audit check confirms that all 220 listed points are numerical strict local maxima; **completeness is not certified**: tolerance-based degeneracy decisions, an early return in the last-resort stage, a Newton-outside-box discard and near-crease coincidences are unproved steps (audit-01 A1). The paper therefore requires: a proved finite reduction, valid identities at degenerate points (including the n = 3 edge case), rigorous root enclosures, complete box-discard arguments, rigorous arithmetic and an independent checker. Until then the counts are proposed corrections only, and no correction should be sent to the authors. The current code is Padua-specific; other node families need new evaluators and degenerate-point handlers. Payoff, after certification: a tool paper in the ETNA-2024 line.

**Paper C — counting theorem, with Kraków–Padova, only if BSV 2012 leaves room.** The candidate counts are non-monotone in n and exceed the observed lower bounds, so the theorem to prove is about crease pairs, not "one maximum per Lissajous cell" (`adversarial-gen4-2.md` (iii)); it is BDS 2026 §4's question and should be done with them. The former sub-problem 4 (Padua–Jacobi / checkerboard Fekete) is not a paper: BSV 2012 own the family and the degree-dependent Λ-optimal parameters (Table 5), De Marchi–Usevich 2014 Prop. 1 is the separability, and determinant-optimal sets lose to BSV's (Λ 6.60 vs 5.92 at n = 10).

## Why a project and not a programme

Criterion 5 fails, flagged by gate, adversary and decisive test alike: classical approximation theory plus certified numerics; none of AC's structured-matrix tools (HSS, bulge chasing, IEPs, exact BTTB) is needed. Criterion 1's escalating horizon is gone: sub-problem 2 has no surviving mechanism after the (16/π⁴) log² n ceiling of the projection route, sub-problem 4 is BSV 2012 plus DU 2014, so the "Erdős–Bernstein theory for the square" meant to carry years 3–5 has no anchor. What remains meets criteria 2–4 at project scale provisionally — two or three separable papers, decisive tests already run, no owner of the exact questions found — but both first papers still need their main work (a proof for A, a certification for B).

## Risk

Speed: Padova and Kraków publish here yearly, own LEBEVAL, and can produce a certified table quickly once a method is public; Kolomoitsev–Lomako's machinery may reach the second term with little extra work. Mathematics: κ may have no closed form; the expansion and the argmax may resist proof (the argmax needs an a-priori bound away from two points). Numerics: the present counts are uncertified, and the certification work (Paper B's substance) may reveal additional or spurious maxima. Fit: the project spends AC's time outside the structured-matrix line; it is justified only as a bounded 12–24-month side project, and neither first paper is close to finished.
