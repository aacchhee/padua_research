## Residue under test

The adversary left one algorithmic paper: a pencil-pair inverse eigenvalue problem (nodes, weights and one linear pole per degree → generalized-Hessenberg pencil pair sharing one unitary Q) realised as a windowed rational Lanczos with a two-block generation window and a **three-block orthogonalisation window**, used as a *conditioned solver* (orthonormal basis without forming the ill-conditioned evaluated basis) for total-degree rational spaces V_n = Π_n/π_n whose poles are conjugate pairs of complex lines (point poles ∏|z−ζ_j|², or BHH's complex-shifted parallel lines x = 1 ± iε_j); the stated customer is the least-squares problem of Boullé–Herremans–Huybrechs (KU Leuven NUMA), the realistic form a Leuven (NALAG + NUMA) collaboration. Everything else in the draft was reduced to a section (window lemma with parity), a note (implicit Q), or an unmotivated track (commuting-pair rational Krylov). What is NOT yet measured anywhere: the window recurrence beyond n = 8 and beyond M = 120, its orthogonality in double precision at N ≈ 300, whether it buys accuracy over QR/SVD of the evaluated basis on BHH-type spaces, and whether the rational classes beat polynomials by a useful factor at N ≈ 300 (the adversary's gains at N ≤ 55 were 2–33×, borderline against its own ≥ 10× rule). Also unexamined: BHH's actual space is tensor-type with many more poles than polynomial degree, whereas the pencil class forces #poles = total degree n = O(√N); whether the pencil class is even the right space for BHH-type problems is tested as an informative item (T2b), not as a kill.

## Pre-registered tests and kill criteria

Common scope S1 (primary): 64×64 Chebyshev (first-kind) product grid on [−1,1]², M = 4096 nodes, unit weights; final degree n = 24 (N = (n+1)(n+2)/2 = 325; even n so that the complex conjugate-pair spaces are conjugation-closed and their LS residual for a real target equals that of the real space of real dimension N); the N ≈ 300 statement is made at n = 24 (N = 325) with n = 22 (N = 276) also reported. Even n = 2..24 reported for convergence curves. Validation grid: 97×97 Chebyshev grid (no node in common with the 64² grid). Scope S2 (secondary, the orchestrator's "M = 200–400 nodes"): M = 400 uniform random nodes in [−1,1]², unit weights, n = 22 (N = 276), seeds 1 and 2; T1/T3 quantities only. Errors are relative RMS over the grid in question. Pole taper for m poles (pairs or lines): ε_j = exp(−σ(√m − √j)), j = 1..m (ε_m = 1), with σ fixed by the nearest distance ε_1 = ε_min; **primary ε_min = 3e-4** (the 64² grid's corner resolution 1 − cos(π/128) = 3.0e-4, the hard regime where BHH-type practice lives), secondary ε_min ∈ {3e-3, 3e-2}. Pole ordering in the degree sequence: primary "far-first" (degree 1 gets the farthest pole), secondary "near-first". Targets (both non-harmonic, singular at the corner (1,1), the adversary's frame): **f_A(x,y) = √(2 − x − y)** (BHH's f₂ = √(x+y) translated from [0,1]² to [−1,1]², singular only at the corner; BHH pole placement = conjugate pairs on the two edges through the corner) and **f_B = r^{1/2}(1 + ½ sin 3θ)**, r = |(x,y) − (1,1)|, θ = atan2(1−y, 1−x) (the adversary's f7, non-harmonic corner target). Informative third target f_C = √(1−x)(1 + ½ sin 2y) (pure edge singularity) for T2b only.

Admissible pencil-pair classes (one linear factor per degree; all real-valued spaces at even n):
- `Ppt`: point poles on the outward diagonal, ζ_j = (1+i) + ε_j e^{iπ/4}, 12 conjugate pairs at n = 24;
- `Pbhh`: BHH edge pairs x = 1 ± iε_j and y = 1 ± iε_j, 6 distances per edge, interleaved;
- `Ldiag`: 24 real parallel lines x + y = 2 + ε_k (lightning in the single variable s = 2−x−y);
- `Pdiag`: 12 conjugate pairs of diagonal lines x + y = 2 ± iε_j;
- `Mpt`: mixed class Π_24/π_12, 6 point-pole pairs (degrees 13–24) after 12 lines at infinity (degrees 1–12);
- `Pedge` (f_C and f_A only): 12 point poles along the edge x = 1 at y ∈ {−0.75, −0.25, 0.25, 0.75} × distances {ε_min, √ε_min, 1}.
- `P`: polynomials Π_24 (Chebyshev product basis), the control; polynomial windowed Lanczos (ℓ_k ≡ 1) as the K3 control.
Methods: `L3` = windowed rational Lanczos, three-block orthogonalisation (two projection passes per step, block basis by SVD keeping exactly dim W_{t+1} = t+2 vectors); `LF` = same with full reorthogonalisation against all previous blocks (O(MN²), the rational-Arnoldi-type route); `R1` = Householder QR (numpy) of the unit-column-scaled real evaluated basis T_a(x)T_b(y)/π_n(x,y); `R2` = SVD of the same matrix truncated at 1e-12·σ_max (BHH's truncated-SVD practice); `R3` = Householder QR of the unit-column-scaled graded complex basis T_a(x)T_b(y)/π_t(x,y), a+b = t ≤ n. Reference `LF80` = LF in 80-bit extended precision (numpy longdouble, u ≈ 5e-20, pivoted two-pass Gram–Schmidt instead of SVD). The rotation-based (Givens-updating) form of the pencil-pair IEP is NOT implemented; the Lanczos/Arnoldi route computes the same output (orthonormal Q and the recurrence pencil blocks), and K1 is about the output, not the route.

**T1 (K1 — conditioned-solver payoff).** Quantities at n = 24, S1, primary ε_min, far-first, classes Ppt (target f_B) and Pbhh (target f_A): (i) in-space residual ε_span = max over 5 random g = p/π_24 (p with N(0,1) Chebyshev coefficients, evaluated as a Chebyshev sum then divided by the node values of π_24, so every entry of g is accurate to ~1e-15 relative and g ∈ V_24 exactly) of ‖g − Q(QᴴWg)‖/‖g‖, and the same for 5 random g = p/π_12 ∈ V_12 ⊂ V_24; (ii) training-grid LS error for the target, compared with LF80; (iii) κ₂ of the scaled evaluated basis (from R2's singular values) and the per-step kept/discarded singular-value gap of L3; (iv) wall time of L3, LF, R1, R2. Runtime budget: < 60 s for all of T1 in double, < 3 min for LF80. **K1 fires iff**, for BOTH (Ppt, f_B) and (Pbhh, f_A), the best of {R1, R2, R3} (a) attains ε_span ≤ 10 × ε_span of the best of {L3, LF} on the g = p/π_24 test AND (b) reports a training-grid LS error within a factor 2 of LF80's. (If (b) holds but (a) fails, K1 does not fire but the payoff is recorded as "accuracy-marginal": QR already gets the answer.) S2 (M = 400, n = 22) is reported alongside and does not change whether K1 fired.

**T2 (K2 — approximation payoff).** Quantity: validation-grid (97²) relative RMS error of the LS fit at n = 24 (N = 325), S1, for each admissible class and for P, on f_A and f_B, each class at its best ε_min ∈ {3e-4, 3e-3, 3e-2} (the taper is a free parameter of the method; the training-grid error is reported too, so that fits that only exploit the grid are visible). The fit is computed by the method with the smallest ε_span in T1 (expected L3 or LF; if R1 differs by more than 2× from it the discrepancy is reported). Runtime budget: < 90 s. **K2 fires iff** on BOTH targets the best admissible pencil class improves on P by a factor < 10 on the validation grid at n = 24. Informative T2b (not a kill): BHH's own tensor space at matched dimension — real span of {T_k(y)(1−x)/((1−x)²+ε_j²), T_k(y)/((1−x)²+ε_j²)} ∪ {x↔y} ∪ Π^{tensor}_{N_p}, N_q = 12 pairs per edge, N_p = 5, dimension 4·12·6 + 36 = 324 — on f_A, f_B, f_C, solved by R2-type truncated SVD on its partial-fraction basis; plus the best pencil class and P on f_C. Pre-stated reading: if the BHH tensor space beats the best pencil class by > 10× on f_A (its own target), the "solver for BHH-type spaces" framing is wrong and the residue narrows to total-degree corner/point-pole spaces.

**T3 (K3 — the windowed recurrence as a solver).** Quantity: orthogonality defect δ = max_{i,j} |(QᴴWQ − I)_{ij}| of the L3 basis at n = 24 (N = 325), S1, class Ppt, primary ε_min, each of the two orderings; the per-degree growth of δ; the same for Pbhh, for the polynomial control, and for L3 in 80-bit (if δ scales with u the loss is rounding-driven; if not, the three-block algebra fails beyond the adversary's n ≤ 8 scope); S2 reported alongside. Runtime budget: < 30 s. **K3 fires iff** min over the two orderings of δ > 1e-6 for class Ppt at the primary ε_min in double precision. (If K3 fires for Ppt but LF stays at δ ≈ 1e-14, the residue survives only as an O(MN²) rational-Arnoldi solver, i.e. with no cost advantage over QR; that is recorded in the verdict.)

Not run, by design: the rotation-based IEP; stability theory; sub-problem 4 (commuting-pair Krylov); any n > 24, M > 4096, or grids finer than 97².
