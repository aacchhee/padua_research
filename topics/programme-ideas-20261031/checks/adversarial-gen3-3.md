**KILLED (ii)**, with (i) and (iii) each independently sufficient for the parts they touch. After removing what is already known or wrong, no substantive sub-problem is left: sub-problem 1 is Theorem 3.1 of Álvarez de Morales–Fernández–Pérez–Piñar (JCAM 214 (2008) 447–456) specialised to Φ = πI, Ψ = (α+1)∇π (band [n−s−1, n+p−1] with p = b, s = b−2, i.e. exactly (b−1, b−1); fulltext read), and it is not even the sharpest banded calculus on polygons (a degree-(b−1) matrix Pearson equation gives bandwidth b−2, MEASURED); sub-problem 2's Petrov–Galerkin matrix is not banded (upper part full, MEASURED), the banded symmetric variant has κ ≈ n^{4.5–5} and block-diagonal preconditioning does not change the exponent (the draft's own kill criterion fires), while the well-conditioned variant (κ ≈ n^{1.8}) is the 1D Shen / Olver weighted-ladder choice and, as a trial space, the 1990–93 pb-2 Rayleigh–Ritz construction of Liew–Lam–Wang; sub-problem 3 is disowned to R3 T4; sub-problem 4 rests on a cost miscount — the graded recurrence has ≈ 3.7·N^{3/2} nonzeros (MEASURED), so recurrence-based evaluation costs Θ(M·N^{3/2}) > dense Θ(MN), and its pre-registered kill criterion (exponent ≥ 1.9) fires in advance; sub-problem 5's premise ("polygon OP codes stop at degree 5") is false — Sommariva–Vianello's Matlab implementation generated graded OPs on convex and non-convex polygons and curvilinear regions to degree 40 at 1e−13 orthonormality in 2016, citing both Huhtanen–Larsen and the seed. ## Attack (ii): really a note

1. **Sub-problem 1 is a corollary, stated in 2008.** Lebesgue measure on Ω with ∂Ω ⊂ {π = 0} satisfies div(πI·u) = (∇π)ᵗu by one integration by parts (the boundary term carries π = 0); so does π^α dx with Ψ = (α+1)∇π. With p = deg Φ = b and q = deg Ψ = b−1, s = max(p−2, q−1) = b−2 and Thm 3.1 gives the band j ∈ [n−b+1, n+b−1]: degree-bandwidth (b−1, b−1) for both components of π∇P_n — the draft's structure theorem verbatim, including both limits. "Sharpness" (generic non-vanishing of the extreme blocks) is a one-line remark. MEASURED here on pentagon, heptagon, quartic curved domain, non-convex L-shape (π changes sign inside; the theorem only needs π = 0 on ∂Ω) and weights π¹, π²: bands exactly (b−1, b−1) in every case (n = 8, tolerance 1e−9). Moreover the scalar calculus is not the sharpest: for any polygon Φ = Σ_i (∏_{j≠i}ℓ_j) τ_iτ_iᵗ (τ_i the edge tangent) has degree b−1 and Φn_i = 0 on edge i, so it is a matrix Pearson equation of class b−3 and Thm 3.1 predicts bandwidth (b−2, b−2) for the components of Φ∇p; MEASURED (3,3) on the pentagon and (5,5) on the heptagon (n = 8). The remaining question "minimal class of Lebesgue measure on a given polygon" is a Granada-framework question (non-uniqueness of Pearson equations is their Example 1), not structured NLA.
2. **Sub-problem 2 collapses to a basis choice already in the literature.** The draft's Petrov–Galerkin matrix M_ij = ⟨q_i, Δ(πp_j)⟩ (trial πΠ_n, test Π_n) is not banded: Green's identity leaves the boundary term ∮ q_i p_j ∂_nπ ds, so the upper part is full (MEASURED band (3, 8) at n = 8, i.e. lower band b−2, upper band = n). The symmetric Galerkin variant (test πΠ_n) is banded (2b−2) (MEASURED (8,8)), but κ(S) ≈ n^{4.5–5.0} and block-diagonal (degree-block) preconditioning only halves it (exponent 4.2–4.7, n = 4..16): the draft's own kill criterion ("faster than n² after block-diagonal preconditioning") fires. The formulation that works — trial u = π·p̃ with p̃ orthonormal for π dμ, range basis the dμ-OPs — gives weighted differentiation D_x : p̃ ↦ ∂_x(πp̃) block-banded in degrees deg+1..deg+b−1 (MEASURED (4,−1)), stiffness D_xᵗD_x + D_yᵗD_y banded (b−2, b−2) (MEASURED (3,3)) and κ ≈ n^{1.8} (n^{1.5–1.7} block-preconditioned). This is the Shen 1994 Legendre–Galerkin basis in one variable, the W^{(1,…)}P^{(1,…)} → P^{(0,…)} ladder of Olver–Townsend–Vasil / Snowball–Olver / YFO in two, and the pb-2 trial space of Liew–Lam–Wang (1990–93) with Gram–Schmidt orthogonalisation — all by the same integration by parts. Nothing is left to prove beyond writing the exponents down; the "conditioning law" is the Markov inequality on Ω.
3. **Sub-problem 3** is R3 rank 5 / T4 and disowned by the draft; in the discrete setting the π-weighted OPs needed in item 2 are a second weighted QR on the same mesh, so this application does not even need a conversion operator (the "application driver" role claimed for T4 is empty).
4. **Sub-problem 4 rests on a wrong count.** The graded recurrence (H_x, H_y) of a numerically generated basis is block-tridiagonal with dense blocks: nnz(H_x)+nnz(H_y) = 210, 546, 1122, 2002, 3250 at N = 15, 28, 45, 66, 91 (≈ 3.7·N^{3/2}, MEASURED on the pentagon). Evaluating all N basis functions (or one Clenshaw sweep) at one point costs Θ(nnz) = Θ(N^{3/2}); at M points Θ(MN^{3/2}) — a factor √N more than the dense Q·c, not less. The draft's "O(N^{3/2}) total" is per point. The pre-registered kill criterion of entry point 4 (measured exponent ≥ 1.9) fires: the exponent is 2.5. What remains is the scoped negative (Q not butterfly-compressible) — a remark that strengthens an existing obstruction row, not a paper.
5. **Sub-problem 5's premise is false.** Sommariva–Vianello (2016/17) already generate graded OPs on convex and non-convex polygons and curvilinear regions at machine precision to n = 40 with a public Matlab code (citing HL and the seed); Lai's degree-5 spline construction is not the state of the art. A PDE layer on top of that code is a software note.

After removal, zero substantive sub-problems remain. The bundle fails criterion 2 of CAMPAIGN_CONTEXT.

## Attack (iii): ill-posed or blocked

The central question asks for a "stable, quasi-optimal-complexity spectral method" on non-Koornwinder domains. The brief's obstruction table does not contain this row, so the hypotheses are stated here:

- **Dense blocks.** On a domain without product/Koornwinder structure the degree blocks of every operator are generically full: MEASURED in-band block density 0.99 (pentagon), 1.00 (heptagon), 0.95 (L-shape) versus 0.24 for the square, where the numerically generated graded-lex basis coincides with the tensor Legendre basis (first element of each degree block is L_d(x), etc.) and the blocks are banded; the quartic domain shows 0.47 only because π is even in x. Consequences: every operator has Θ(N^{3/2}) nonzeros (π∂_x: 1660 at N = 45 versus 149 on the square); a banded direct solve costs Θ(N·(b·n)²) = Θ(b²N²); YFO 2026 report O(dof) nonzeros with blocks of bandwidth (2,2) precisely because of the Koornwinder structure the candidate discards. Escape would need an in-block sparsifying similarity for all operators simultaneously; none is proposed.
- **Generation cost.** The basis itself costs Θ(MN^{3/2}) (HL/BJ recurrence, R3 §2) or Θ(MN²) (QR); the operator assembly by quadrature Θ(MN·bandwidth). With M ≳ N this is Θ(N^{2.5})–Θ(N³) before the first solve. "Quasi-optimal" is unreachable without a fast transform, and item 4 of (ii) shows the recurrence gives none; mesh-structured transforms are exactly the Koornwinder/tensor case.
- **Corners.** On polygons the method converges algebraically (YFO 2026 report this for their corner domains; the draft concedes it). Lightning solvers give root-exponential convergence for Laplace problems on polygons (Gopal–Trefethen 2019); ultraSEM handles polygons by quads/triangles with hp-adaptivity. The curved non-Koornwinder boundary, where spectral rates are possible, is where YFO's case (ii) (ρ = sqrt of a polynomial with roots off [α, β]) already lives; what is left are curved domains not of the form γρ(x) ≤ y ≤ δρ(x) after an affine map — these can be split into such pieces (p-FEM, Snowball–Olver Appendix A) at the price the Olver line already pays.
- **Conditioning.** Stated under (ii) item 2: the proposed formulation fails the draft's own criterion; the working one is known.

The π-weighted trial space also imposes homogeneous Dirichlet data only (the draft says so); Neumann/Robin need the parameter hierarchy, which is again YFO's W^{(a,b,c,d)} machinery.

## Attack (iv): renaming of an exclusion

Nearest items: judged "measure modification (narrow)" / R3 rank 5 / T4 (sub-problem 3, disowned by the draft and by the gate, Cluster E); the obstruction "Q not HSS-compressible" (sub-problem 4 extends the negative but offers nothing positive once the cost count is corrected); exclusion (d) untouched. The separating property claimed against the Olver line — "no Koornwinder product structure, replaced by the π-weighted calculus available for any algebraic boundary" — is not a property of the candidate: the π-weighted calculus is Snowball–Olver's stated general principle (2019) and Granada's Thm 3.1 (2008), and the thing the Koornwinder structure buys (banded blocks, O(dof) nonzeros, fast transforms) is lost rather than replaced. So the candidate is, relative to the owners, the same object with a worse basis; relative to the registry it is a renaming of the T4 application plus a negative remark on the P5 obstruction.
