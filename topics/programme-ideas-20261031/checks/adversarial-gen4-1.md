## Attack (ii): really a note

**(a) The grid case is a product, so every grid statement in the draft is a corollary of the 1-D theory.** On the product grid G×G with equal (product) weights, the 1-D discrete orthonormal polynomials φ_i on G give the orthonormal family φ_i(x)φ_j(y); the total-degree space Π_k is spanned by the subset i+j ≤ k of this family. Hence, with u_i(x) := φ_i(x)²/(n+1) (a probability vector over degrees i = 0..n for each grid point x),

  s_k(x,y) = Σ_{i+j≤k} u_i(x) u_j(y)  (exact algebra; Script P2: identity holds to 6e-16 at n ≤ 8, all θ).

Immediate consequences (Script P3 checks the inequalities, zero violations):
- Sandwich: max_{a+b=k} s_a^{1D}(x) s_b^{1D}(y) ≤ s_k(x,y) ≤ s_k^{1D}(x) s_k^{1D}(y). The upper bound alone forces "corner first": the edge midpoint (1,0) has s ≤ s_k^{1D}(0) < 1 bounded away from 1 for θ < 1 (0 is in the 1-D unsaturated band), while the corner needs both coordinates saturated. The "product-set versus corner-set dichotomy" that sub-problem 2 proposes to prove is therefore not a dichotomy: the saturated set is sandwiched between two corner-type sets, {max(a(x),a(y)) ≤ θ} ⊇ S_θ ⊇ {a(x)+a(y) ≤ θ}.
- Identification of S_θ: the 1-D Christoffel/saturation law (LITERATURE: Rakhmanov 1996, Dragnev–Saff 1997, Kuijlaars–Rakhmanov, Kuijlaars 2000; strong form BKMM 2007; not re-proved here) says the degree distribution u_·(x) of a grid point x is asymptotically supported on degrees ≤ a(x)·n with positive mass below. The finite convolution above then gives s_{θn}(x,y) → 1 iff a(x)+a(y) ≤ θ, i.e. **S_θ = {√(1−x²)+√(1−y²) ≤ θ}**, and off S_θ the limit is the explicit convolution ∫U_x(θ−β) dU_y(β) of the 1-D cumulative leverages: this is the "Christoffel law" of sub-problem 2, in closed form, with no bivariate constrained problem. Consistency check at the generator's own sizes (scope extension, n = 24, 40): every node with s > 1−10⁻³ lies inside the predicted sum-region (24/24 at n = 40, θ = 0.9), none outside; the predicted region is far from filled at these n because the 1-D deficit 1−s decays slowly (corner 1−s = 1.1e-7 at n = 40, θ = 0.9).
- So sub-problem 2 ("formulate the constrained extremal problem for total degree in 2D and identify S_θ") collapses to one page given the 1-D theorem. The gate's "tensor artefact (row/column restriction)" target is confirmed in a sharper form: it is a product-basis identity, not just a restriction bound.

**(b) C₂ = C₁ is a short corollary, not Paper C.** For p ∈ Π_k with |p| ≤ 1 on the grid, Σ_B w_B p(B)² ≤ 1, so |p(z)|² ≤ K_k(z,z) = Σ_{i+j≤k} φ_i(x)²φ_j(y)². Each φ_i is a degree-i polynomial with grid sup-norm ≤ √(n+1), so the 1-D theorem (Kuijlaars 2023, with Coppersmith–Rivlin for small i/n) gives sup_{[−1,1]}|φ_i| ≤ √(n+1) e^{n(C₁(i/n)+o(1))}. Hence K_k(z,z) ≤ N_k (n+1)² exp(2n max_{i+j≤k}[C₁(i/n)+C₁(j/n)] + o(n)) ≤ N_k (n+1)² e^{2nC₁(θ)+o(n)}, because C₁ is convex with C₁(0) = 0, hence superadditive, and increasing. So (1/n) log sup|p| ≤ C₁(θ)+o(1); the lower bound is p(x,y) = q(x). The generator's remark that "row-then-column only gives 2C₁ and a bidegree-splitting argument is needed" is correct, and the splitting is supplied for free by the product orthonormal basis. Uniformity of the o(1) in i/n is routine (monotonicity of the extremal value in the degree plus continuity of C₁; Coppersmith–Rivlin's explicit bound for i² ≤ cn). Script P6 (n = 8): LP ratio 2D/1D = 1.018–1.019 (as the generator found), Christoffel bound and split bound above the LP value, as the inequalities require. The "irregular domain" and "Chebyshev lattice" extensions in sub-problem 3 are respectively a different (non-product) problem not in the draft and a triviality (Padua has Λ = O(log²n), Bos–Caliari–De Marchi–Vianello–Xu 2006, so the growth constant is 0 for every θ ≤ 1 without any computation).

**(c) Sub-problem 4 (re-housed by the gate) is a three-line lemma plus a tolerance artefact.** Let v_B = (φ_a(x_B)φ_b(y_B))_{a+b≤k} be the coefficient vector of the projected point mass K_k(·,B), ‖v_B‖² = K_k(B,B), u_B = v_B/‖v_B‖. Then (x−x_B)K_k(·,B) vanishes at B, so ‖(x−x_B)K_k(·,B)‖²_w = Σ_{B'≠B} w_{B'}(x_{B'}−x_B)² K_k(B',B)² ≤ 4(K_k(B,B) − w_B K_k(B,B)²), and projecting onto Π_k does not increase the norm; therefore

  ‖H_x u_B − x_B u_B‖ ≤ 2√(1−s_k(B)), and likewise for H_y, for any node set.

(Script P4: no violations, measured ratio residual/√(1−s) ≤ 0.79 on grid and Padua at n ≤ 8, ≤ 0.37 at n = 24, 40.) So joint Ritz convergence of a node is equivalent, up to constants, to its saturation, while single-operator convergence of H_x needs only x-saturation (a(x) ≤ θ): the "genuinely bivariate effect" is exactly the gap between the max-region and the sum-region of (a). The generator's "0 joint converged pairs everywhere" is a tolerance artefact: at n = 40, θ = 0.9 the corner has 1−s = 1.1e-7 and joint residual 1.2e-4, so a 10⁻⁶ threshold sees nothing, while the generator's own eigenvector test at thresholds 10⁻³/10⁻² returns 8/28 joint pairs (scope extension). This correction should be passed to gen2-draft-3's and (b) L1's adversary question (gate §3, Cluster A): joint Ritz pairs do converge on grids, at the saturated nodes, with residual ≍ √(1−s).

**(d) Sub-problem 1 (Padua) is the judged Uvarov item plus two tautologies.** (i) With cubature weights, RES fact 1 (product-Chebyshev basis discretely orthonormal for k ≤ n−1) and the Padua weight identity w_B(K_n^{cont}(B,B) − T_n(x_B)²) = 1 (generator's test 2, confirmed) give the closed form s_k(B) = K_k^{cont}(B,B)/(K_n^{cont}(B,B) − T_n(x_B)²) for k ≤ n−1 (Script P5: exact to 7e-16), hence no saturation and bulk value ≈ N_k/N = θ² from the known Christoffel asymptotics of the product Chebyshev measure: nothing to prove. (ii) Equal weights = (n/(n+2))·cubature measure + positive masses on the 2n+1 boundary nodes: the multivariate Uvarov modification already judged (R2 row 2, RES 2b, Delgado et al. 2010); the Christoffel ratio K^{eq}_k/K^{cub}_k is the Woodbury correction with the explicit product-Chebyshev kernel on 2n+1 boundary nodes, and its asymptotic evaluation near the boundary is a computation (a note), not a theorem of a new kind. (iii) The "corner value → (1/2)² = 1/4 at θ = 1" and the edge value → 1/2 are not boundary-layer constants: at k = n Padua is unisolvent, every node has s = 1 for any weights, so K^{eq}_n(B,B)/K^{cub}_n(B,B) = w^{cub}_B/w^{eq}_B = (n+2)/(4n) at corners and (n+2)/(2n) on edges (Script P5: exact to 9e-16; 0.3125 at n = 8, limit 1/4). The generator's "edge (1,0) → 0.68" is a non-node of Pad_n for n = 40, 80, which is why it does not match 1/2. (iv) "No bulk saturation on Padua" is the 2-D instance of the classical 1-D fact that Chebyshev-distributed nodes carry no active constraint (node density = equilibrium density), and the finite-rank boundary masses cannot change that in the bulk; this is also why the generator's pre-registered Padua kill fired. (v) The first-paper content "closed-form (H_x, H_y) for Padua cubature weights, proof to write" is the judged item "explicit Padua recurrences: corollary of CDMV 2008 eqs 9–10 + BDMVX 2007 Thm 2.3" (R2 §1), which the draft itself says it does not claim.

Sub-problem 4: re-housed by the gate, and a lemma. Sub-problem 1: judged item + tautology + a Uvarov computation (note). Sub-problem 5: data appendix. ## Attack (iii): ill-posed or blocked

The brief §3 table has no row that blocks an exact-arithmetic limit law, and the draft's escapes (OBST-04 not applicable, commutation irrelevant in exact arithmetic) are correct but beside the point. The problem is the central question's object:

- **The mechanism has no instance among the candidate's node sets.** On product grids the "bivariate constrained extremal problem" is the 1-D constrained problem applied twice through the product identity of (ii)(a); the draft's constrained pluripotential problem V_{K,σ} is never defined (no functional, no constraint class, no variational statement), and the one existing several-variable "forbidden region" theory (partial Bergman kernels: Ross–Singer 2017, Zelditch–Zhou) concerns subspaces of holomorphic sections, not discrete measures. On Padua, LC(m,n) and all Chebyshev lattices the constraint is slack: their counting measures converge to the product arcsine = pluripotential equilibrium measure of the square for every ratio m/n, so the draft's year-3–5 track "LC(m,n) with m ≠ n+1 as one-parameter densities σ" is empty (no saturation can occur; only the degree-to-node ratio changes). The template theorem is not false in 2-D; it is trivially true on grids and vacuous on Chebyshev-type sets.
- **The only genuinely bivariate version** would take a non-product node family with non-equilibrium density (hexagonal lattice in the square, equispaced points in a disk or triangle, uniform random points) and ask whether S_θ is determined by the density alone (the 2-D analogue of Rakhmanov's theorem that the constrained equilibrium depends only on σ). There the 1-D template (zero counting measures, logarithmic energy) has no counterpart, the product-grid answer gives at most a conjecture, and the natural tools (Kroó-type needle polynomials, local comparison of Christoffel functions) are approximation theory, not structured NLA. It is not in the draft and would fail criterion 5 as the gate already flagged.
- Measured scope caveat: the exact non-saturation at finite n (Script P1: no grid node is exactly saturated for k < n, by restriction to a grid row) shows that "saturation" is an exponentially-small-deficit statement, as in 1-D; the generator's "s(1,1) = 1.000" is 1−s ≈ 1e-7 at n = 40, so every finite-n statement in the draft (0.6 % saturated at n = 60) is tolerance-dependent and must be read through the convolution formula.
