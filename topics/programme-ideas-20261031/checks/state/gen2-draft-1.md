# Two-index divide-and-conquer: product-structured joint eigenvectors of commuting banded pairs and the merge-based bivariate IEP
Model: claude-fable-5-1, effort xhigh (as requested by the orchestrator) · Angle B · 2026-10-31 · Status: returned

## Central question
Is the joint eigenvector matrix Q of a commuting symmetric generalized-Hessenberg pair (H_x, H_y) of order N (bandwidth ≈ √(2N), the sections of a discrete bivariate measure) representable, stably and constructively, as a product of O(√N · log N) HSS-compressible Cauchy-like factors carrying O(N^{3/2} polylog N) parameters in total, and does the inverse map (nodes, weights) → (H_x, H_y) admit a node-set merge (divide-and-conquer) at the same cost, below the O(N^{5/2}) of Huhtanen–Larsen and the seed's Givens IEP?

## Template theory
Univariate, mature:
- Forward: Cuppen / Gu–Eisenstat divide-and-conquer for the symmetric tridiagonal (and banded / HSS) eigenproblem. Tearing a tridiagonal at the middle is a rank-one modification; the eigenvector matrix of D + ρuuᵀ is Cauchy-like in (d_i, λ_j) (secular equation, Löwner-type formula), and the full eigenvector matrix is a product of O(log N) block-diagonal Cauchy-like factors. SuperDC (Ou–Xia; AC's pySuperDC) keeps the factors in HSS form, giving O(N r² log² N)-type costs for HSS rank r and a structured eigenvector representation with O(N r log N) storage instead of N². [UNVERIFIED from memory for the exact exponents; AC owns the code and the paper.]
- Inverse: Gragg–Harrod 1984 (RKPW, stable O(N²) Jacobi IEP) and the "merge two Jacobi matrices" divide-and-conquer (Laurie, JCAM 1999, accurate recovery of recursion coefficients from Gauss rules; Elhay–Golub–Kautsky 1991 updating). Leuven 2025: "not aware of any extension of Laurie's approach to MOPs" (Faghih et al., as reported in R1 §Leuven; LZ 037). Brief §4 lists exactly this as a seed template.
- Key theorems a bivariate version must deliver: (T1) a tearing lemma: a generalized-Hessenberg pair torn at a degree boundary is a rank-b modification with b = O(√N) of a block-diagonal pair, and the modification is jointly structured (the two sections share the tearing subspace); (T2) a joint secular equation: the joint eigenvalues/eigenvectors of a commuting rank-b update of a commuting block-diagonal pair are given by Cauchy-like formulas in the joint variable z = x + iy (Z = H_x + iH_y is normal, complex symmetric, eigenvalues the nodes z_i = x_i + i y_i, distinct for distinct nodes even when x_i repeat); (T3) an interlacing/stability statement replacing the real-line interlacing that makes the univariate secular equation safe; (T4) an inverse merge: from (H_x^1, H_y^1, w^1) and (H_x^2, H_y^2, w^2) on disjoint node sets to the pair of the union, with a cost bound and a backward-error bound in the pattern class.

## Entry points and cheap decisive tests
1. **Factor-rank test (seconds).** Random nodes in [−1,1]², n = 20 (N = 231) and n = 44 (N = 1035); cubature-weighted Padua n = 20, 44. Compute H_x by Householder QR of the graded Chebyshev–Vandermonde (dense, exact reference; closed form for Padua via `closed_form_H.py`). Tear H_x at the degree boundary nearest N/2 into blkdiag(H₁, H₂) + U Sᵀ (rank b = bandwidth, record b). Eigendecompose H₁, H₂ densely, then apply the b rank-one updates sequentially (ordinary real secular equations, LAPACK dlaed-style), storing each Cauchy-like eigenvector factor explicitly; measure the HSS off-diagonal ranks (tol 1e-10, relative to the factor norm) of every factor under (a) eigenvalue-sorted order and (b) kd-tree order of the nodes in R². Quantity: total parameters Σ_factors (N · HSS rank · log N) versus N² and versus N^{3/2}. **Kill criterion (pre-written):** if for both orders and both node families the summed factor storage exceeds 0.5·N² at N = 1035, the product representation does not compress and the forward half dies (and with it the "structure in the factors" escape from the measured Q obstruction). Secondary kill for the joint part: on Padua, if the within-cluster refinement by H_y produces joint eigenvectors with ‖Qᵀ(H_x+iH_y)Q − diag(z)‖ > 10³ u ‖Z‖, the cluster/deflation step is not stable as proposed.
2. **Merge-cost test (n ≤ 8, exact count).** Take N = 45 nodes (n = 8) split into two unisolvent subsets of 15 and 30 points (n₁ = 4, n₂ = 6; the second is not of the form dim Π_k on purpose, to see degree extension). Build (H_x^i, H_y^i, Q_i) for each subset by the seed's Algorithm 1 (reference implementation, exact arithmetic over Q via fractions where feasible, floating point otherwise). Form the block-diagonal pair with start vector (‖w¹‖e₁; ‖w²‖e₁), apply the single merging rotation, and count the rotations and the fill (number of nonzeros outside the target pattern after each rotation) needed by the seed's chase to restore the generalized-Hessenberg pattern. Quantity: rotations R(N) and total touched entries T(N) for N = 10, 15, 21, 28, 36, 45 (n = 3..8), fitted exponent of T(N). **Kill criterion:** fitted exponent of T(N) ≥ 2.4 (i.e. no better than the O(N^{5/2}) per-point algorithm) kills the "merge is cheaper than re-adding points" mechanism; an exponent ≤ 2.0 on this range keeps it alive for the N = 231 run.
3. **Joint-secular test (n ≤ 6).** For the complex symmetric normal Z = H_x + iH_y with a rank-b tear, verify numerically that the eigenvectors of the torn update are Cauchy-like in (z-eigenvalues of the halves, nodes z_j) to 1e-12 (i.e. the displacement equation D_z V − V diag(z) has rank ≤ b). Kill: displacement rank > b + 2 at N = 28.

## Why it is not an exclusion
- (a) exact complexity of nodes → recurrences: sub-problem 5 is explicitly a dictionary only; no exact-arithmetic complexity claim below the curse is made. Separation: floating point, unitary, cost measured in flops with stability; collapse if the only route to a fast merge is an exact polynomial-matrix computation (then it is (a)'s X2/X6).
- (b) finite-precision theory of the truncated recurrence: no window recurrence, no loss-of-orthogonality statement, no backward interpretation via measures. The joint eigensolver is a forward problem on a given commuting pair. Collapse: if the merge's backward error can only be stated as "sections of a nearby measure" (L3's object), then that statement is (b)'s; here the backward error is stated in the pattern class of pairs.
- (c), (d): untouched.
- Already judged, nearest: **P5 HSS IEP (LZ 037, "None planned"; R2 §2 row 5; measured: Q not HSS-compressible, top-level ranks ≈ 430/517)**. Separation (mathematical): the object compressed is not Q but the Cauchy-like factors of a rank-b tear, whose off-diagonal ranks are governed by the separation of eigenvalue clusters along the cluster tree (a Cauchy-kernel low-rank argument), not by the CS decomposition of a single unitary; brief §3 records "divide-and-conquer merges" and "structure in the rotation sequence" as the escape. Observation that collapses the separation: entry-point test 1 failing (factor ranks ≈ N/4), or the eigenvalue clustering of x-coordinates (Padua) making every factor's Cauchy kernel non-separable.
- Also near: "Q-free downdating" (settled: classical R-factor downdating) — the un-merge is a subset removal, not single-node downdating; if subset removal reduces to repeated R-factor downdates with no cost gain, sub-problem 4's downdating use is dropped (the merge itself stands).

## Risks and obstructions
Nearest recorded obstruction: **"Q not HSS-compressible"** (brief §3: unitary ⇒ CS decomposition ⇒ off-diagonal ranks generically near full; measured 429–431 of 517 at N = 1035). Its exact hypothesis is compression of the single matrix Q under one row/column partition. This candidate escapes only if the factors (not Q) compress, which is the recorded escape clause, and only test 1 decides. Second: the "Popov curse" analogue in floating point: the tear rank b ≈ √(2N) means √N secular solves per level, so the best conceivable cost is Õ(N^{3/2}) per level for eigenvalues alone, and the eigenvector products add a factor; a target of Õ(N²) (not N^{3/2}) is the honest one, against the current O(N^{5/2}). Third: "H_x, H_y never commute exactly in floating point": the forward solver must accept a pair with O(u)-commutator; the joint refinement inside clusters is where this enters (the within-cluster H_y block is only approximately symmetric-invariant); this is handled as a mixed forward–backward statement and does not need a nearby measure. Fourth: single-operator spectrum clustering (Padua, or random nodes with close x) makes the D&C on H_x alone ill-conditioned for eigenvectors; the joint refinement is mandatory, not optional, and its stability is sub-problem 1's content.
