# Saturated discrete bivariate OPs: constrained-equilibrium asymptotics on Padua-type measures
Model: claude-fable-5-1, effort xhigh · Angle D · 2026-10-31 · Status: returned

## Central question
What are the weak and strong asymptotics (block-Jacobi limits, Christoffel functions, zero/Ritz distribution) of discrete bivariate orthonormal polynomials of total degree k = θn on Pad_n (equal weights, and more generally positive weights on Chebyshev-lattice / Lissajous nodes) as n → ∞, and what is the bivariate constrained-equilibrium problem whose saturated region explains them?

## Template theory
Univariate discrete-OP asymptotics in the "saturated" regime: for N nodes with degree k ≍ θN the zero counting measure converges to the minimizer of the logarithmic energy under the constraint μ ≤ σ (σ = asymptotic node density), with a saturated region where μ = σ (Rakhmanov 1996; Dragnev–Saff 1997; Kuijlaars–Van Assche 1999; Kuijlaars–Rakhmanov 1999; Baik–Kriecherbauer–McLaughlin–Miller 2007 for strong asymptotics via Riemann–Hilbert; Kuijlaars JAT 2023 for the n-grid Coppersmith–Rivlin growth rate). Consequences: (i) limits of recurrence coefficients exist and are explicit; (ii) Ritz-value / Lanczos convergence theory (Kuijlaars 2000 "which eigenvalues are found"); (iii) growth rate of ‖p‖_K/‖p‖_{grid} (impossibility theorems). A bivariate version must deliver: a constrained extremal problem for probability measures on [−1,1]² with constraint μ ≤ σ_Pad (= product arcsine density, measured limit of Padua counting measures), identification of the saturated set S_θ, the limit of the block-Jacobi entries (H_x, H_y) along graded index sets, and a Christoffel-function law λ_k(x) ≈ 1/(N_k·density) off S_θ versus λ_k(x) ≈ node weight on S_θ. The pluripotential analogue of the unconstrained problem exists (BBWN 2011 equidistribution; Bloom–Levenberg Christoffel/Bergman asymptotics for Bernstein–Markov pairs), but a *constrained* pluripotential (Monge–Ampère) problem for discrete bivariate OPs has no published version in the inputs; its univariate degeneration is well understood.

## Entry points and cheap decisive tests
1. **Christoffel ratio test (seconds, n ≤ 40).** For Pad_n with equal weights, compute K_k(x,x) = Σ_{|α|≤k} p_α(x)² for the discrete orthonormal basis (QR of the Vandermonde in product-Chebyshev basis) and compare with the product-Chebyshev continuous kernel K_k^{cont}(x,x). Quantity: r_k(x) = K_k^{disc}(x,x)/K_k^{cont}(x,x) on a fine grid, for k/n = 0.5, 0.8, 0.95, 1. Kill criterion: if r_k(x) → 1 uniformly (within 5%) for every θ < 1 up to n = 40, there is no saturation layer and the programme collapses to "bulk universality + a trivial top block" (note-sized; the Uvarov rank-(2n+1) explains everything). Expected under the programme: r_k deviates from 1 on a region whose area grows with θ, concentrated at the boundary/corners (where Padua density is lowest relative to dim).
2. **Joint Ritz-value test (seconds, n ≤ 30).** Compute H_x^{(k)}, H_y^{(k)} (sections of the exact banded recurrences for cubature weights — closed form per RES 2a — and equal weights via QR) and the joint eigenvalue cloud of the commuting-in-subspace pair (via eigenvalues of H_x^{(k)} + i H_y^{(k)} in the normal case or min-commutator joint diagonalisation). Quantity: number of Ritz pairs within distance 10⁻⁸ of a Padua node ("converged") as a function of k/n. Kill criterion: if the converged fraction is 0 for all k < n and jumps to 1 at k = n (no intermediate saturation), the univariate template does not transfer and sub-problem 3 is dead; if the converged fraction grows continuously (first near the boundary of the square), the saturated-region picture holds.
3. **Growth-constant test (minutes, n ≤ 20).** For θ = 0.5, 0.75, 0.9 compute max ‖p‖_{[−1,1]²}/‖p‖_{Pad_n} over Π_{θn} by the SVD of the Vandermonde ratio on a fine Chebyshev mesh (admissible mesh of degree 2n, constant known). Quantity: (1/n) log of the ratio vs n. Kill criterion: if the ratio stays bounded (O(1) or O(log² n)) for θ < 1, Padua is a norming set at every fixed ratio θ and sub-problem 4 is trivial (no multivariate Coppersmith–Rivlin phenomenon on Padua, since Padua is a WAM with polylog constant). NOTE: this is the expected outcome for Padua (it is a WAM!), in which case test 3 is re-aimed at *equal-weight equispaced* 2D grids / non-Chebyshev lattices; the Padua case then plays the role of the "Chebyshev n-grid" where C(α) ≡ 0.

## Plausible first paper (≈6 months)
"Discrete orthogonal polynomials on Padua points: exact bulk, saturation layer and the Uvarov correction." Content: closed-form H_x, H_y for cubature weights (RES 2a, proof to be written); the rank-(2n+1) Uvarov structure of equal weights (known, R2 §1); a theorem that for k ≤ n−1−m the equal-weight orthonormal polynomials differ from the Chebyshev ones by O(ρ^m) in an explicit sense (this is a discrete "Uvarov decay" lemma: perturbation at the boundary points affects degree k only through the Christoffel–Darboux kernel at the boundary, whose relative size is computable); numerics for the Christoffel ratio and joint Ritz clouds up to n = 200; the constrained-equilibrium conjecture stated precisely with the measured S_θ. ## Why it is not an exclusion
- (a) 2-D Schur/Levinson: that is exact complexity of moments → OPs; here the object is the *limit law* of the OPs as n → ∞, no complexity claim. Nearest item: none in (a).
- (b) Finite-precision bivariate Lanczos (L1, L3): (b) studies rounding-error growth governed by Ritz residuals; this candidate studies the exact-arithmetic *limit distribution* of the joint Ritz values at degree θn (sub-problem 3). Separation: the quantity here is defined in exact arithmetic and its n → ∞ limit; (b)'s quantities are O(u) perturbations at fixed n. Collapse observation: if LZ's L1 ends up proving a Ritz-value limit law as a lemma, sub-problem 3 must be handed over; the rest stands.
- (c) Unisolvence over fields: unrelated (asymptotics over ℝ).
- (d) MZ constants: (d) studies λ_min/λ_max of the Gram matrix of *continuous* OPs under a discrete rule at degree n ≤ N−1 (fixed-ratio η(θ) in M3 is the nearest). Separation: (d)'s object is the Gram matrix of the continuous basis (a norm-equivalence constant); this candidate's object is the discrete OP family itself and its Christoffel function/zero distribution. The two are linked (λ_min(G) is the worst-case Christoffel ratio) and the collapse observation is: if M3's fixed-ratio analysis already identifies the saturated region S_θ as the set where the Christoffel ratio degenerates, sub-problem 2's measure-theoretic content would be a corollary of M3 and this candidate shrinks to sub-problems 1, 3, 4.
- Already judged: "empirical Christoffel via QR" (standard practice) — here QR is only the data generator; "explicit Padua recurrences" — used as benchmark, not claimed; "Uvarov reweighting" — judged narrow as an *algorithm*; here the rank-(2n+1) structure is an *analytic* tool for the saturation-layer theorem, a different use. "Padua hyperinterpolation trivial" — not touched.

## Risks and obstructions
- Nearest recorded obstruction: OBST-04 in LZ ("Padua sets have M = N, so there is no long run of the recurrence"); exact hypothesis: forward-stability analogues need M ≫ N. This candidate *uses* M = N (k = θn < n keeps M/N_k = 1/θ² > 1) and asks the exact-arithmetic question, so the obstruction's hypothesis (finite precision, M ≫ N needed) does not apply.
- "Computed H_x, H_y never commute exactly": irrelevant in exact arithmetic; for numerics the joint-eigenvalue extraction must use a commutator-robust method (He–Kressner–Plestenjak 2025 is already catalogued, LZ 034).
- Genuine risk 1: Padua is a WAM with polylog constant, so the growth test 3 may be trivial on Padua (expected); the programme then rests on sub-problems 1–3 and shifts test 3 to non-Chebyshev lattices.
- Genuine risk 2: the pluripotential constrained problem may not have a tractable variational characterisation (Monge–Ampère with an obstacle σ); the univariate theory relies on logarithmic potentials. Mitigation: the Padua density is a product measure, so a separable ansatz may reduce S_θ to a univariate problem in each Chebyshev angle — this is the first thing the Christoffel-ratio test will show (is S_θ a product set or not?).
- Genuine risk 3: duplication with M3 of (d) in the fixed-ratio regime (see collapse observation above).
