# The √N-size inverse problem: rectangular two-parameter pencils from the top recurrence blocks, their conditioning, normal forms and stable node extraction
Model: claude-fable-5-1, effort xhigh (as requested by the orchestrator) · Angle B · 2026-10-31 · Status: returned (with a flagged note-size risk)

## Central question
Can the N×N commuting generalized-Hessenberg pair (H_x, H_y) of a unisolvent node set be replaced, as the numerical object for node recovery, updating and conditioning questions, by an (n+2)×(n+1) linear rectangular two-parameter pencil L(x, y) = L₀ + x L_x + y L_y built directly from the top recurrence blocks (the degree-n → n+1 residuals), with a Givens "staircase" normal form, a conditioning theory of its rank-drop points relative to the N×N joint spectrum, and a stable O(n³)-type node extraction — and what is the inverse problem "nodes → structured pencil" in this √N-sized form?

## Template theory
- Univariate: nodes of a Gauss rule are eigenvalues of the n×n Jacobi matrix; the "last row" of the recurrence (the degree-n polynomial p_n = det(xI − J_n)/const) carries the nodes; Golub–Welsch; inverse problem = Jacobi IEP; companion/comrade/colleague pencils and their conditioning (Chebyshev colleague matrices, Good 1961; Barnett; backward stability of colleague eigenvalues, Nakatsukasa–Noferini 2016 [UNVERIFIED from memory]).
- Bivariate, exact: the ideal of a unisolvent set X (N = dim Π_n) has a linear minimal resolution; Hilbert–Burch gives I(X) as the maximal minors of an (n+2)×(n+1) matrix of linear forms (Gaeta 1951; Eisenbud GTM 229 Ch. 3; reported in R3 X3 and NOTES `exact_arithmetic_ideas.md` §B; checked over F_p for n ≤ 6 on Padua and random sets, Padua pencil has O(n) nonzeros — MEASURED by the earlier agent). So X is the rank-drop locus (the "eigenvalues") of a linear rectangular two-parameter pencil in the sense of Hochstenbach–Košir–Plestenjak, NLA 31 (2024) e2540 (web-checked 2026-10-31: their rectangular MEP paper is the forward problem for ARMA/LTI models; no inverse problem, no ideals of points, no OPs on Plestenjak's 2023–26 list).
- Scalar bivariate: Xu's and Schmid's matrix equations for cubature (the (n+1)×(n+2) blocks A_{n,i} and the quadratic equations in Γ), reported in `adv_exact_X1_X3_X7.md` §3.2 as "the Hilbert–Burch syzygies are the polynomial form of these matrix conditions" [inference of that agent].
- What a bivariate version must deliver: (T1) an explicit map (A_{n,x}, A_{n,y}, B_{n,x}, B_{n,y}, and the degree-(n−1) blocks) → L, i.e. the pencil in the orthonormal-basis coordinates (a "comrade pencil" for bivariate OPs), with the linear syzygies read off the commutativity relations [a probe below shows the relevant syzygies lie in the recurrence span]; (T2) a unitary normal form of L (rotations acting on the (n+2) rows and (n+1) columns from left and right, two-sided, preserving the rank-drop set) and its uniqueness; (T3) a conditioning theory: sensitivity of the rank-drop points of L vs sensitivity of the joint eigenvalues of (H_x, H_y) and vs the Lebesgue/Christoffel data of X; (T4) a stable forward solver (nodes from L) in O(n³)–O(n⁴) with an orthonormal-basis backward-error statement (the Graf–Townsend instability theorem covers monomial multiplication matrices only, brief §3); (T5) the inverse problem nodes → L directly, at o(N^{3/2}) numerically (not exact-arithmetic complexity; that is (a)).

## Entry points and cheap decisive tests
1. **Syzygy-from-recurrence probe (done, seconds; scratchpad `hb_from_recurrence.py`, MEASURED in this scope).** Random real nodes, n = 5, N = 21, M = N. The 2(n+1) residuals r_x,k = x p_{n,k} − Σ H_x[j,k] p_j and r_y,k (degree n+1, vanish on the nodes to 1.8e-14) span a space of dimension n+2 (as Hilbert–Burch requires: n+2 generators), with n constant relations among the 2(n+1) residuals; the space of linear syzygies Σ(α_k x + β_k y + γ_k) r_k = 0 has dimension 21 = 3n + (n+1): exactly n+1 genuine linear syzygies beyond constant multiples, matching the Hilbert–Burch (n+2)×(n+1) count; and each y r_x,k − x r_y,k lies in span{xR, yR, R} to 1e-13, i.e. the commutator-type syzygies are carried by the recurrence data. So T1 is a construction, not a hope. **Pre-written kill for the next step:** if the explicit pencil assembled from (A, B) blocks fails to drop rank on the nodes to O(u κ) at n = 10 for random sets (κ the Vandermonde condition in the OP basis), the construction has a hidden normalisation and T1 is harder than a formula.
2. **Conditioning probe (seconds, n ≤ 8).** Perturb L entrywise by 1e-10 (random), recompute the rank-drop points by brute force (rank of L(x,y) on a fine grid plus Newton on det of square (n+1)×(n+1) submatrices... or via HKP compression), compare node displacement to the displacement under the same relative perturbation of (H_x, H_y) entries. Kill for the "√N-size object is as well conditioned" claim: node sensitivity from L exceeds that from the pair by > 10³ for Padua n = 8 (then L is a compact but ill-conditioned representation and the programme keeps 1 and 2 only).
3. **Staircase probe (n ≤ 6).** Apply Givens rotations from the left (n+2 rows) and right (n+1 columns) to L₀, L_x, L_y simultaneously to zero a prescribed pattern (e.g. L_x upper-triangular with positive diagonal and L_y with its first subdiagonal zeroed where possible); count the free parameters of the resulting form and compare with 2N (nodes) and with the pencil's 3(n+1)(n+2) entries minus the (n+2)² + (n+1)² gauge. Kill: no pattern with exactly 2N free parameters is reachable by unitary two-sided reductions at n = 6 (then the staircase form is not a canonical form and sub-problem 2 is wrong as stated).
