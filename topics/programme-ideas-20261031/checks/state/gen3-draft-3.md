# Total-degree spectral methods on arbitrary planar algebraic domains with numerically generated orthogonal polynomials: the boundary-polynomial operator calculus
Model: claude-fable-5-1, effort xhigh (as requested by the orchestrator) · Angle C · 2026-10-31 · Status: returned (with a flagged overlap of one sub-problem with R3 rank 5 / T4)

Aliases: π-weighted differentiation; boundary-polynomial calculus; semiclassical structure relation for numerically generated bivariate OPs; weighted trial spaces π·Π_n (Dirichlet); spectral methods on polygons / non-Koornwinder domains; ultraspherical method in two variables on general domains; conversion operators between dμ and π²dμ; fast transforms for discrete bivariate OPs.

## Central question
On a bounded planar domain Ω whose boundary is the zero set of a polynomial π of degree b (polygons, curved algebraic boundaries, unions, non-Koornwinder shapes) with the OP basis of a quadrature measure on Ω available only numerically through a discrete recurrence (seed/HL), which differential operators have block-banded total-degree representations — the measured answer is π∂_x, π∂_y with degree-bandwidth b−1 and π²Δ with 2b−2 — and does that calculus support a stable, quasi-optimal-complexity spectral method for Dirichlet/Neumann problems whose conditioning, preconditioning and fast transforms can be proved rather than observed?

## Template theory
One variable: the ultraspherical spectral method (Olver–Townsend SIREV 2013) — differentiation maps C^{(λ)} to C^{(λ+1)} as a diagonal operator, conversion operators are banded, and the resulting almost-banded systems solve in O(n) with provable conditioning; the Legendre/Chebyshev–Galerkin weighted bases (1−x²)·p_n for Dirichlet problems (Shen 1994); the semiclassical structure relation π·p_n′ = Σ (banded) p_k for weights with π·w′/w polynomial (Pearson equation), which is what makes "π times derivative" banded for every semiclassical weight. Two variables on Koornwinder domains: Olver–Townsend–Vasil (triangle, sparse ladder operators), Yao–Fasondini–Olver arXiv:2606.24423 (2026; generalised Koornwinder domains y between γρ(x) and δρ(x), basis analytic via univariate semiclassical families, operators banded, "3D analogues … left for future work"; fetched 2026-10-31), Gao–Iserles W-systems on the triangle (IMA JNA 2025; efficient computation "subject to ongoing research"). What a general-domain version must deliver: (a) the structure theorem: in the graded OP basis of (Ω, w) with w semiclassical for π, the operators π∂_x, π∂_y are block-banded with bandwidth b−1 (MEASURED here on an irregular pentagon with Lebesgue measure, n = 10: bands (4,4), (4,4); plain ∂_x full upper; π²Δ band (8,8)); (b) a Dirichlet formulation in the weighted trial space π·Π_n whose stiffness matrix is block-banded and whose conditioning is controlled; (c) conversion operators between the OPs of dμ and of π²dμ (a measure modification with a modifier vanishing exactly on the boundary — see the overlap note below); (d) values ↔ coefficients transforms for numerically generated bases at cost below dense O(MN), and the error/stability of the whole chain at degrees 50–200 where current polygon OP codes stop at degree 5 (Lai arXiv:2601.04022, Jan 2026, abstract fetched 2026-10-31: degrees 1–5; NOTES `recent_adjacent_areas.md` §2 records orthonormality ≈ 1e−4).

## Entry points and cheap decisive tests
Script `polygon_sparse_ops_probe.py` (numpy, 0.14 s; scratchpad `/tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/1d04beeb-ff17-58e2-9826-26d049691b0c/scratchpad/`). MEASURED 2026-10-31 on an irregular convex pentagon, Lebesgue measure, 720-point Gauss–Duffy triangle mesh, graded Chebyshev–Vandermonde QR twice (orthonormality 6e−15), n = 10: degree bands π∂_x = (4,4), π∂_y = (4,4), H_x = (1,1), plain ∂_x = strictly upper (full), πΔ = (3,10) (not banded below), π²Δ = (8,8).

1. **Sharpness test (n ≤ 8, seconds).** Repeat on (i) a domain with curved boundary π of degree 4 (e.g. π = 1 − x⁴ − y⁴ − 0.3x²y), (ii) a non-convex polygon, (iii) weights π^α with α = 1/2, 1. Pre-written kill criterion for sub-problem 1: if the measured bandwidth of π∂_x exceeds b−1 at tolerance 1e−9 for any of these (meaning the integration-by-parts argument fails for some reason, e.g. non-semiclassical weight), the structure theorem is false as stated; if the bandwidth is strictly smaller than b−1 generically, the sharpness claim is false and the theorem is weaker than needed.
2. **Conditioning-law test (n ≤ 30, seconds).** Form the Petrov–Galerkin stiffness for −Δ with trial π·Π_n on the pentagon and measure κ(n) with and without block-diagonal (degree-block) preconditioning. Kill criterion for sub-problem 2: κ growing faster than n⁶ unpreconditioned or faster than n² after block-diagonal preconditioning means the formulation is no better than collocation and the method has no complexity advantage; then sub-problem 2 reduces to an engineering comparison.
3. **Convergence test (n ≤ 40, seconds).** Poisson with a smooth analytic right-hand side on the pentagon; expected algebraic rate limited by corner singularities (compare with Yao–Fasondini–Olver's rates on corner domains). Kill: no convergence beyond 1e−4 at n = 40 (i.e. worse than Lai 2026's orthonormality level) means the numerically generated basis is too inaccurate and sub-problem 5's premise fails.
4. **Transform-cost test (seconds).** Compare the graded Clenshaw evaluation via H_x, H_y against dense Q·c for N up to 5000; kill for sub-problem 4's O(N^{3/2}) claim: measured exponent ≥ 1.9.

## Risks and obstructions
- Nearest recorded obstruction: **Curve-zero ranks (R04/R07)**, hypotheses: modifiers ≥ 0 vanishing on an *interior* curve, lattice/degree orderings — not applicable because π vanishes on the boundary only; but T4 says the straight-edge boundary case needs C3's boundary method and a rank theorem must exclude curve zeros — so sub-problem 3 inherits the rank-5 risk entirely.
- **Q not HSS-compressible**: confirmed and strengthened (not butterfly-compressible); sub-problem 4 escapes only via the recurrence-based route, with exponent 3/2, not log-linear.
- Ownership risk: the Olver/Fasondini/Slevinsky line owns analytic sparse spectral methods on algebraic domains and could extend to general π by the same integration-by-parts argument at any time; the Piñar group owns the semiclassical structure relations (UNVERIFIED fulltext). The candidate's defensible niche is numerically generated bases on domains without product structure plus the structured-NLA layer; a 2026 search found no paper doing that (search-negative).
- Convergence risk: corner domains give algebraic rates only; the method's advantage over Koornwinder-piece domain decomposition must be shown on curved non-Koornwinder boundaries, where spectral rates are possible.
- The π-weighted trial space imposes homogeneous Dirichlet data only; inhomogeneous data need a lifting, standard but adds work.
