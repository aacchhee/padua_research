# Discrete orthogonal polynomials of sampled trajectory measures: recovering bivariate polynomial information from data along a closed curve
Model: claude-fable-5-1, effort xhigh (as requested by the orchestrator) · Angle C · 2026-10-31 · Status: returned

Aliases: curve-supported discrete measures; trajectory measures; non-Lissajous scanning trajectories; Fourier-along-a-curve reconstruction; restriction operator Π_n → L²(γ, dt); Hilbert function of a sampled trigonometric curve; pulled-back Szegő/Laurent problem; MPI beyond Lissajous.

## Central question
For a positive measure supported on (equispaced time samples of) a closed trigonometric curve γ of trigonometric degree m in [−1,1]² (the only places where an MPI-type scanner, a Lissajous-scan microscope or a trajectory-sampled sensor sees the field), which total-degree polynomial information is recoverable from finitely many Fourier coefficients of the signal along γ, with what conditioning as a function of (n, m, γ), and through which structured discrete-OP computation, once γ is not a Lissajous–Chebyshev curve?

## Template theory
One variable: a periodic signal along a parametrized closed curve is a univariate object. The mature theory is (i) trigonometric interpolation and the Fourier/DCT transforms with their exact stability constants; (ii) Szegő/CMV theory of OPs on the unit circle for the pulled-back measure, with the Laurent-polynomial recurrence, the Geronimus/Verblunsky parametrisation and known conditioning of the moment → recurrence maps (Ammar–Gragg–Reichel 1992 for Szegő downdating, cf. brief §4); (iii) restriction/extension of algebraic polynomials to a curve as a question about the Hilbert function of the curve (h(d) = D·d + const for d ≥ D − 2 for an irreducible plane curve of degree D).

The Lissajous–Chebyshev case is exactly the case where (i)–(iii) collapse to classical Chebyshev facts: a polynomial of total degree n restricted to γ(t) = (cos n₁t, cos n₂t) is a cosine polynomial of degree ≤ n·max(n₁,n₂), the time-sampled measure is exact for the product Chebyshev measure up to a known degree, and the node points are Padua/Lissajous points with O(n² log n) DCT transforms (Erb–Kaethner–Dencker–Ahlborg DRNA 2015; Dencker–Erb 2017). A bivariate version for general γ would have to deliver: (a) the Hilbert function and the kernel (ideal) of the restriction Π_n → L²(γ, dt), i.e. which polynomials are invisible along γ; (b) the recurrence matrices (H_x, H_y) of the sampled curve measure, their block sizes (expected to stabilise at the algebraic degree D of γ) and bandwidths, and their relation to the Szegő recurrence of the pulled-back measure on the circle; (c) the conditioning of the restriction operator, i.e. a Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund-type two-sided inequality ‖p‖_{L²(γ,dt)} ≍ ‖p‖_{L²(w)} on Π_n for a reference weight w on the square, as a function of γ; (d) a stable reconstruction algorithm from Fourier data along γ with a state-independent (function-independent) error bound in the spirit of the Lissajous Lebesgue-constant bounds C ln²(n).

1. **Hilbert-function test (n ≤ 8, seconds).** For random trig curves of degree m = 2, 3, 4, compute the rank of the Chebyshev–Vandermonde matrix on M ≫ 2mn time samples for d ≤ 2m+2 in rational arithmetic on a few exact rational-cosine curves, or in double precision with M = 10⁴ and tolerance 1e-8. Pre-written kill criterion for sub-problem 1: if the increments do not stabilise at the algebraic degree of the implicit curve (compute it by resultant) for ≥ 3 of 5 random curves, the "block size = algebraic degree" structure claim is wrong and 1 reduces to a numerical-rank remark.
2. **Conditioning-growth test (seconds).** For the rhodonea family r = cos(kθ), k = 3, 5, 7, 9, and the generic trig family, fit log κ_n against n for n ≤ 8 (where double precision resolves it). Pre-written kill criterion for sub-problems 2 and 4: if κ_n grows faster than exp(c·n) with c > 1 for every non-Chebyshev curve tried (no family with polynomial or mildly exponential growth), then reconstruction beyond degree ≈ 6 is hopeless for any non-Lissajous trajectory, and the programme collapses to the owned Lissajous–Chebyshev case; the whole candidate is then "not returned (collapsed to Erb)". If instead the rhodonea family shows κ_n ≲ n^c, sub-problem 2 has a theorem to prove.
3. **Commutation test for sub-problem 3 (n ≤ 6).** Build H_x, H_y by the seed's Givens IEP on the M sampled nodes and check that both are polynomials in the circulant shift after transformation to the Fourier basis (they are functions of the same unitary, so ‖[H_x, H_y]‖ should be at rounding level even for the truncated sections of the full M×M problem, unlike generic nodes). Kill criterion: if the truncated sections of the Fourier-basis reduction do not commute to O(u·‖H‖) for M = 64, 128, the Szegő-type reduction has no exact structure to exploit and sub-problem 3 is dropped.
4. **Unisolvence of time samples (n ≤ 6; adjacency with exclusion (c)).** Which equispaced sample sets {γ(2πk/M)} are unisolvent for the quotient Π_n / I_γ? Only as a diagnostic; the field-theoretic unisolvence question belongs to (c) and is not pursued here.

## Plausible first paper (≈6 months)
"Discrete orthogonal polynomials and polynomial reconstruction on trigonometric curves beyond Lissajous": (i) Hilbert function and ideal of the sampled measure of a trig curve of degree m, with the degenerate (Chebyshev-variety) cases singled out; (ii) the exact-commutation structure of (H_x, H_y) in the Fourier basis and a Szegő-type reduction algorithm; (iii) measured conditioning laws for rhodonea and generic curves and a reconstruction experiment on a synthetic MPI-type phantom sampled along a non-Lissajous trajectory. Reachable because every ingredient is a finite computation plus classical algebraic facts; the only theorem needed for (i) is the Hilbert-function statement, whose proof is textbook once the implicit degree is known.

## Why it is not an exclusion
- (a) 2-D Schur/Levinson, moments → OPs exact complexity: not touched; sub-problem 5 is exact arithmetic on the curve ideal, not on moment tables, and makes no complexity claim below the curse.
- (b) Finite-precision bivariate Lanczos: the candidate uses the recurrence as a tool; its central question is the restriction/ideal/conditioning theory of a specific measure class. Nearest item: OBST-04 ("Padua sets have M = N, so there is no long run"; regular measures with M ≫ N) and settled-background 007 (Chen–Trogdon scoping). Separation: the hypotheses here are about the support of the measure (a curve, where polynomials of degree ≥ D have a kernel), not about rounding; the sampled curve measure is not a "regular 2D measure" in the Chen–Trogdon sense. The separation collapses if sub-problem 3's exact commutation structure turns out to be the only content, in which case it is a finite-precision statement about a special node family and belongs to (b).
- (c) Unisolvence over fields: entry point 4 is a diagnostic only; no field-theoretic claims.
- (d) MZ constants: (d) concerns CC/Padua rules on Π_{N−1} and their λ_max/λ_min laws. Sub-problem 2 asks for MZ-type constants of a different object (a continuous curve measure versus a reference weight, with a kernel beyond degree D − 1). Separation: the curve measure is not a quadrature rule for the square and the inequality is one-sided beyond degree D − 1. Collapse: if angle D's generators pick up "MZ constants of Lissajous curve measures", sub-problem 2 must be handed over.
- Already judged: "explicit Padua recurrences" (corollary of CDMV + BDMVX) — the Lissajous–Chebyshev case of sub-problem 1 is exactly that corollary and is NOT claimed; the candidate starts where the curve is not a Chebyshev variety. "Histopolation on Padua triangles" (done) — unrelated. "Laurent bivariate OPs" (F10 trivial) — the pulled-back problem is univariate Laurent on the circle, not bivariate Laurent. "Moments → Jacobi as Prony" — not used. The mechanism separating the candidate from the judged items is the kernel of the restriction (the curve ideal) entering the recurrence block sizes; the observation that would collapse it: if every trig curve of interest is a Chebyshev variety up to affine change (so that the Dencker–Erb theory already covers it), the candidate is a renaming.

## Risks and obstructions
- Nearest recorded obstruction: **Q not HSS-compressible** (brief §3, hypotheses: unitary Q from generic nodes; kd-tree order). The candidate escapes because the nodes are not generic: X and Y are polynomials in one circulant shift, so the reduction has exact structure in the Fourier basis (entry point 3 tests exactly this). If the test fails, nothing escapes.
- **Computed H_x, H_y never commute exactly** (brief §3): for sampled curve measures they commute exactly in exact arithmetic for the full M×M problem (both are functions of one unitary), but the truncated sections still need not; the candidate makes no backward-stability claim.
- **Curve-zero ranks R04/R07** (brief §3, modifiers vanishing on an interior curve are pass-band): different object (a weight vanishing on a curve versus a measure supported on a curve); not applicable, but the analogy warns that curve geometry produces ranks growing like n.
- Main scientific risk: exponential conditioning for every non-Chebyshev trajectory (entry point 2's kill). The measured 1.6e8 at degree 9 for a generic degree-5 curve is a warning sign; the rhodonea numbers (1.7e3 at degree 7) are milder. If only near-Chebyshev curves are usable, the applied payoff shrinks to "robustness of Lissajous reconstruction to trajectory distortion", a smaller but still real MPI question (field inhomogeneities distort the nominal Lissajous path).
- Ownership risk: Erb's rhodonea line (2018) and a possible unpublished extension; coordinate before investing in sub-problem 2's rhodonea family.
