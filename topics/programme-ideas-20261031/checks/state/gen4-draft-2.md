# Lebesgue theory of checkerboard (Padua-type) interpolation: second-order term, maxima geometry, and Fekete nodes within the family
Model: claude-fable-5-1, effort xhigh · Angle D · 2026-10-31 (tests 2026-10-31/11-01) · Status: returned

## Central question
Is there an Erdős–Bernstein-type theory for total-degree interpolation on the square — a universal lower bound Λ ≥ (4/π²) log² n − O(log n), an identified second-order term for Padua, and the structure (count and location) of the Lebesgue-function maxima — and are the Fekete points among Harris–Simanek checkerboard sets explicit Gauss–Lobatto–Jacobi families?

## Template theory
Univariate Lebesgue-constant theory on [−1,1]: Erdős lower bound Λ_n ≥ (2/π) log n − O(1) for all nodes; the Chebyshev value (2/π) log n + (2/π)(γ + log(8/π)) + o(1); Kilgore–de Boor–Pinkus optimality via equioscillation of the n−1 interior maxima; Brutman/Vértesi theory of the local maxima of λ_n; local Bernstein theory giving lower bounds on subintervals and for ∫λ_n (Tao, arXiv 2603.21453, March 2026 — web-checked 2026-10-31, univariate only); Fekete points of [−1,1] = Gauss–Lobatto nodes, and Fekete points for Jacobi-weighted Vandermondians = Gauss–Lobatto–Jacobi nodes (Stieltjes). A bivariate version must deliver: (i) a lower bound over *all* unisolvent total-degree sets on the square (none exists in the inputs: NOTES padua_lissajous_theory Q1 "no square analogue of the Erdős/Kilgore–de Boor–Pinkus theory"; web search 2026-10-31 found none); (ii) the second-order term of Λ(Pad_n), open per Kolomoitsev–Lomako 2021 Thm 2.1 (web-checked 2026-10-31: remainder O(log n), no explicit second term); (iii) a theorem for the count of local maxima (Białas-Cież–De Marchi–Suder arXiv 2605.23299: observed inner ≥ n(n−1)/2, total ≥ (n+1)(n+2)/2, open; web-checked 2026-10-31); (iv) explicit Fekete points inside the checkerboard family (Bloom–Bos–Calvi–Levenberg Problem 5: no explicit Fekete points for any K ⊂ ℂ^d, d > 1).

## Sub-problems (≥3, each with a separate payoff)
| # | Sub-problem | Kind | Payoff (what paper) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Second-order term of Λ(Pad_n). MEASURED (n = 10…640, exact corner evaluation, corner is the argmax): Λ(Pad_n) − (4/π²) log² n = 1.212 log n + 1.93 with residual < 0.01; and Λ(Pad_n) − λ_{n+1}^{Cheb}(1)² → ≈ 0.92 slowly (0.68 → 0.913), where λ_{n+1}^{Cheb}(1) is the Lebesgue function at x = 1 of n+1 Chebyshev zeros. Conjecture: Λ(Pad_n) = λ_{n+1}^{Cheb}(1)² + c + o(1), equivalently (4/π²) log² n + 2(2/π)c₁ log n + O(1) with c₁ = (2/π)(γ+log(8/π)) = 0.9625, so the conjectured log-n coefficient is 2(2/π)c₁ = 1.2255 (fixed-a fit gives 1.212, free fits 1.18–1.19: 1–3% agreement; the discrepancy is of the size of the slowly decaying correction, see residual fits). Prove it. | theory + numerics | Paper A: "The second-order term of the Padua Lebesgue constant" |
| 2 | Lower bound for the square: Λ(X) ≥ (4/π²) log² n − C log n for every unisolvent X ⊂ [−1,1]² for Π_n, via a bivariate localisation of Erdős/Tao (restrict to the diagonal or a corner wedge where the Lagrange basis reduces to univariate structure) or via the Lebesgue constant of the Chebyshev projection (MEASURED: the rhombus Dirichlet kernel on 𝕋² has ‖D_n‖_1 / log² n = 1.08 → 0.57 decreasing towards 4/π² = 0.405 at n = 160; the projection constant of Π_n in C([−1,1]²) is a lower bound for minimal Λ up to the Lozinski–Kharshiladze mechanism, which has no multivariate algebraic version in the inputs). | theory | Paper B: "A lower bound for Lebesgue constants of total-degree interpolation on the square" |
| 3 | Maxima geometry: prove #interior maxima ≥ n(n−1)/2 via the Lissajous-cell decomposition (MEASURED: each of the (n+1)(n+2)/2 cells of the generating curve carries at most 1 maximum in all but O(n) cells; cells with 2–3 maxima are the "extra" ones near fundamental-polynomial zero curves; counts reproduce the published table for n ≤ 8 and extend to n = 12: 69/94 interior/total vs bounds 66/91). Mechanism: on each cell the Lebesgue function is a sum of |ℓ_B| whose sign pattern is fixed, hence a polynomial, with a unique interior critical point by a convexity argument in the Chebyshev angles. | theory | Paper C: "Local maxima of the Padua Lebesgue function: a cell-by-cell count" |
| 4 | Fekete points among checkerboard sets: maximise |det V| over Harris–Simanek sets X×Y (checkerboard parity) with endpoints fixed. MEASURED (n = 4…16): log det separates exactly (X-optimum independent of Y to 4e-8, consistent with De Marchi–Usevich 2014 separation), the optimal X is symmetric and fits Gauss–Lobatto–Jacobi(n, α) nodes with α → −1/2 slowly (−0.26, −0.30, −0.33, −0.35, −0.38 for n = 6, 8, 10, 12, 16; residual < 1e-6) while the optimal Y is *asymmetric* (sym. error 0.098 → 0.013); the gain in Λ is 7–8% (4.41 → 4.09 at n = 4, 6.88 → 6.60 at n = 10). Payoff: explicit asymptotically-Fekete-within-family nodes; identification of the Y-asymmetry (degree parity of the checkerboard); link to Bloom–Bos–Calvi–Levenberg Problem 5 restricted to a family. | theory + numerics | Paper D: "Fekete points of Padua-like checkerboard sets" |
| 5 | Certified counts and constants at high degree: interval-Newton on ∇λ_n with DCT-fast Padua evaluation and the ETNA 2024 Chebyshev-mesh brackets, n ≤ 200. | numerics-software | companion software note / appendix |

## Entry points and cheap decisive tests
1. **Corner evaluation (seconds to a minute).** Λ(Pad_n) at the corner (1,−1) by the explicit Lagrange formula (cost O(Nn) with cumulative sums), n = 10·2^j up to 640. Pre-written kill: if the free fit of a log² n + b log n + c gives a ≠ 4/π² by more than 2% or the residual exceeds 0.05, there is no clean second-order law. **Outcome: a = 0.4086 free (4/π² = 0.4053), fixed-a fit b = 1.2121, c = 1.929, max residual 0.0084.** Survives; b is within 1–3% of the conjectured 2(2/π)c₁ = 1.2255 (the residual term decays slowly, so the fit is biased low).
2. **Cell count (20 s).** Count local maxima of λ_n on 641² Chebyshev and uniform meshes, n ≤ 12, map each to its Lissajous cell. Kill: if the number of cells with ≥ 2 maxima grows like n², the cell argument fails. **Outcome: cells with ≥ 2 maxima: 1–6 for n ≤ 12 (O(n) or less) on a 641² mesh; but the counts are mesh-sensitive (n = 8: 28–32 interior on 641²–2001² meshes vs 38 published), so the cell statistic is indicative only and certified counting (sub-problem 5) must precede the theorem.** The test did not kill the cell mechanism, but it did not confirm it either.
3. **Checkerboard Fekete (2 s).** BFGS on log det over interior nodes, n ≤ 16; separability check; GLJ fit. Kill: if the optimum is not separable or not symmetric in X, the GLJ hypothesis dies. **Outcome: separable to 1e-7; X symmetric to 1e-7 and GLJ with α drifting toward −1/2; Y asymmetric.**
4. **Rhombus Dirichlet L¹ (1 s).** ‖D_n^{rhombus}‖_{L¹(𝕋²)}/log² n for n ≤ 160. Kill for sub-problem 2's projection route: if the ratio does not approach 4/π² (e.g. stabilises above 0.6), the projection constant is not asymptotically (4/π²) log² n and the lower bound must come from a different mechanism. **Outcome: 0.57 at n = 160 and still decreasing (the 1D analogue is 0.66 vs limit 0.405 at the same n), so undecided at this n; compute to n = 10⁴ with FFT before committing.**

## Plausible first paper (≈6 months)
Paper A: "Second-order asymptotics of the Lebesgue constant of Padua points". Content: proof that the maximum is attained at the two corners (1,−1), (−1,1) (MEASURED: argmax at those corners on all meshes, values 1.0 at the other two corners); exact corner formula Λ(Pad_n) = Σ_B w_B |K_n(B,c) − T_n(x_B)T_n(x_c)| reduced to univariate Dirichlet-type sums; derivation of the (4/π²) log² n + 2(2/π)c₁ log n + c + o(1) expansion (c₁ = (2/π)(γ + log 8/π)), with c identified or bracketed; comparison with Kolomoitsev–Lomako's O(log n) remainder. Reachable because the corner value is an explicit double sum of Chebyshev cosines with the Padua weights, so the analysis is a two-dimensional Euler–Maclaurin / Fejér-type computation; the conjectured b agrees with the 1D-derived constant to 1–3%, and Λ − λ_{n+1}^{Cheb}(1)² fits c + d/√n with c = 0.96 (alternatives c + d/log n give 1.06; the limit constant is not yet pinned).

## Closest prior work
- Kolomoitsev–Lomako, Appl. Math. Comput. 403 (2021) (arXiv 2009.07114; web-fetched 2026-10-31): Thm 2.1 gives Λ_{mn}^{LC} = (4/π²)(2 log m log n − log² m) + O(log n + p log(m/p)); Padua is (m,n) = (n,n+1); no second-order term; Remark 2.1 says asymptotics of ‖F_mn‖ are "generally unknown". They did NOT identify the corner as argmax nor the log n coefficient.
- Bos–Caliari–De Marchi–Vianello–Xu, JAT 143 (2006): Λ = O(log² n) and the Lagrange formula with the T_n correction (NOTES; MAP has no PDF).
- Białas-Cież–De Marchi–Suder, arXiv 2605.23299 (May 2026; web-fetched 2026-10-31): tables for n ≤ 8, open questions on counts; no cell decomposition, no proofs in 2D.
- Beberok–Białas-Cież–De Marchi, Constr. Approx. 63 (2026): Λ_MP = O(n²) proved; "estimate can be improved"; lower bound open (NOTES recent_padua_cubature_theory P7).

- Bos–De Marchi–Waldron DRNA 2 (2009); De Marchi–Usevich LAA 449 (2014); de Camargo–De Marchi DRNA 2015 (CH records LIT-030/031/041): Vandermonde product formula separates in X and Y — the separation we measured for the Fekete optimum is a consequence; they did NOT optimise the nodes or relate them to GLJ families.

## Why it is not an exclusion
- (a), (b): no complexity or finite-precision content.
- (c) Unisolvence over fields: sub-problem 4 uses Harris–Simanek checkerboard sets over ℝ with *ordered* nodes and optimises a determinant; (c) studies when the determinant is nonzero over arbitrary fields. Separation: an extremal problem on the real family vs a vanishing criterion over fields. Collapse observation: if CH's PD-T001 factorisation gives the determinant as an explicit product that makes the Fekete optimisation a univariate Stieltjes electrostatics problem in each variable, sub-problem 4 becomes a corollary of (c) + Stieltjes (this is plausible and is exactly what the GLJ fit suggests; then sub-problem 4 shrinks to identifying α(n) and the Y-asymmetry).
- (d) MZ constants: unrelated (sup-norm interpolation operator vs L² norm equivalence).
- Already judged: "Morrow–Patterson O(n²)" is proved — we do not touch MP's upper bound; the MP lower bound (P7) is a possible extension of sub-problem 2 and is not on the judged list. "Histopolation on Padua triangles" not touched. ## Risks and obstructions
- Nearest recorded obstruction: "Morrow–Patterson O(n²) proved" (upper bound; lower bound/exact constant open per P7) — not an obstruction to this candidate, a possible extension.
- Competition risk (sub-problem 3): Kraków–Padova may prove the count first; mitigation: lead with Paper A, which they have not announced.
- Risk: the lower bound (sub-problem 2) may require genuinely new multivariate Bernstein-type tools; Tao's local theory is univariate and its 2D analogue is not obvious. The projection-constant route needs the Chebyshev-projection Lebesgue constant on the square (rhombus Dirichlet kernel, MEASURED slow convergence) and a multivariate Lozinski–Kharshiladze; both open. This sub-problem is the 3–5-year escalation, not the first paper.
- Risk: the second-order constant c may depend on arithmetic properties of n (parity effects as in Kolomoitsev–Lomako's p log(m/p) term); MEASURED only for n = 10·2^j — test odd n and n ≡ 2 mod 4 before claiming a single c.
