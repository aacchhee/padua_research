# Bivariate orthogonal rational functions: which nested pole structures admit short recurrences, their pencil IEPs, and rational least squares/Krylov in two variables
Model: claude-fable-5-1, effort xhigh (as requested by the orchestrator) · Angle A · 2026-10-31 · Status: returned

## Central question
For a discrete inner product on nodes in ℝ² (or ℂ²) and a nested sequence of rational spaces V_0 ⊂ V_1 ⊂ … with prescribed poles (lines, product poles, or curves), which pole structures make the orthonormal basis satisfy a graded short recurrence encoded by a pair of generalized-Hessenberg pencils (H_x,K_x), (H_y,K_y) sharing one unitary Q, how is that pair computed stably by a unitary IEP (seed-type rotations or a two-operator rational Lanczos), and what do rational least squares and rational Krylov for commuting pairs gain from it?

## Template theory
Univariate orthogonal rational functions (ORFs): Bultheel–González-Vera–Hendriksen–Njåstad (CUP 1999); Van Buggenhout–Van Barel–Vandebril, "Generation of orthogonal rational functions by procedures for structured matrices", Numer. Algorithms (2022), arXiv:2103.04788 (pencil IEP: X Q K = Q H with H, K Hessenberg, rotations and updating; rational Arnoldi equivalence); Mach–Van Barel–Vandebril TW629/JCAM 2014 (extended Hessenberg IEP for Laurent/extended Krylov); Vermeiren arXiv:2601.11317 (vector ORFs, pairs of k-Hessenberg matrices, lightning poles); Faghih et al. arXiv:2407.05945 (rational least squares via Arnoldi); rational Gauss quadrature (Van Assche–Vanherwegen 1993; Gautschi; Deckers–Bultheel). Key theorems: (i) with a pole sequence ξ_1, ξ_2, … the ORFs satisfy a three-term recurrence with coefficients depending on consecutive poles; (ii) the recurrence is a Hessenberg pencil, and the IEP "given nodes, weights, poles → (H,K)" is solved by rotations (Van Buggenhout et al. 2022, Thm on the pencil structure); (iii) rational Krylov spaces are generated by the same recurrence (Ruhe); (iv) rational Gauss rules come from the generalized eigenproblem of the pencil.
A bivariate version must deliver: (a) a classification of nested rational spaces in two variables for which multiplication by x and y map V_n into "V_{n+1} times a denominator factor" — candidates: one linear pole ℓ_{n+1}(x,y) = a x + b y − c per degree (V_n = Π_n/∏_{k≤n}ℓ_k), product poles (φ_{a,b} = x^a y^b/(p_a(x)q_b(y)), tensor pencils), and pole curves; (b) the corresponding pencil pair structure (which of H_x, K_x, H_y, K_y are generalized Hessenberg, how the pole of degree n+1 enters: (a X + b Y − c)Q_{n+1} ∈ span(Q_n, XQ_n, YQ_n)); (c) a unitary IEP and a windowed rational Lanczos with O(MN^{3/2}) cost; (d) the implicit-Q uniqueness for pencil pairs; (e) applications: rational least squares in two variables with poles clustered near a singular curve (bivariate "lightning"), and rational Krylov approximations of f(A,B)u for commuting A, B with shifts — the scalar bilinear form uᵀf(A,B)u is the Gauss-rational cubature of draft 1's type.

## Entry points and cheap decisive tests
1. **Recurrence window (seconds).** M = 25 random nodes, random weights; poles: one random line ℓ_k per degree k = 1..4 (avoid the nodes). Build V_n = Π_n/π_n (n ≤ 4, N = 15) by explicit evaluation, orthonormalise (QR), form A = Qᵀ diag(x) Q, B = Qᵀ diag(y) Q and, for each new degree, the matrix of coefficients expressing ℓ_{n+1}(X,Y)φ_{n+1,s} in the basis. Quantity: the number of nonzeros per column of the coefficient matrix, as a function of n, and the lowest degree index involved. Pre-written kill: if ℓ_{n+1}(X,Y)φ_{n+1,s} needs components from degrees < n−1 (window growing beyond 2n+1-type) for a generic linear-pole sequence, the linear-pole class has no short recurrence and sub-problem 1 reduces to product poles (then the programme shrinks to a transcription of the univariate pencil IEP — note-sized; record as such).
2. **Product poles (seconds).** φ_{a,b} = x^a y^b/(p_a(x) q_b(y)) with ξ_a, η_b random outside [−1,1]; same measurements; expected: (X − ξ_{a+1}) and (Y − η_{b+1}) shifted pencils with the seed's generalized-Hessenberg pattern; kill: pattern not generalized Hessenberg → classification claim wrong.
3. **Conditioning gain (seconds).** Fit f(x,y) = √((x−1.01)² + y²)... on 400 random points in the square by (i) polynomial degree 10, (ii) rational with 6 linear poles through (1.01, 0) at random angles (degree 10), measuring LS error and the condition number of the ORF Gram (should be 1) vs the partial-fraction basis. Kill for sub-problem 3's motivation: rational LS does not beat polynomial LS by ≥ 10× at equal dimension on this test (then poles-per-degree is the wrong pole model).

## Why it is not an exclusion
- (a) 2D Schur/Levinson: no moment complexity; the Loewner/Cauchy displacement remark in sub-problem 3 is a structured-Gram observation, not an exponent claim; collapse observation: if the programme's value turns out to be a fast solver for two-level Cauchy-like systems, it is (a)'s / bttb's.
- (b) finite-precision bivariate Lanczos: exact-arithmetic recurrence structure and IEP come first; the finite-precision theory of the rational window recurrence would be a later, (b)-dependent question. Separation: pencils with poles vs the polynomial pair; collapse: if every admissible pole structure reduces by a change of variables to the polynomial pair on transformed nodes (true for product poles with a single pole each, i.e. Möbius maps per variable — which is why single-pole classes are excluded from the claim).
- (c), (d): untouched.
- "Already judged": Laurent bivariate OPs (F10, trivial as seed instance with X, X⁻¹, Y, Y⁻¹): nearest. Separation: general pole sequences (one new pole per degree) are not generated by a fixed finite set of commuting diagonal operators, so the seed's "four operators" trick does not apply; the pencil (shifted operator per degree) is the new mechanism. Collapse observation: if, for every admissible structure, the space V_n equals the span of a fixed finite operator family applied to earlier basis vectors (as for Laurent), then the candidate is F10 again. Humet Ch. 5 "rational orders": Humet's slide lists rational *orders* for one variable within the nested-space IEP H_k = Q*Z_kQ; a bivariate rational order with per-degree poles is not listed (NOTES `adv_F2_F5_F10_F9.md` F10 cites slide 13 — recheck the slide before claiming). Risk flagged.
- Measure modification (narrow): fixed denominator D(x,y) for all degrees is a reweighting by 1/D² — excluded from the candidate by construction (the poles must grow with the degree).

## Risks and obstructions
- Nearest recorded obstruction: "Huhtanen 2002 collapse" — for commuting Hermitian pairs rational Krylov of the pair might reduce to rational Krylov of a single normal operator N = X + iY with complex shifts; the hypothesis (B = q(A), deg q ~ M) gives no graded structure, so the collapse does not apply to the graded spaces, but it does mean sub-problem 4 must compare against rational Krylov on N = X + iY with complex poles (polyanalytic rational spaces) — a real competitor to be included as baseline.
- Ownership: KU Leuven (Van Barel, Vandebril, Van Buggenhout, Vermeiren) owns univariate ORF IEPs actively (2021–2026); a bivariate extension is a natural next step for them. AC should coordinate (co-authorship is the realistic route) — not an exclusion by the campaign's rules, but the strongest competition risk among the three drafts.
- Mathematical risk: the linear-pole-per-degree class may have window growth (entry point 1's kill), leaving only product poles, which are a near-transcription; then the programme is note-sized.

## Pre-gate screen (MEASURED by the generator, 2026-10-31; script in the generator's scratchpad, not committed)
Scope: M = 40 random nodes in [−1,1]², random weights, one random linear pole ℓ_k = cos θ_k x + sin θ_k y − c_k per degree with |c_k| ∈ [1.6, 2.5] (poles are lines outside the square), V_n = Π_n/π_n, n ≤ 5, orthonormal basis by Householder QR, three seeds.
- Entry point 1 (window of the recurrence): for every t = 1..4 and every s, ℓ_{t+1}φ_{t+1,s} lies in span{φ of degree t−1 and t} + x·span{φ of degree t} + y·span{φ of degree t} to relative residual ≤ 2e-14, and NOT in the span restricted to degree ≥ t for odd t (residual 0.3–0.5). So the linear-pole-per-degree class has a three-degree window (t−1, t, t+1), the same width as the polynomial pair; the kill criterion ("components from degree < t−1 needed") did not fire. The algebraic reason is the identity Π_{t+1}/π_t = V_t + xV_t + yV_t plus orthogonality of φ_{t+1} to V_t — the window statement itself (why degree t−2 and lower drop out) still needs a proof (conditional reasoning: ⟨ℓ_{t+1}φ_{t+1}, ψ⟩ for ψ of degree ≤ t−2 equals ⟨φ_{t+1}, ℓ_{t+1}ψ⟩ with ℓ_{t+1}ψ ∈ Π_{t−1}/π_{t−2}; this lies in V_t only if π_t/π_{t−2} divides appropriately — it does, since Π_{t−1}/π_{t−2} = (ℓ_{t−1}ℓ_t Π_{t−1})/π_t ⊂ Π_{t+1}/π_t, but that is not V_t; the measured vanishing therefore depends on the product structure of the poles and must be proved, not assumed).
- Entry points 2 and 3 not run.
- Pencil relations (same scope, M = 60, n = 6, one seed): the number of independent linear relations of the form x·(Σ_{deg∈{t−1,t,t+1}} c_j φ_j) = Σ_{deg∈{t−1,t,t+1}} d_j φ_j is exactly t+2 for t = 1..5, identical to the polynomial control run (rational = False), and widening the window to degree t−2 adds only the expected extra relations. So the linear-pole class carries a pencil pair (H_x,K_x) with the polynomial pair's block band — the object sub-problem 2 needs exists in this scope. The polynomial control confirms the counting (t+2 = dim of degree t+1).
