# Joint spectral theory of banded commuting pairs: two-index Sturm counts, bisection, relative perturbation, and certified joint spectra of nearly commuting sections
Model: claude-fable-5-1, effort xhigh (as requested by the orchestrator) · Angle B · 2026-10-31 · Status: returned

## Central question
Is there a two-index Sturm/Sylvester theory for commuting symmetric banded pairs (H_x, H_y) — an inertia-based count of joint eigenvalues in a rectangle computable by banded factorizations, a 2-D bisection with proved monotonicity and O(N²)-type cost per probe, and a Demmel–Kahan-type relative perturbation theory for the joint eigenvalues of pairs given by their bivariate recurrence coefficients — and does the count stay certified when the pair commutes only up to a defect ‖[H_x, H_y]‖ = δ (the computed sections), with an explicit δ-dependent tolerance?

## Template theory
Univariate, mature, and listed in brief §4 as "not in inputs": Sturm sequences and bisection for symmetric tridiagonals (Givens 1954; Wilkinson; Sylvester's law of inertia via LDLᵀ of T − σI, backward stable per Demmel–Kahan / Kahan's "accurate eigenvalues of a symmetric tridiagonal" 1966); Demmel–Kahan 1990 (relative perturbation of bidiagonal singular values and of Jacobi matrices given by their entries: relative changes of entries give relative changes of eigenvalues; qd/dqds); the Gauss-quadrature consequence that the nodes of a rule are computed to high relative accuracy from the recurrence coefficients (Laurie; Gautschi). What a two-index version must deliver: (T1) an inertia theorem: for an exactly commuting pair with real joint spectrum {(x_i, y_i)}, the number of joint eigenvalues in the rectangle (a, a′)×(b, b′) equals ½(n₊(M_x M_y) − N + n₊(M_x) + n₊(M_y)) with M_x = (H_x − a)(a′ − H_x), M_y analogous — a two-line consequence of simultaneous diagonalisability (checked, MEASURED below), but the point is (T2) a factorization-based evaluation (LDLᵀ of the banded product, or of M_x restricted to the M_y-positive invariant subspace) with a backward-stability statement and a cost of O(N b²) = O(N²) per probe (bandwidth b ≈ √(2N) of the pair; 4b of the product); (T3) a 2-D bisection (quadtree refinement) with monotone counts and a termination theorem, giving each joint eigenvalue to absolute accuracy ε in O(N² log²(1/ε)) per node cluster, and the joint eigenvectors by inverse iteration on the pair; (T4) a relative-perturbation theorem for joint eigenvalues of banded commuting pairs under relative entrywise perturbations of the recurrence coefficients (the bivariate analogue of Demmel–Kahan for Jacobi matrices), at least for pairs with the Padua/Harris–Simanek sparsity (entries in {1, ½}); (T5) a nearly-commuting extension: for ‖[H_x, H_y]‖ ≤ δ, the inertia count is unchanged for rectangles whose boundary is at distance ≥ c(N) δ^{α} from every joint eigenvalue, with explicit c and α (the measured count below is unchanged up to δ = 10⁻², far beyond what a generic almost-commuting bound would give; the right exponent is the open question).

## Entry points and cheap decisive tests
1. **Inertia-count test (done, seconds; scratchpad `sturm_pair_check.py`, `nearly_commuting_probe.py`).** Random real nodes, n = 8, N = 45, Chebyshev–Vandermonde QR sections; commutator 1.9e-15; block-tridiagonality 2e-15. For four rectangles the formula ½(n₊(M_xM_y) − N + n₊(M_x) + n₊(M_y)) returned the true counts 10, 8, 24, 8 (MEASURED, this scope). Under random symmetric pattern-preserving perturbations with ‖[H_x,H_y]‖ = 1.1e-12 … 1.1e-2 the counts were unchanged for rectangles whose boundaries were ≥ 7e-4 from the nearest node coordinate (MEASURED; the symmetrised product was used for the inertia). Pre-written kill for the next step (not yet run): if at N ≥ 231 (n = 20) the count computed through a banded LDLᵀ of the symmetrised product (not eigvalsh) disagrees with the true count for rectangles at distance ≥ 10⁻³ from nodes on more than 1 % of 1000 random rectangles, the product-based Sturm object is not stably evaluable and T2 needs the restricted-subspace object instead; if that also fails, sub-problem 1 is dead.
2. **Relative-perturbation test (seconds, n ≤ 8).** Padua n = 8 with cubature weights (closed-form sparse pair, entries {1, ½}): perturb every nonzero entry by a relative η = 10⁻¹⁰ random factor and measure max relative change of joint eigenvalues |Δx_i|/|x_i|, |Δy_i|/|y_i| (nodes include values near 0 — use |Δ|/dist-to-nearest-other-node too). Kill criterion for T4 as stated: if the relative changes exceed 10³ η for a node family with uniformly bounded recurrence coefficients, no Demmel–Kahan-type relative theorem holds in that generality and T4 must be restricted (e.g. to nodes away from 0 or to graded relative perturbations).
3. **δ-exponent test (seconds, n ≤ 10).** For the perturbed pairs of test 1, find for each rectangle the largest δ for which the count is still correct, as a function of the boundary-to-node distance d; fit δ_max ∝ d^{1/α}. Kill criterion for T5 as a structured improvement: if α ≈ ½ (the generic almost-commuting scaling), structure gives nothing beyond Lin-type bounds and sub-problem 3 shrinks to a numerical comparison.

## Why it is not an exclusion
- (a): no exact-arithmetic complexity claim; test data only.
- (b): nearest items are L3 (backward interpretation: does a nearby measure exist whose sections are the computed pair) and LZ 034 (R10, deferred: He–Kressner–Plestenjak on sections). Separation: this is a FORWARD spectral theory for a given pair (count, localisation, perturbation), with no measure and no window recurrence; the δ-theorem (T5) is a statement about inertia of a given nearly commuting pair, not about the existence of a representing measure. Collapse: if the only proof of T5 goes through constructing a nearby exactly commuting pair (then it is Lin's theorem applied, and L3's object); and if L3 resumes 034 with exactly the inertia/bisection mechanism, sub-problem 3 is theirs and 1, 2, 4 remain.
- (c), (d): cubature EXISTENCE (Γ, minimal rules, Li–Sun–Xu) is excluded/angle D; sub-problem 5 is explicitly "given Γ, compute" and the 3D Padua goal is not pursued.
- Already judged: "3D Padua via cubature" (not pursued); "moments → Jacobi as Prony" (not touched: input here is the pair, not moments); "f(A,B) total-degree Krylov" (Huhtanen collapse: commuting Hermitian pairs reduce to one normal matrix — this is relevant: Z = H_x + iH_y is normal, so the joint spectrum is the spectrum of one normal banded matrix and could be computed by QR on Z; separation: Sturm counts, bisection and relative perturbation have no analogue for non-Hermitian normal matrices, and the banded symmetric structure of the pair is lost in Z; collapse if a stable structured QR on the banded normal Z at O(N²) exists and gives the same certificates).

## Risks and obstructions
Nearest recorded obstruction: "Computed H_x, H_y never commute exactly" (brief §3) — this candidate takes it as the hypothesis of T5 rather than as a block; it escapes the "no literal structured backward stability" conclusion because it never claims one. Nearest second: "Huhtanen 2002 collapse" (commuting Hermitian pair = one normal matrix): the joint eigenproblem is in principle a single normal eigenproblem; the programme's value rests on the symmetric/banded tools (inertia, bisection, relative accuracy) that the normal formulation does not have, and on the nearly commuting regime where Z is not normal. Third: cost — the product M_xM_y has bandwidth 4b ≈ 4√(2N), so an LDLᵀ probe costs O(N b²) = O(N²); 2-D bisection to accuracy ε over N clusters costs O(N³ log²(1/ε)) if every cluster is refined separately, which does not beat dense joint diagonalisation; the gain is in certification, selective computation (a few nodes / a region), and relative accuracy, not in asymptotic cost unless probes share work (HSS/divide-and-conquer, draft 1). This must be stated honestly in the first paper. Fourth: Sturm counts on the product M_xM_y use a symmetrised matrix that is not the Sturm object in the nearly commuting case; the restricted-subspace variant may be needed (test 1's next step decides).
