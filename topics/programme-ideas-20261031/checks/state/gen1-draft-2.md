# Discrete bivariate Sobolev (Hermite-data) orthogonal polynomials: non-normal commuting node operators, band-plus-low-rank recurrences, and the Alexander–Hirschowitz breakdown
Model: claude-fable-5-1, effort xhigh (as requested by the orchestrator) · Angle A · 2026-10-31 · Status: returned

## Central question
For a discrete bivariate Sobolev inner product ⟨p,q⟩ = Σ_i w_i p(z_i)q(z_i) + Σ_{j∈S} λ_j ∇p(z_j)·∇q(z_j) (values at M points, gradients at s of them), what is the structure of the recurrence matrices of the graded orthonormal basis, which IEP computes them stably by unitary transformations, and how do breakdowns of the recurrence relate to the Hilbert function of fat-point ideals?

## Template theory
Univariate Sobolev OPs: Marcellán–Xu survey (Expo. Math. 2015); discrete Sobolev inner products with mass points (Alfaro–Marcellán–Rezola–Ronveaux); coherent pairs (Iserles–Koch–Nørsett–Sanz-Serna 1991; Meijer's classification); the computational side: Van Buggenhout, Numer. Math. 2023 ("On generating Sobolev orthogonal polynomials", arXiv:2302.10691) — the Sobolev recurrence matrix is Hessenberg = symmetric tridiagonal + low rank (rank = number of derivative functionals), generated by a Krylov/Hessenberg IEP with updating; Faghih–Van Barel–Van Buggenhout–Vandebril 2024 (Arnoldi for polynomial/rational Sobolev LS). Key univariate theorems: (i) multiplication by x is not self-adjoint, so the recurrence is long in general, (ii) for discrete Sobolev parts the non-symmetric part has rank ≤ #derivative functionals, so a structured (banded + low-rank) short representation exists, (iii) coherent pairs give fixed-length recurrences, (iv) the IEP is an orthogonal reduction of a (diagonal + nilpotent) node operator.
A bivariate version must deliver: (a) the structure theorem for the pair (H_x,H_y) = Qᵀ(X̃,Ỹ)Q with X̃, Ỹ commuting non-normal node operators (Jordan-type 3×3 blocks at the s gradient points); (b) a unitary IEP (seed-type Givens with Jordan node blocks, or a Huhtanen–Larsen-type window recurrence with a rank-s correction) and its cost; (c) uniqueness/implicit-Q for the pair with non-normal operators; (d) the role of Hermite unisolvence: the Gram of Π_k under the Sobolev product is singular exactly when Π_k contains a polynomial vanishing to order 2 at all of S and to order 1 on the rest — the Hilbert function of the fat-point scheme (Alexander–Hirschowitz theorem for generic double points), so breakdowns are classified by a 1995 theorem of algebraic geometry rather than by "non-unisolvent prefixes".

## Entry points and cheap decisive tests
1. **Rank structure (seconds).** M = 12 random points, s = 2 gradient points, degrees ≤ 3 (N = 10); build the Sobolev Gram via the (M+2s)×N Hermite–Vandermonde and node operators X̃, Ỹ (diag(x) plus unit couplings from the value row to the ∂_x row at the s points); Q = orthonormal basis; compute H_x = QᵀX̃Q. Quantities: the maximal rank over i of the blocks H_x[deg ≤ i, deg ≥ i+2] (upper off-band quasiseparable rank), the same for the lower blocks, and rank(H_x − H_xᵀ). Pre-written kill: if the upper off-band block rank exceeds 2s or any lower off-band block is nonzero on ≥ 1 of 20 random instances, the structure theorem as stated is false (then record the observed rank law).
2. **Alexander–Hirschowitz breakdown (exact, seconds).** 5 random rational double points (s = M = 5, no extra value points), degree 4: the Hermite–Vandermonde is 15×15; compute its rank over ℚ (sympy/fractions). Kill for sub-problem 3's thesis: rank 15 (no defect) — AH predicts rank 14 (the double conic). Control: 4 double points, degree 3 (12 = dim Π_3 − ... actually 12 functionals vs 10 = dim Π_3; use degree 4 with 4 double points + 3 simple points = 15 functionals: AH predicts full rank). Then run the exact Givens IEP in ℚ and confirm a zero pivot at degree 4 for the 5-double-point case.
3. **Commutator defect (seconds).** On instance 1, rank of [H_x, H_y] and of X̃Ỹ − ỸX̃ (= 0 by construction); the sections' commutator rank vs s.

## Why it is not an exclusion
- (a) 2D Schur/Levinson: exact complexity of moments → OPs; here no moments, no exponent claims. Sub-problem 3 uses exact arithmetic only to certify defects (rank of a 15×15 matrix).
- (b) finite-precision bivariate Lanczos: its object is the standard discrete inner product with normal node operators (diagonal X, Y). Separation: non-normal commuting node operators (nilpotent parts) and the resulting non-symmetric, band+low-rank recurrences — a different object whose Paige-type theory, if ever needed, is downstream. Collapse observation: if the Sobolev pair reduces by a cheap similarity to a standard pair on an enlarged point set (it does not, since X̃ is not diagonalisable), then (b) covers it.
- (c) unisolvence over fields: nearest for sub-problem 3 (unisolvence of simple-point checkerboards via alternation functionals). Separation: fat points / Hermite functionals and Sobolev quadratic forms; the alternation mechanism is for simple nodes on rectangular patterns. Collapse: if every Hermite configuration of interest reduces to a simple-point checkerboard by a limit argument that the charter branch adopts.
- (d) MZ constants: untouched.
- "Already judged": multiple-OP stability (Leuven's future work) is the nearest in spirit (Leuven toolkit transferred); separation: Sobolev ≠ MOP (one inner product, non-normal operators vs several measures); Humet Ch. 5 vector weights (separation: rank-one matrix weights have normal node operators). F10 Laurent (trivial as seed instance): the Sobolev case is NOT a seed instance because the seed assumes normal (diagonal) node operators and real/complex nodes; the Jordan blocks change the implicit-Q uniqueness and the band structure.

## Risks and obstructions
- Nearest recorded obstruction: "Computed H_x, H_y never commute exactly" (brief §3). Here even the exact pair has the same property only through the rank-s defect of the node operators' non-normality; the obstruction's hypothesis (floating point, any bivariate IEP) applies to the algorithm of sub-problem 2 but not to the structure theorem.
- "Huhtanen 2002 collapse" needs commuting Hermitian pairs; X̃, Ỹ are not Hermitian, so no reduction to a single normal operator is available — this cuts both ways (no shortcut, no collapse).
- Ownership risk: Leuven (Van Buggenhout, Van Barel, Vandebril) could extend their univariate Sobolev IEP to several variables at any time; AC should coordinate. Analytic multivariate Sobolev theory is owned by the Granada group (Piñar, Fernández, Pérez) and Xu; they do not do discrete numerics.
- Value risk: who needs gradient-data bivariate least squares at high degree? Gradient-enhanced surrogates and PDE-residual fitting are plausible but unproven customers.
