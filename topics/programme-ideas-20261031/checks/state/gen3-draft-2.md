# Phase-space design theory for continuous-variable quantum tomography: Padua points versus structured designs in the Gaussian-weighted bivariate polynomial model
Model: claude-fable-5-1, effort xhigh (as requested by the orchestrator) · Angle C · 2026-10-31 · Status: returned

Aliases: Padua-point tomography; Husimi-Q / Wigner interpolation; Landon-Cardinal–Govia–Clerk 2018; Gaussian-weighted bivariate Vandermonde; truncated-Fock sensing matrix; phase-space sampling design; Gauss–Laguerre ring designs; optimal displacement amplitudes; Fock-cutoff conditioning.

## Central question
For a single bosonic mode with Fock cutoff N, the Husimi Q-function (and the Wigner function) is e^{−|α|²} times a bivariate polynomial of bidegree (N, N) in (ᾱ, α) with (N+1)² unknown coefficients ρ_{jk}; which finite phase-space designs {α_i} minimise the conditioning of the linear map ρ ↦ (Q(α_i))_i for a given number of settings, what is the exact role of the Padua points used in the only published polynomial-interpolation scheme (Landon-Cardinal–Govia–Clerk PRL 2018), and how does the answer change when positivity/trace constraints and finite-shot statistics are folded into the design?

## Template theory
One variable: the theory of weighted polynomial interpolation and least-squares designs for the Hermite/Laguerre weight: Gauss–Hermite and Gauss–Laguerre nodes, Christoffel functions of e^{−x²}, Mhaskar–Rakhmanov–Saff ranges (the polynomial "lives" on |x| ≲ √(2N)), weighted Lebesgue constants, and the conditioning of weighted Vandermonde matrices V_{ij} = e^{−x_i²/2} H_j(x_i) at Gauss nodes (orthogonal up to scaling). In two variables with the radial weight e^{−|α|²} the natural counterpart exists only in part: the polynomials ᾱ^j α^k/√(j!k!) are orthonormal in L²(ℂ, e^{−|α|²}dα/π) (Bargmann–Fock), the angular variable separates (coefficient p = k − j is a Fourier index), and the radial problem for fixed p is a univariate Laguerre problem in r². A bivariate design theory would have to deliver: (a) the conditioning of the Gaussian-weighted bivariate Vandermonde matrix on a design {α_i} and its minimisation over designs of fixed cardinality (the 1D answer is "Gauss nodes"); (b) a weighted Lebesgue-constant/Christoffel theory for the truncated Fock space on the relevant disk |α| ≲ √N (MRS range analogue); (c) the design's behaviour under the noise model (Gaussian errors per point; finite shots) and under the physicality projection; (d) extension to multimode (the polynomial is in 2d variables; the design question becomes genuinely multivariate and Padua points no longer exist beyond d = 1 complex dimension = 2 real).

1. **Optimality-within-rings test (N ≤ 8, seconds).** Replace Gauss–Laguerre radii by (i) equispaced radii, (ii) radii optimised by a Nelder–Mead on log κ. Pre-written kill criterion for sub-problem 1: if the optimised radii beat Gauss–Laguerre radii by more than a factor 2 in κ for some N ≤ 8, the closed-form optimality claim is false and sub-problem 1 shrinks to "ring designs are good", a remark. If Padua (any R) beats the best ring design at some N ≤ 8, the Padua choice has a conditioning justification the programme would have to explain instead.
2. **Growth-law test (N ≤ 12, seconds).** Fit log κ_ring(N) and log κ_Padua(N); the table suggests both exponential-type growth with κ_Padua/κ_ring increasing (1.1 → 7.8). Kill criterion for sub-problem 2: if the ratio stops growing (stays within [1, 3] for N = 9..12), Padua and ring designs are equivalent up to constants and the "why Padua" question has a one-line answer (any reasonable design with the right scale).
3. **Error-law test for sub-problem 2 (N ≤ 8, seconds).** Interpolate e^{−|α|²}p(α) for random ρ on Padua(2N) scaled by R and measure the coefficient-growth quantity whose boundedness the PRL assumes; kill: if it grows like C^N with C > 2 for the κ-optimal R, the PRL's quasi-exponential claim is design-limited rather than state-limited and the paper must say so.
4. **Christoffel-adaptivity test for sub-problem 4 (N = 6, seconds).** Compute the discrete-OP Christoffel function of the Gauss–Laguerre ring design and compare its level sets with the PRL's threshold region; kill: if the Christoffel-guided subset of 65 points (their thresholded count) is not better conditioned than their thresholded Padua subset by at least a factor 2, drop sub-problem 4's adaptivity claim.

## Why it is not an exclusion
- (a), (b), (c): untouched (no exact bivariate moment problem, no finite-precision recurrence theory, no unisolvence over fields).
- (d) MZ constants: the conditioning numbers κ(A) are MZ-type constants of a weighted design; the separation is the Gaussian weight and the complex-conjugate bidegree structure (not a quadrature rule on [−1,1]² for Π_n). It would collapse if angle D proposes MZ constants of Gaussian-weighted designs, which its listed M1–M7 do not.
- Already judged: "empirical Christoffel functions via QR" (computation is standard) — sub-problem 4 does not propose a computation but an extremal/adaptivity question for a specific weighted model, and it is the weakest sub-problem; the programme stands on 1–3 without it. "Histopolation on Padua triangles" (done) — unrelated. "Multivariate Stieltjes d ≥ 4" (trivial) — sub-problem 5 uses it as a tool only. The separating mechanism from the judged items: the Gaussian weight and the exact angular decoupling (block-diagonal Gram in p = k − j for any rotationally equispaced design), which produces a closed-form design theory that no Padua/Christoffel computation gives. Observation that would collapse the separation: if the Shen et al. ring configurations with their numerically optimised radii already coincide with Gauss–Laguerre radii and their κ asymptotics are already the closed form (their κ ~ m_c^{1/2} is for the Q_n sensing map, not for Q, so this is not yet the case), sub-problem 1 is a rederivation.

## Risks and obstructions
- Nearest recorded obstruction: none of the brief's §3 obstructions applies directly; the nearest is **Padua hyperinterpolation trivial** (Gram = I + rank-1), which says that on the square the Padua design is already quasi-orthogonal — but for the Gaussian-weighted bidegree space the Padua Gram is not I + rank-1 (MEASURED: κ up to 6.2e4 at N = 8), so the obstruction does not transfer.
- Risk 1: ownership by the physics community (Shen et al. 2016 already optimise designs numerically; a group could publish the analytic version any time). Mitigation: the first paper is short and quick.
- Risk 2: the quantity physicists care about is fidelity after physical projection and under finite shots, not κ; sub-problem 3 addresses this, but a reviewer may judge κ-optimality as a mathematical curiosity. Mitigation: report fidelity on standard states (cat, Fock superpositions) with the PRL's noise levels.
- Risk 3: exponential growth of κ in N for every design (the table shows ×2.7–3.4 per unit N for rings) means the design question matters only for N ≲ 15; this is however the regime of all current experiments (the PRL used N ≈ 10).
- Fit risk: AC has no quantum-optics background; the mathematics is self-contained but the venue and the noise models need a collaborator.
