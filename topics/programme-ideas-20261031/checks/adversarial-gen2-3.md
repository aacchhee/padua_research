## Attack (ii): really a note

After removing what is known, nothing programme-sized remains.

- **T1 (inertia identity).** Two lines from simultaneous diagonalisability (the generator says so; replicated, `t1_replicate_n8.py`). Not a theorem.
- **T2 (stable banded evaluation).** The question "compute the inertia of a banded symmetric indefinite matrix stably in O(Nb²)" is classical: Wilkinson 1965, Martin–Wilkinson 1967/1971 (band equations with interchanges), Gupta 1969–70, Scott 1984 (as listed by Druinsky–Carlebach–Toledo, NLAA 2017, whose own inertia-revealing factorization covers any sparse symmetric matrix), Jones–Patrick 1989 (ICASE 89-37; banded Bunch–Kaufman). The product S = sym(M_xM_y) is just one more banded symmetric indefinite matrix. MEASURED here (`t2_ldl_N231.py`): unpivoted banded LDLᵀ of S at N = 231 gives 0 wrong counts in 388 rectangles with line distance ≥ 10⁻³, with element growth median 1.9·10², max 3.8·10⁵ — i.e. the generator's "next step" works, and the kill rule it pre-registered does not fire. There is no new stability theorem to write: the generic backward-error statement for unpivoted LDLᵀ is false (growth unbounded) and the pivoted banded factorizations are published.
- **T3 (2-D bisection, monotone counts, termination).** In exact arithmetic the count of nodes in nested boxes is monotone by definition; termination is bookkeeping. The only non-trivial issue, monotonicity of the *floating-point* count, is Demmel–Dhillon–Ren, ETNA 3 (1995) 116–149 (tridiagonal and acyclic matrices), and the fix (take the max) is in ScaLAPACK. Ji 1992/1996 already ran 2-D bisection with Sturm counts.
- **The univariate route makes the two-index object unnecessary.** For a commuting pair the x-coordinates of the joint spectrum are the eigenvalues of H_x; for simple x-spectrum the joint eigenvectors are those of H_x and y_i = v_iᵀH_yv_i; for multiple x-values one diagonalises H_y on the (small) eigenspace. MEASURED (`t4_univariate_route.py`): N = 1035 (n = 44, random nodes), eigh(H_x) + Rayleigh quotients: max |Δx| = 5.6·10⁻¹⁶, max |Δy| = 3.2·10⁻¹⁵, 0.25 s, with minimal x-separation 3.9·10⁻⁷; box counts by a 1-D Sturm slice on H_x plus the slice's Rayleigh quotients: 20/20 correct at N = 231 and 1035. Cost of the whole joint spectrum: banded symmetric eigensolver O(N²b) = O(N^{2.5}) plus O(N·nnz) = O(N^{2.5}) for the pairing, below the draft's own O(N³ log²(1/ε)) for 2-D bisection and below dense joint diagonalisation. A single x-slice probe costs two LDLᵀ of bandwidth b versus one of bandwidth 4b for the product (a factor ≈ 8 in flops) and also delivers the nodes. This is exactly the Huhtanen collapse (brief §3, H_y = q(H_x)) made algorithmic, and it is the published method of Eidelman–Haimovici for normal matrices.
- **T4 (relative perturbation of joint eigenvalues).** The coordinates of the joint spectrum are the eigenvalues of H_x and of H_y separately; the only joint content is the pairing, which is discrete. An entrywise relative perturbation of H_x alone destroys commutation (MEASURED: commutator 6.7·10⁻⁷ at η = 10⁻⁶, `t5_relative_padua.py`), so "joint eigenvalues of the perturbed pair" are undefined and T4 can only be a statement about each matrix. Padua n = 8 (cubature weights, constructed and checked: exactness residual 7.5·10⁻¹⁶ on Π₁₅): H_x and H_y have ≤ 2 nonzeros per column, entries {½, 1/√2}, H_x has zero diagonal and an **acyclic graph** (40 edges, 5 components among 45 vertices) — the Demmel–Gragg class (Acta Numerica 2008 §2.3.1 lists acyclic sparsity among the known high-relative-accuracy classes), and MEASURED relative eigenvalue changes are 0.9η. For a generic pair (nonzero diagonal, cyclic graph) the statement is false, as in 1-D: a node at x = 10⁻⁶ gives relative changes 8·10⁴η; the 2×2 Jacobi matrix [[1,1],[1,1+10⁻⁶]] gives 10⁶η. The "exactly solvable Padua/HS instance" is therefore a corollary of a 1993 theorem applied to H_x, and the general case is the known univariate obstruction (factored/RRR representations are needed, Dhillon–Parlett). The "bivariate Golub–Welsch weights" are squared first components of eigenvectors of H_x alone.
- **Sub-problems 4 and 5** are software for, and an application of, a method dominated by the univariate route.

Remaining after subtraction: one remark (the half-integer numerator ½(n₊(S) − N + n_x + n_y) ∉ ℤ as a cheap commutation diagnostic for a computed pair — observed in `t1` at label δ = 10⁻¹ and in every failing case below) and a short note "inertia counts for commuting pairs". Fewer than three substantive sub-problems; criterion 2 fails.

## Attack (iii): ill-posed or blocked

Obstruction row applied: "Computed H_x, H_y never commute" (brief §3) with the draft's claimed escape "take δ as a hypothesis and prove the count stays certified for boundaries at distance ≥ c(N)δ^α with α better than the generic ½". The escape fails:

1. **The generator's measurement does not test the claim.** `nearly_commuting_probe.py` perturbs an exactly commuting pair additively by δ·E/‖E‖_F; replicated (`t1_replicate_n8.py`, n = 8, N = 45, seeds 1/7): at label δ = 10⁻² the spectral commutator is 3.3·10⁻³ and the spectral distance to the commuting pair is 3.2·10⁻³ — the pair is at distance δ from a commuting pair by construction, so the count is governed by first-order perturbation of the symmetric matrix S (Weyl), linear in the *distance*, and the commutator is irrelevant. Clean version (`t3b_diagnose.py` (A'), box lines in coordinate gaps, 8 random pattern-preserving directions): the worst-direction threshold is δ_max = 4.0·d for d = 10⁻² … 10⁻⁵ (exactly linear); directions with the lucky sign of the first-order shift never fail through that node, which is what inflates a one-direction scan (slope 0.46 in the generator-style scan of `t3_delta_exponent.py` (A) is meaningless for the same reason). Lin-type/Kachkovskiy–Safarov bounds concern the *commutator*, not the distance; comparing the two is the error — the same point the (b) developer made for L3 (IDEA 305: "Ĥ is already ε-close to the commuting pair H, so any Lin bound f(ε) ≥ ε is dominated by the trivial one").
2. **Within the hypothesis class the exponent is ½, sharp.** [response-01, 2026-11-01, A5: what is shown is that no exponent α > ½ works for the symmetrised-product formula on symmetric banded pairs (explicit counterexamples); correctness of the formula at distances ≫ √δ is not proved here, and the fitted slopes are not needed for the counterexample (PD-N?programme-ideas-claude-01).] Construction: commuting pair from nodes with two nodes ε apart in both coordinates, H_y's eigenbasis rotated by 45° inside the 2-D cluster eigenspace. Commutator δ = ε²/2, distance to the commuting pair ≈ ε/√2. With the box corner at distance d from the nearer node (both cluster nodes inside, all other nodes ≥ 0.086 from the lines) the symmetrised product is indefinite on the cluster block (2×2 analysis: AB + BA for A = diag(1,t), B = R₄₅°AR₄₅°ᵀ has negative determinant for t < 0.17), so the numerator is odd and the "count" is 3.5 instead of 4. MEASURED (`t3b_diagnose.py` (B'), ε = 10⁻¹ … 10⁻⁴): first failure at d/ε = 0.17–0.20, d_fail = 0.24–0.28·√δ, log-log slope of d_fail vs δ = 0.522 (0.550 in `t3_delta_exponent.py` (B)); e.g. δ = 5.0·10⁻⁹ fails at d = 2.0·10⁻⁵. The rotated perturbation is dense, so (C) repeats it **inside the symmetric block-tridiagonal pattern class**: a 2×2 rotated block on indices {0,1} (degree-0/degree-1 coupling block) direct-summed with a commuting block-tridiagonal rest (N = 38): δ = 5.0·10⁻⁷, wrong at line distance 2.0·10⁻⁴ (d/ε = 0.2) and below, correct at 5·10⁻⁴ — exactly the 0.2ε threshold. So no theorem of the form "banded + symmetric + ‖[H_x,H_y]‖ ≤ δ ⇒ count correct at distance c(N)δ^α" holds with α > ½, and the generator's test 3 kill criterion ("α ≈ ½ kills the structured δ-claim") fires.
3. **At scale √δ the count is not even well defined.** The failing configurations are clusters of width ε ∝ √δ whose x- and y-eigenbases differ by 45°: different commuting pairs within distance O(√δ) pair the x's with the y's differently, so "the number of joint eigenvalues in the box" has no meaning for boxes cutting such a cluster. For boxes that do not cut the cluster, the restricted-subspace object n₊(P_yM_xP_y) and the degree-2 disc object n₊(r² − (H_x−c)² − (H_y−c')²) stayed correct in every failing case of (B') (4/4 and 2/2), i.e. the symmetrised product is the worst of the three objects, failing at 0.2ε from a cluster it does not cut. The disc object (bandwidth 2b, degree 2) is the singular-value count of Z − c up to the commutator term i[H_x,H_y] and is Lipschitz in the pair — the natural "Sturm object" is the normal-matrix one the draft dismisses.
4. **The only route to a better exponent is the nearby commuting pair.** For the computed sections of the square case, Q̂ is within O(u) of an orthogonal Q̃ (Householder QR), and (Q̃ᵀXQ̃, Q̃ᵀYQ̃) commutes exactly, so the computed pair is at distance O(u) from a commuting pair and the count is correct at distance O(u) by Weyl — a backward-error statement about the Q-based construction, not about the commutator, and proved by exhibiting the nearby commuting pair. That is the gate's stated collapse condition ("falls back into exclusion (b)"); for Lanczos-type constructions where Q̂ loses orthogonality the statement is (b)'s L1/L3 question itself, and PD-N?…-302 (PROVED, square case) already says a structured nearby commuting pair cannot beat √ε.

The Huhtanen-collapse row also bites, as shown under (ii): the pair's joint spectrum is the spectrum of H_x plus a pairing, and the draft's separation ("Sturm counts, bisection and relative perturbation have no analogue for non-Hermitian normal matrices") is contradicted by Eidelman–Haimovici 2023 (Sturm bisection for normal/unitary matrices via the Hermitian part) and by the disc object above.
