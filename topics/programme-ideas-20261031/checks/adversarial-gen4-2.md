## Verdict

Sub-problem 4 must be re-scoped: the "Padua–Jacobi" family and the degree-dependent optimal Jacobi parameters are Briani–Sommariva–Vianello 2012 (fulltext read: Jacobi–Gauss–Lobatto grids with the Padua parity split, parameters optimised per degree for the Lebesgue constant, Table 5), and separability of the determinant is a theorem (De Marchi–Usevich 2014 Prop. 1), so what is new is a univariate two-species extremal problem with a weak payoff (the determinant-optimal sets have Λ = 6.60 at n = 10 against 5.92 for BSV's Lebesgue-optimised Padua–Jacobi sets). Sub-problem 3's stated mechanism (one maximum per Lissajous cell by convexity) is contradicted where it matters: the extra maxima are crease pairs straddling zero curves of fundamental polynomials (pairs 0.007–0.02 apart in angle at n = 8, MEASURED), i.e. exactly Białas-Cież–De Marchi–Suder's Remark 2, and the counts themselves are uncertified on both sides (their table has no method; my refined counts give 29 interior maxima at n = 7 against their 27). The survivor is a two-to-three-paper project in classical approximation theory (Paper A; a certified-counting tool paper; Paper C in coordination with Kraków–Padova), which fails criterion 5 (fit) as the gate already flagged and criterion 1's "escalating horizon" once sub-problem 2 is gone. Strongest unresolved threat if it proceeds: speed — the expansion is within reach of Kolomoitsev–Lomako's existing machinery or of the Padova group, who already published the heuristic fit ((2/π) log(n+1) + 1.1)² (De Marchi 2022).

## Attack (ii): really a note

**Sub-problem 1 is one clean computation.** The draft's corner value Λ_c(n) was recomputed (its own O(Nn) formula, chunked) for n = 24…2048 and for four residue classes mod 4 (n = 40…323). Findings (MEASURED, `adv_corner_parity.py`, `adv_corner_fit.py`, `adv_corner_const.py`):
- No arithmetic dependence: per-class two-parameter fits of Λ_c − (4/π²) log² n give b = 1.2151–1.2154, c = 1.909–1.911 (residual ≤ 0.0017) for n ≡ 0, 1, 2, 3 mod 4.
- With Euler–Maclaurin-type corrections the series is exact to the data: Λ_c(n) = (4/π²) log² n + b log n + c + d log n/n + e/n + f/n² (+ log²n/n², log n/n², 1/n³) with max residual 1.6e-8 over n = 96…1024 (5 parameters, 8 points), 5.9e-7 over n = 24…1024, and 4.5e-12 over n = 64…2048 with seven terms; free b = 1.22552205 against the draft's conjecture 2(2/π)c₁ = (8/π²)(γ + log(8/π)) = 1.22552213 (eight digits); c = 1.846161 ± 1e-7 (the draft's 1.929 and "b within 1–3 %" were biased by omitting the 1/n terms; its "Λ − λ_{n+1}^{Cheb}(1)² → ≈ 0.92 slowly" is the shift log(n+1) vs log n, i.e. a −2(4/π²) log n/n term; the actual constant is κ = c − c₁² = 0.919711 and the convergence is O(log n/n)). Alternative remainders d/√n or d/log n fit 10²–10⁴ times worse. No closed form for κ is identified (an inverse-symbolic attempt with eight digits is not credible and is disregarded).
- So the draft's headline conjecture is correct and the expansion is of pure Euler–Maclaurin type: the corner sum factorises asymptotically into the square of the 1D Chebyshev expansion plus an O(1) two-dimensional constant with O(log n/n) corrections. The analytic work is a lattice-sum expansion of ½[D_n(u)D_n(v) + D_n(u+π)D_n(v+π)] (rotated rhombus kernel) sampled on the Padua lattice, with the sign patterns depending on (j ± k') mod 4 — longer than Günttner's 1D computation but of the same kind, and with the answer (b exactly, κ to six digits) already known numerically. That is one paper, as the draft itself says (Paper A), and a short one if κ has a closed form. The genuinely open theorem inside Paper A is the corner argmax, which nobody has proved (De Marchi 2022 lists it as an observation; BDS 2026 say nothing in 2D; BCDMVX/BDMV 2006 do not locate the maximum).

The X-problem is the maximisation of an explicit univariate functional: a pure Vandermonde power of the odd-indexed nodes (dCDM (24)) times generalised-Schur-type determinants of the even-indexed nodes (dCDM (25)) — not a Stieltjes log-gas, so "explicit Gauss–Lobatto–Jacobi family" is false as stated (the draft's own α drifts −0.26 → −0.38). Payoff: determinant-optimal checkerboards have Λ = 6.60 at n = 10 (draft) against 5.92 for BSV's Padua–Jacobi and 5.32 for their LEB sets; the family name and the degree-dependent parameters are BSV 2012's. ## Attack (iii): ill-posed or blocked

**Sub-problem 2 — blocked at the constant (decisive).** The Chebyshev projection on the square is the rhombus (ℓ¹) Fourier partial sum on even functions, and its norm equals the rhombus Lebesgue constant (kernel at the corner θ′ = φ′ = 0). Kolomoitsev–Lomako Thm 2.2 (and their ref. [16] for n/m ∈ ℕ) give L_{nn} = (16/π⁴) log² n (1 + o(1)), not (4/π²) log² n. Hence the minimal projection constant onto Π_n in C([−1,1]²) is ≤ (16/π⁴) log² n (1 + o(1)) = 0.164 log² n, and no projection-constant argument (Lozinski–Kharshiladze or otherwise, even if a multivariate algebraic version existed) can give Λ ≥ (4/π²) log² n − O(log n). The one-dimensional analogue says the same: minimal projection constant (4/π²) log n (Chebyshev projection) against Erdős's interpolation bound (2/π) log n. The draft's entry point 4 (gen4_tests16.py) has two errors: (a) the code computes Σ_j e^{ijθ} D_{|j|}(φ) instead of Σ_j e^{ijθ} D_{n−|j|}(φ) (`Dk[np.abs(J)]`), i.e. a different kernel (cross-check at n = 10: 5.74 vs the correct 4.69, `adv_rhombus_crosscheck.py`); (b) the expected limit 4/π² is wrong. Corrected (`adv_rhombus_L1.py`, product identity verified against a brute-force lattice sum): ‖D_n^{rh}‖₁/log² n = 0.885, 0.667, 0.548, 0.473, 0.423, 0.386, 0.359, 0.337, 0.320 for n = 10…2560, still decreasing; fixed a = 16/π⁴ gives b = 1.063, c = 1.263 with residual 0.003 over n ≥ 160; free a = 0.1675. The draft's own pre-registered kill ("if the ratio does not approach 4/π² … the lower bound must come from a different mechanism") has fired. The alternative route ("restrict to the diagonal or a corner wedge where the Lagrange basis reduces to univariate structure") has no stated mechanism — the restriction of a bivariate Lagrange basis to a line is not a univariate Lagrange basis — and no test; and even the order of a universal lower bound is open (BDMV 2006: "the minimal rate of growth is not known"; nothing newer found). Sub-problem 2 as stated is a wish, not a sub-problem.

**Sub-problem 3 — the stated mechanism is wrong where it matters.** The draft's cell argument: on each Lissajous cell the sign pattern of the ℓ_B is fixed, so λ_n is a polynomial with a unique interior critical point "by a convexity argument in the Chebyshev angles". But λ_n = Σ w_B|ℓ_B| has valley creases (not ridges) along every zero curve of an ℓ_B inside a cell, and where the smooth part peaks near a crease the maximum splits into two maxima, one on each side. MEASURED at n = 8 (`adv_maxima_count2.py`): four pairs of interior maxima at mutual distance 6.6e-3 and 2.2e-2 (angle units), each member at crease distance |ℓ_B|/‖∇ℓ_B‖ = 2.5e-3–4.1e-3 from the same B; the pairs are the maxima that single-mesh counts miss (30–36 found per start, 38 only as the union of five starts). The published n = 8 count 38/56 is therefore plausible but was not certified by anyone (BDS 2026 give no method), and my refined counts disagree with BDS at n = 7 (29 interior from a 3201² start, 26–28 from coarser starts, against 27 published; 15 edge maxima + 2 corners against their implied 15 boundary, with a crease pair 0.0125 apart on the edges y = ±1). The lower bounds n(n−1)/2 and (n+1)(n+2)/2 hold in every run. Consequences: (a) a cell theorem cannot be "unique critical point per cell" — it must count crease pairs, i.e. exactly BDS's §4 question; (b) certified counting must come first, and a mesh of any fixed size does not certify (the gate's flag stands, and it also applies to the published table). Not a kill of sub-problem 3, but the claimed new mechanism is removed.

**Sub-problem 1 — not blocked; the opposite.** The corner is the argmax on every mesh (draft) and the expansion is clean (above). Factual correction: for even n the non-node corners are (1,−1) and (−1,−1) (λ = 1 at (1,1) and (−1,1)); for odd n they are (−1,1) and (−1,−1); the draft's "(1,−1) and (−1,1)" mixes the two parities (`adv_corner_parity.out`).

**Sub-problem 4 — well posed but reduced.** Separability is a theorem; the GLJ(α) identification is approximate (α(n) drifts, the energy is not a pairwise log-gas). The brief §3 obstruction table has no row that applies; the draft is right about that.
