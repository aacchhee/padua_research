## Verdict

After the known content is removed (PD-T?…-301, the block anti-Gauss identity, and Degani–Schiff–Tannor 2005 §§2–6, which already contain the symmetric-positive extension formulation, the gauge and dimension counts, the compatibility condition, Möller's bound and the completion computations), fewer than three substantive sub-problems remain: one hard theorem that is Xu's own open problem 1 and two possible notes. Evidence in brief. Sub-problem (1) is a one-paragraph projection identity (the words formula is (b)'s PD-T?…-301; the √2 block anti-Gauss is the block anti-Gauss rule of Fenu–Martin–Reichel–Rodriguez 2013 applied to a block-tridiagonal pair, and the sign flip at lengths 2k+2 and 2k+3 is the same half-page proof as Laurie's), and beyond Π_{2k+1} the "rule" of a non-commuting pair is not a functional on polynomials at all (word-order dependent, sign-indefinite, lift-dependent — MEASURED below). Sub-problems (2) and (3) are, object for object, Degani–Schiff–Tannor (Numer. Math. 101, 2005): real symmetric extensions only (their §2), positive weights = squared first components (Thm 10), the compatibility condition (Thm 10), the parameter/dimension count modulo the O(N−n) gauge (eqs. 11–15, §4 block-tridiagonal case, §5.3 eqs. 75–77), Möller's bound as Thm 2/Thm 12, non-existence of symmetric Möller-size extensions for generic rank-2 pairs (Thm 4), and the completion computed by gradient flow / Newton / Jacobi rotations (§3, §6) — of which this campaign had read only the abstract (gen4-draft-3) and, via bttb-research's literature check, Def. 1, Thm 2 and Thm 4 (`bttb_repo_exact_mapping.md`); the gate flagged exactly this risk. What is left of (2) is Xu's own open problem 1 (sharper lower bounds), a single hard approximation-theory theorem whose classical precedent (Cools–Schmid 1993, Verlinden–Cools 1992, Hirao–Sawa 2009) the drafts do not cite; what is left of (3) is one more near-minimal-rule solver in a field that already has Schmid's matrix equations (SIMAX 1995), Collowald–Hubert 2018 and Abril Bucero–Bajaj–Mourrain 2016. Sub-problem (4) rests on recurrences (b) has measured as unstable on exactly the supports a normal matrix's spectrum has (PD-N?…-102, PD-T?…-107/M3), and for every f of the form φ∘g the pair is dominated or matched at equal cost by univariate Lanczos on g(X,Y). Survivors are notes, not papers; the one open theorem is owned as a question by Xu and does not fit AC's edge.

## Attack (ii): really a note

What the four sub-problems reduce to once the known content is removed:

**(1) Exactness and bordering — a one-paragraph identity.** Let P_k be the orthogonal projector onto Π_k and A = H_x^{(k)} = P_kXP_k, B = H_y^{(k)}. For any word w = s_1⋯s_L with L ≤ 2k+1, split w = uv with |u| ≤ k, |v| ≤ k+1; then u(A,B)ᵀe₁ is the exact vector of the monomial m_u ∈ Π_k and v(A,B)e₁ = P_k m_v, so e₁ᵀw e₁ = ⟨m_u, P_k m_v⟩ = ∫m_u m_v dμ. That is the words formula, already PROVED in (b) as PD-T?…-301 (and DST Thm 11 in the commuting case; Sauer–Xu Thm 4.1 gives 2n−1 for their indexing). At length 2k+2, with w = uv, |u| = |v| = k+1: e₁ᵀw e₁ = ⟨m_u,m_v⟩ − ⟨(I−P_k)m_u,(I−P_k)m_v⟩. Scaling the degree k→k+1 coupling blocks of the (k+1)-section by √2 multiplies the degree-(k+1) components of both half-words by √2, so the value becomes ⟨m_u,m_v⟩ + ⟨(I−P_k)m_u,(I−P_k)m_v⟩: the sign flip is immediate, word by word; the same bookkeeping gives the flip at length 2k+3 (sketch not written out here; MEASURED ratio exactly −1, script 1, T4). This is Laurie's 1996 proof with scalars replaced by blocks, i.e. the block anti-Gauss construction of Fenu–Martin–Reichel–Rodriguez 2013 applied to the block-tridiagonal pair. "Radau/Lobatto bordering as a pair IEP" has no meaning for a non-commuting pair (there is no node to prescribe); for a point rule it is cubature with preassigned nodes (Luo–Meng–Zhang, Appl. Anal. 87 (2008); Cools–Haegemans embedded formulae) and collapses into (3). On product measures the pair rule is not the tensor Gauss rule (the projector is the total-degree one), but it has no content of its own either: on Π_{2k+1} it is the product moment sequence, and at degree 2k+2 it is the word-dependent quantity above. Remainder: a remark.

**(2) Symmetric Möller-size extensions.** "Symmetric + positive first-row weights" is not an added hypothesis: for any real symmetric commuting extension the weights are squared first components (DST Thm 10/11), ~~so positivity is automatic~~ [response-01, 2026-11-01, audit-01 A9.7: so the weights are nonnegative; strict positivity needs the first unit vector to have nonzero overlap with every joint eigenvector (cyclicity), or removal of zero-weight modes — appending a decoupled joint eigenvector gives a zero weight while keeping symmetry, commutation and compatibility], and DST already restricted to symmetric extensions (their §2, Thm 5). The "compatibility conditions as explicit algebraic equations in the (B, D) blocks" are DST eq. (65) written out, i.e. Xu's "non-linear system of equations in the structural matrices" (Pitman RN 312, CUP 2025 Ch. 6) and Schmid's matrix equations (SIMAX 1995). The "dimension count modulo the O(m) gauge" is DST Thm 3 + eqs. (11)–(15) + §4 (block-tridiagonal) + §5.3 eqs. (75)–(77), with the explicit warning that counting "can be misleading" and the rigorous Thm 2 instead. The MEASURED "positive-dimensional families for n ≤ 4, isolated for Legendre n = 5, 6, nodes outside the square for Chebyshev" (gen4-draft-3) reproduces: DST §6.1 (one-parameter families of 7-point degree-5 rules on square and disk — my script gives local dimension 1 for both, Table below), Möller 1976 / Cools–Haegemans 1988 (isolated 17- and 24-point rules on the square: local dimension 0 measured), and the standard fact that Möller's bound concerns real nodes anywhere (nodes outside the domain appear already in DST §6.1 for a non-standard domain). The rank bound equals Möller's (gen4-draft-3 entry point 1) because DST Thm 12 *is* Möller's bound. What remains is Xu's open problem 1 — sharper, weight-specific lower bounds — which is one theorem, not three sub-problems, has a precedent (Cools–Schmid 1993; Verlinden–Cools 1992; Hirao–Sawa 2009 for degree 9), and is posed and worked by Xu himself.

**(3) Completion IEP with Padua benchmarks.** The "sections given → extension" inverse problem is exactly what DST §3 solved by gradient flow, Newton and Jacobi rotations (with slow convergence and local minima reported), what Schmid and Cools–Schmid solved as matrix equations in 1989–1995, and what Collowald–Hubert 2018 and ABM 2016 solve as flat moment-matrix extensions — the pipeline R3 F9 already lists as standard. Padua as a sparse extension is RES 2026-10-31 item 2a ("an instance of Xu 1994: modified truncated block Jacobi matrices whose joint eigenvalues are cubature nodes"), judged known; the Padua Christoffel-number identity is, by the draft's own admission, a corollary of the 2006 Lagrange formula. "Padua minimal among Lissajous-spectrum rules" would be a corollary of the Dencker–Erb 2017 classification of Lissajous–Chebyshev node sets (UNVERIFIED; not checked here). Kronrod-type extensions in 2D are the embedded-cubature programme of Cools–Haegemans (1987–1994), with existence known to be the obstacle. A Givens/bulge-chasing solver for the DST equations would be a method paper competing with node elimination and SDP relaxations, with no complexity or stability claim in hand.

**(4) Normal-matrix functionals.** See (iii): the premise is contradicted by (b)'s measurements and the only well-defined non-polynomial instances are univariate.

Count after removal: zero substantive separable sub-problems with a template theorem to prove; one hard open theorem (Xu's) and two possible notes (bracketing of a chosen lift; a rotation-based solver). Criterion 2 fails.

## Attack (iii): ill-posed or blocked

**(a) The functional half is undefined beyond Π_{2k+1} (brief §3, "Huhtanen 2002 collapse" row, hypothesis "a functional calculus f(H_A,H_B) is needed").** The escape accepted by the gate — scalar functionals on ordered words — covers only the trivial regime. MEASURED (script 1, M = 30 random nodes, k = 1..4): at length 2k+2 the value e₁ᵀw e₁ depends on the order of the letters (k = 1: the three orderings of x²y² give 0.0815, 0.0039, 0.0039 against the moment 0.0631), so the "error of the rule on x^a y^b" is not defined; the error for the split (m_u, m_v) is exactly +⟨(I−P_k)m_u,(I−P_k)m_v⟩, sign-definite only for palindromic words and of either sign across measures for m_u = x^{k+1}, m_v = y^{k+1} (both signs observed). Hence (α) Gauss and anti-Gauss bracket polynomials of degree 2k+2, 2k+3 trivially (opposite errors) and no sign-definite error representation exists in general, ~~so Laurie-type bracketing *theorems* for non-polynomial f have no 2D analogue on the pair;~~ [response-01, 2026-11-01, audit-01 A5: so there is no automatic, lift-independent bracketing theory for the pair; a bracketing theorem for a specified lift or function class is a different question, not excluded here;] (β) for non-polynomial f the rule requires a non-commutative lift: f = 1/(c−xy) admits at least three natural lifts (AB, BA, symmetrised) whose values differ by 1e-6…5e-4 relative (T6), comparable to the error itself. The only order-free case is f = φ∘g with g a polynomial in one combination, e.g. 1/((x−a)²+(y−b)²) through G = (A−a)²+(B−b)²; there the pair-Gauss and its √2-anti-Gauss bracket the exact value in 196–200 of 200 instances (so the generator's own kill for entry point 3 does not fire), but univariate Lanczos on g(X,Y) with the same number of X/Y-products goes from 10× worse at k = 1 to parity at k = 4 (median relative errors 1.5e-2/1.7e-1, 1.2e-3/5.7e-3, 9.2e-5/2.0e-4, 3.7e-6/3.8e-6 for pair/Lanczos), being exact to g-degree 2m−1 ≈ dim Π_k against k for the pair (T5, T5b), ~~so it wins for every larger k~~ [response-01, 2026-11-01, A5: measured at k ≤ 4 only; the exactness-degree comparison does not order the errors for every function, measure or larger k] — the Huhtanen collapse in functional form: whatever f can be lifted without ambiguity is a function of one Hermitian operator.

**(b) Sub-problem (4)'s mechanism is contradicted by (b)'s measurements.** The claimed advantage over Arnoldi is O(√N)-window storage at high degree. (b) L1 R01 measured: the complex HL SIMAX windowed recurrence loses orthogonality like 3ⁿ from degree ≈ 6 on every set (PD-N?…-102); the real pair recurrence loses exponentially from degree 8–12 on every non-product support (rotations, L-shapes, annuli, disks, triangles; PD-T?…-107, M3), with the J-defect growing — and the spectrum of a generic normal matrix is a non-product support. Below degree ≈ 8 the window is ≤ 45 vectors and Arnoldi's storage is not a problem; above it the window recurrence does not deliver sections of anything. Whether the computed functional still approximates u*f(N)u after loss of orthogonality is (b)'s open reading (G), not this candidate's. Katz/commute functionals of undirected graphs are functions of a symmetric matrix (univariate); inclusion regions are HL's own §5–6.

**(c) "Not flat at colength N" (brief §3) does not bite, as the draft says, but the escape leads straight into DST.** Positive measures have all extensions; the minimal symmetric one is DST's object, and DST Thm 4 already shows the symmetric requirement can exclude the Möller size for generic rank-2 pairs. For measures the exclusion is weight-specific: MEASURED (script 2, Table) Möller-size graded symmetric extensions exist for Chebyshev and Legendre (n = 2..6), disk (n ≤ 4), triangle (n ≤ 4), and were not found from 8 random starts for the disk at n = 5, 6 (degrees 9, 11; best residual 3.6e-2, 3.0e-2), the triangle at n = 5, 6 (1.4e-2, 9.7e-3) and the non-symmetric Jacobi weight at n = 5, 6 (1.8e-2, 1.5e-2) — in agreement with Hirao–Sawa 2009 / Verlinden–Cools 1992 (no Möller-attaining degree-9 rule for the disk) and with the best known triangle rule of degree 9 (19 nodes > 17). Non-convergence is not a certificate; the point is that the "δ(W) > 0" phenomenon is classical and the drafts' Chebyshev/Legendre-only tests could not see it.
