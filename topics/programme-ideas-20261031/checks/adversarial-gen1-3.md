## Verdict
**WOUNDED.** Not owned by anyone on exactly this question, and the central object exists (the window recurrence is a generic fact, now with a proof sketch, and it survives complex lines). But: (1) sub-problem 1's "classification theorem" is a half-page lemma with a parity refinement the draft missed (two-degree window at even degree, three at odd), so it is a section, not a paper; (2) the draft's pole model (real lines, one per degree, in generic directions) is the wrong one for the two canonical singularity types — interior curves need complex shifts and an edge-adjacent point cannot be reached by real lines at all (99.35 % of lines through the draft's own test point (1.01, 0) cross the square) — while the best-performing instance of its class is conjugate pairs of complex lines, i.e. point poles |z−ζ|², which also contain the analytic-lightning baseline as a subspace; (3) the application half (bivariate rational least squares with clustered poles) is an explicitly stated open problem of Boullé–Herremans–Huybrechs (KU Leuven NUMA, 2023–24: "a more efficient solver for the large least squares problems"), one corridor away from the NALAG pencil-IEP line (Van Buggenhout–Van Barel–Vandebril 2022, Faghih et al. 2025, Vermeiren 2026). What survives is one algorithmic paper (pencil-pair IEP + three-block windowed rational Lanczos + its use as a conditioned solver for BHH-type spaces) plus a thinly motivated commuting-pair Krylov track; as a 2–5-year AC-owned programme it does not stand, as a collaboration proposal to Leuven it does. Gate conditions: Humet Ch. 5 does **not** contain the per-degree-pole IEP (resolved below); product poles already dropped.

## Attack (ii): really a note

1. **The window theorem is a lemma (proof sketch).** Setting as in the draft, real lines ℓ_k, π_t = ∏_{k≤t}ℓ_k, V_t = Π_t/π_t, W_t = V_t ⊖ V_{t−1}, U_{t+1} = Π_{t+1}/π_t ⊃ V_t, H_d = forms of degree d, ℓ̂_k = linear part of ℓ_k.
   (F1) h ∈ U_{t+1} lies in V_t iff the degree-(t+1) form of hπ_t vanishes; so L: U_{t+1} → H_{t+1}, h ↦ top form of hπ_t, has kernel V_t and is generically onto. (F2) Hence for φ ∈ W_{t+1}, ⟨φ, h⟩ = Λ_φ(Lh) for a functional Λ_φ ∈ H_{t+1}*; likewise for v ∈ W_t on U_t. (F3) Generically the top-form map T: W_t → H_t is bijective, so V_t + xW_t + yW_t = U_{t+1} and the gauge space K_t = {(v,w) ∈ W_t²: xv + yw ∈ V_t} ≅ H_{t−1} (T v = y c, T w = −x c), dim t.
   *Lemma.* For φ ∈ W_{t+1} and any (v,w) with x Tv + y Tw = L(ℓ_{t+1}φ), the V_t-element g = ℓ_{t+1}φ − xv − yw has coefficients ⟨g, φ_{j,r}⟩ = μ(ℓ̂_{j+1}⋯ℓ̂_{t−1}P_{j,r}) for all j ≤ t−1, where P_{j,r} ∈ H_j is the top numerator form of φ_{j,r} and μ ∈ H_{t−1}*, μ(m) = Λ_φ(ℓ̂_{t+1}ℓ̂_t m) − Λ_v(xm) − Λ_w(ym). Proof: ⟨ℓ_{t+1}φ, φ_{j,r}⟩ = ⟨φ, ℓ_{t+1}φ_{j,r}⟩ and ℓ_{t+1}φ_{j,r} ∈ U_{t+1} has top form ℓ̂_{t+1}ℓ̂_{j+1}⋯ℓ̂_t P_{j,r}; ⟨xv, φ_{j,r}⟩ = ⟨v, xφ_{j,r}⟩ and xφ_{j,r} ∈ U_t has top form x ℓ̂_{j+1}⋯ℓ̂_{t−1}P_{j,r}; apply (F2). ∎
   So the t(t+1)/2 coefficients of g on V_{t−1} are the t numbers μ|_{H_{t−1}}, and those on V_{t−2} are the t−1 numbers μ|_{ℓ̂_{t−1}H_{t−2}} (a rank collapse of reproducing-kernel type). A gauge change c ∈ H_{t−1} changes μ by Φ(c,·), Φ(c,m) = B(yc,xm) − B(xc,ym) with B(a,b) = ⟨T⁻¹a, ℓ_t T⁻¹b⟩ symmetric; Φ is therefore **skew-symmetric** on H_{t−1}, of even rank. For t even Φ is generically nonsingular and μ can be killed entirely: ℓ_{t+1}W_{t+1} ⊂ W_t + xW_t + yW_t (two-degree window). For t odd, rank Φ = t−1 and μ can be killed on ℓ̂_{t−1}H_{t−2} but not on H_{t−1}: three-degree window, two impossible. In one variable K_t = 0 and the draft-form window fails, which is why the classical ORF recurrence carries pole-cancelled terms ℓ_{n−2}φ_{n−2}. Status: conditional reasoning on the generic rank statements; every rank statement is MEASURED below (n ≤ 8, real, complex and conjugate-pair lines). This closes the proof gap the gate flagged and shows the draft's statement is not sharp. A lemma with a parity remark is not a Constr. Approx./JAT paper; the classification part of sub-problem 1 is otherwise dimension counting (one finite line per degree is forced by nestedness of total-degree numerators; product poles reduce; degree-≥2 curves break the count — all in the draft already).
2. **The draft's own first paper already contains sub-problems 1–3.** What is left for years 2–5 is sub-problem 4 (no named application: for Kronecker pairs the joint spectrum is a grid and the natural functions of A⊗I + I⊗B are univariate in the sum, Kressner's tensor Krylov covers f{A,B}(C), and for analytic f Huhtanen's reduction to one normal matrix is better — measured below), sub-problem 5 (a note by the draft's own words), stability (owned by (b)), vector ORFs (Vermeiren's framework), cubature (M1's). After removing what is known, the substantive remainder is: the pencil-pair IEP with its windowed Lanczos (algorithms + software), the conditioned solver for BHH-type spaces (numerics, Leuven's open problem), and a commuting-pair Krylov theory without a use case. Three items, two of them one paper in the draft's own plan.
3. **Not separable:** the IEP (2) is the engine and the LS application (3) is its first experiment; the draft merges them itself.

Conclusion: survives (ii) only narrowly; it is one algorithmic paper plus a direction list.

## Attack (iii): ill-posed or blocked

- **Existence of the object: not blocked.** MEASURED (window_test.py, M = 120 random nodes, n ≤ 8, three seeds; window_complex.py, two seeds each for real, conjugate-pair and independent complex lines): residual of ℓ_{t+1}φ_{t+1,s} against W_{t−1}+W_t+xW_t+yW_t is 1e-15…7e-13 for all t = 1..7 and all s, with span rank 4t+2 inside an ambient (t+2)(t+3)/2 (non-vacuous from t = 3; the generator's n ≤ 5 run had exactly one non-vacuous point, t = 4). Against W_t+xW_t+yW_t: residual ~1e-15 for t = 2, 4, 6 and 0.33–0.73 for t = 1, 3, 5, 7 (the parity law). Gauge kernel dim = t as predicted. Same numbers for complex lines and conjugate pairs. 1-D control: the draft-form span fails (0.19–0.33 for n = 3..8) and the classical pole-cancelled span holds (≤ 5e-15).
- **The recurrence is not Lanczos-extractable from two blocks.** MEASURED: C_t = (W_{t−1}+W_t+xW_t+yW_t)/ℓ_{t+1} ⊂ V_{t+1} has dim 4t+2 and C_t ∩ V_t has dim 3t; orthogonalising C_t against W_t ⊕ W_{t−1} leaves 2t+1 directions, i.e. t−1 spurious ones in V_{t−2}; orthogonalising also against W_{t−2} leaves exactly t+2 = dim W_{t+1} with min singular value 1.00 against W_{t+1} (t = 1..7). So a windowed rational Lanczos exists with a two-block generation window and a **three-block orthogonalisation window**; the draft's O(MN^{3/2}) cost claim survives, its "same window as the polynomial pair" wording does not.
- **The pole model is wrong for the canonical applications.** (a) A line through a point adjacent to a convex domain crosses the domain unless the point sees the domain in less than 180°; from (1.01, 0) the square subtends 181.1°, and 99.35 % of uniformly random lines through it cross the square (MEASURED, 20 000 angles). The draft's entry point 3 would therefore build bases with poles inside the domain; with that model the rational class is 7–70× *worse* than polynomials on its own test function (approx_baseline2.py, column L1cone for f6: 0.25–0.40 against 3.7e-3–6.1e-2, then rank loss). (b) Interior singular curves (|x−y|) need complex shifts (BHH put them on the imaginary axis); real lines near the diagonal give 0.51–0.59 at every dimension vs 2.8e-2 for polynomials. (c) The literal class Π_n/π_n contains no non-constant polynomial (qπ_n ∉ Π_n for deg q ≥ 1), so "lightning + polynomial" needs the mixed class Π_n/π_m, m < n (lines at infinity), which the draft does not mention.
- **Huhtanen collapse / rational Krylov on N = X+iY (gate's third target): refuted as a universal winner, but it wins where it matters most.** MEASURED (approx_baseline2.py; 32×32 Chebyshev grid, best relative LS error at real dimension N = 55, corner (1,1), poles tapered to the grid resolution σ = 2.3): harmonic targets Re√w and log r: Re/Im-span of analytic lightning (Zr) 5.1e-6 and 3.5e-5 vs best pair class 2.2e-4 and 2.7e-3 vs polynomials 2.4e-3 and 9.0e-2 — the single-normal-matrix baseline wins by a further 40–80×; non-harmonic targets r, r^{1/2}, r^{1/2}(1+½ sin 3θ), |x−y|, the draft's f6: Zr stagnates at 3.4e-2, 1.1e-2, 7.8e-2, 2.1e-1, 5.2e-2 while the best pair class reaches 1.2e-4, 3.1e-4, 8.6e-4, 1.4e-2, 6.7e-4 (polynomials 6.8e-4, 3.0e-3, 9.1e-3, 2.8e-2, 3.7e-3). The purely complex analytic space (Zc, complex dim N) stagnates at 0.14–0.43 on every target. So sub-problem 4 is alive only for non-analytic f of a commuting pair, and such an application is not named.
- **Gains over polynomials are modest in scope and the draft's own kill criterion is borderline.** At N = 55 the best pair class beats polynomials by 6× (r), 10× (r^{1/2}), 11× (f7), 2× (|x−y|), 5.5× (f6), 33× (log r, where Zr is 100× better still). The ≥10× criterion fires on two of the five non-harmonic tests (r^{1/2}, f7) and on both harmonic ones, where the analytic baseline is far better still. Scope caveat: N ≤ 55 and grid resolution ~1e-3 cannot show root-exponential regimes.
- **Which instance of the class actually works:** conjugate pairs of complex lines (= real denominators ∏|z−ζ_k|², point poles) are best, or within a factor 1.3 of best, on r, r^{1/2}, Re√w, f7, |x−y|, f6; real lines in random cone directions (the draft's separating feature) beat parallel/product lines by 3–10× on corner singularities, beat point poles by 7× on log r and by 1.3× on f7, and lose to them elsewhere. The point-pole class contains Re(p/∏(z−ζ_k)) at double numerator degree, i.e. the analytic-lightning baseline is a subspace of the pair's class in its conjugate-pair form — the right frame, absent from the draft.
- Obstruction table: Popov curse — irrelevant (numerical). Q not HSS — unchanged. Computed pairs never commute — applies verbatim to the pencil pairs (the stability question is (b)'s). Huhtanen collapse — see above: binding for analytic f. Measure modification R04/R07 — the mixed class with slowly growing π_m is the "q nearly vanishes" regime of F2/T4 (poles clustered at the boundary), where R3 recorded no sparsity; the draft escapes only because π grows.

Conclusion: not ill-posed, but the draft's pole model, window statement and entry-point-3 design are each wrong in a way that re-centres the programme on BHH/Leuven territory (complex-shifted lines, point poles, mixed classes).

## Attack (iv): renaming of an exclusion
- **F10 (bivariate Laurent, judged trivial):** nearest item. Laurent = lines x = 0, y = 0 repeated — a degenerate (concurrent, two fixed directions) configuration of the draft's class, where the generic ranks above fail and five-term recurrences appear (Cruz-Barroso–Fernández). Generic lines are a different object with a genuinely new mechanism (gauge kernel, skew form); separation stands.
- **Measure modification (narrow, R3 F2/T4):** fixed denominator = fixed π; the draft's π grows with the degree, so the pencil is not a Christoffel/Uvarov step. Not a renaming, but note the mixed class Π_n/π_m at fixed m *is* measure modification by 1/|π_m|² in the near-singular regime; the programme must say which growth rate it wants.
- **(b) finite-precision Lanczos:** the windowed rational Lanczos' rounding theory is (b)'s by construction; the draft defers it. Not a renaming of (b) because the exact structure and the IEP come first.
- **(a) 2-D Schur/Levinson:** the two-level Cauchy/Loewner remark is a structured-Gram observation; its univariate version is already in Van Buggenhout's thesis (displacement rank of rational Gram matrices). If pursued as a fast solver it is (a)'s/bttb's; drop it.
- **"f(A,B) total-degree Krylov" (judged):** sub-problem 4 is its rational version; the judged reason ("projections do not commute") applies unchanged to pencil projections. It survives the judgement only through the scalar bilinear-form route (M1's mechanism) or an application with non-analytic f; neither is in the draft.
