## Extracted published statements

### LIT?programme-ideas-claude-77 — fact: ε-approximate ideals (Definition 2) and their non-additivity

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Exact ideals are incompatible with perturbed data or floating-point evaluation, since an arbitrarily small coefficient perturbation destroys f(ξ) = 0. The paper therefore calls a polynomial "small on Ξ" when the p-norm of its value vector is at most ε, and, because that is not scale-invariant, normalises by the coefficient norm ‖f‖ induced by the Macaulay inner product. Definition 2: the p-approximate ideal of accuracy ε is the set of f with ‖f(Ξ)‖_p/‖f‖ ≤ ε. It is closed under nonzero scaling; for two members that are orthogonal in the coefficient inner product, the sum lies in the √2ε-approximate ideal (Lemma 1), but in general the sum need not be small (Example 2, a two-polynomial example on one point whose ratio tends to 1), which the author says corrects a false claim in an earlier version, acknowledging C. Fassino and the referee. Theorem 3 (p = 2): any finite-dimensional polynomial space splits orthogonally into a part with ‖f(Ξ)‖_2 > ε‖f‖ and a maximal subspace of the approximate ideal, found by thresholding the singular values of the Vandermonde matrix at ε.
- Hypotheses and notation: Ξ ⊂ K^d finite; 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞; coefficient norm from the Macaulay inner product (eq. (9), not read); Theorem 3 restricted to K = R.
- Page / theorem / equation: eq. (17) and Definition 2 (eq. (18)), Lemma 1, p. 305; Example 2 and Theorem 3, p. 306; proof of Theorem 3, pp. 306–307.
- Reading evidence and limitations: Read from page images; the proof of Theorem 3 (SVD thresholding) was read, the Macaulay inner product (9) was not.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Background for any programme candidate on certified approximate vanishing ideals (round-3 F4 / round-2 D). The non-additivity is the reason the "ideal generated by an approximate H-basis" question (next statement) is non-trivial.

### LIT?programme-ideas-claude-78 — open problem: is the ideal generated by an approximate H-basis an approximate ideal?

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: After remarking that the SVD-based approximate ideal is not "structured" (not convex, for example), the paper takes the approach of selecting basis elements inside the approximate ideal and generating an ideal from them. Definition 3: an approximate H-basis of accuracy ε for I(Ξ) is a finite set H that is an H-basis and lies in the p-approximate ideal of accuracy ε. The author then states that, unlike exact H-bases, approximate H-bases are accessible numerically, but that it is unclear whether, or under which conditions, the ideal generated by an approximate H-basis is again an approximate ideal, and that this question requires further investigation.
- Hypotheses and notation: as above; "H-basis" in the Macaulay sense (not read here).
- Page / theorem / equation: Definition 3 and the following paragraph, p. 307 (PDF p. 13).
- Reading evidence and limitations: Read from the page image. The reported locator "p. 307" in the inputs is confirmed.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): The only author-stated open question in the pages read. Round 3 (F4) judges it "nearly tautological" and names a whole-ideal backward certificate (Fassino 2010) as the real open core; that judgement is the report's, not the source's.

### LIT?programme-ideas-claude-79 — fact: a-priori bound with an exponential constant (Theorem 5)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Along the degree-by-degree algorithm, the coefficient vectors of f and a nearby f' differ by at most ε·‖R_k^{-1}‖_2 ≤ ε·2^{#F_k^+}·max_j |(R_k)^{-1}_{jj}| (eq. (28)); the author calls this very pessimistic but notes it isolates two quantities: the diagonal of R_k must be large relative to ε, and the sets F_k^+ must not be too large. The 2^{n−1} factor is sharp for unit-triangular inverses in general (eq. (29) and the standard example) though usually much smaller. Theorem 5: if the algorithm terminates at step n and f ∈ F_0, there is a polynomial f' vanishing on the union of the accepted point sets with ‖f − f'‖ ≤ ε Σ_{k=deg f}^{n} 2^{#F_k^+} max_j |(R_k)^{-1}_{jj}| (eq. (30)).
- Hypotheses and notation: pivoted QR factors R_k per degree k; F_k^+ the accepted ("plus") set at degree k; termination at step n.
- Page / theorem / equation: eqs (28)–(30), Theorem 5, p. 312 (PDF p. 18).
- Reading evidence and limitations: Statement read from the page image; the proof and the definitions of F_k^+, Ξ_k^+ (pp. 308–311) were not read.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Baseline bound for any certified-approximate-ideal programme; constants exponential in the number of accepted polynomials per degree, which is the gap round 3 F4 names (polynomial constants).
