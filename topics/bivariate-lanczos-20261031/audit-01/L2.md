### L2-F1 — Medium: the residual identity does not prove a universal obstruction

Locations: `tracks/bivariate-lanczos/theory/commutator-shadow-recursion-and-span-drift-bound.md:71`; `topics/bivariate-lanczos-20261031/report.md:10` and `:41`. T202's Scope says K “is not acted on by reorthogonalization” and “Hence no reorthogonalization rule can by itself make the implicit J-section accurate or the computed spaces J-closed.” The report calls this a “proved obstruction.”

The proof establishes a residual recursion with coefficients that **do change** under reorthogonalization: its own line 65 contains the terms `−Σ g_l K_{m,l}` and the changed formed-section coefficients. It establishes no lower bound on K and no invariance of K under a choice of g. The absence of an explicit reset analogous to the Gram reset does not prove the stated impossibility for every rule. T202's History at line 80 already acknowledges this distinction, but Scope and the synthesis still overclaim it.

Required correction: preserve the PROVED identities; describe their implication as an additional residual not controlled by semi-orthogonality alone; attach failure of the **tested** one-relation reorthogonalization schemes to MEASURED N201. Remove the universal “no rule” deduction unless a separate impossibility theorem is supplied. This does not weaken the measured CGS2 span-drift finding.

### L2-F2 — Medium: T201 drops normalization/near-breakdown factors outside its exact theorem

Locations: `tracks/bivariate-lanczos/theory/semi-orthogonality-implies-accurate-F-section.md:25`, `:27`, and the “constants polynomial” description at `:48`.

Theorem A at line 20 is an exact bound on computed quantities and passed reconstruction. Corollary A′ at line 25 explicitly retains

`χ = max_k ||F q_parent(k)|| / β_k`.

That factor is necessary; β>0 does not bound χ by a polynomial in N and M. Consequently the subsequent “i.e. ... O(u)||F|| with polynomial constants” must retain χ or assume a quantitative nonbreakdown bound. This audit does **not** refute the displayed χ-dependent corollary.

The F8 extension at line 27 has a more concrete algebraic omission. From its displayed inequalities one obtains terms

`η³ ||h^(1)||/β_k + η² γ/β_k + ρ′_k/β_k`

(and the appropriately normalized earlier rounding terms), not `O(η³ + η²γ/β_k + u)` uniformly in β. Both the first term and the rounding remainder lost their normalization factor. Retain these factors and prove the extended-step statement with them, or explicitly impose nonbreakdown assumptions. The theorem's exact bound itself requires no such new assumption.

Independent local countercheck, [check_local_beta.py](L2/check_local_beta.py), isolates the omitted division without claiming a counterexample to the entire BJ theorem. Let the two unit columns Q_W have Gram matrix `[[1,t],[t,1]]`, set `a=q_1+t² e_3`, and perform three **exact** CGS passes. The residual is exactly `r_3=−t³ q_2+t² e_3`, so β is asymptotic to t², normalized local overlap to t, and the full three-column Gram Frobenius error to 2t. Setting `t=√u/4` gives η≤√u but local overlap divided by `η³+u` diverges. At u=2^-40, 2^-60, 2^-80 the verified ratios are 2.62144e5, 2.68435e8, 2.74878e11; 100-digit formula residuals print zero. This is a countercheck of the F8 intermediate bound for its stated local CGS assumptions, not a constructed complete BJ run and not a refutation of Theorem A or the χ-dependent bound.

## Independent stable-reference drift experiment

New [independent_drift.py](L2/independent_drift.py) imports no developer or earlier reviewer code. It reconstructs BJ plus two CGS passes against all earlier columns, seed-11 node generation, and independent reference spaces. Rotated-square references use total-degree Legendre and Chebyshev products in the inverse-rotated coordinates; triangle uses a Dubiner basis. QR plus direct residual singular values measures principal-angle sine without `1−cos²` cancellation. The code also computes the running defect lower bound from orthonormalized computed spans.

| Case | Reference condition | max Gram error, double | final degree drift, double | final degree drift, long double | running defect bound, double |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| rotated square, n=24 | 782.84 (Legendre), 4262.82 (Chebyshev) | 6.8834e-15 | 0.00410546935 | 7.69069e-6 | 0.00028167921 |
| triangle, n=20 | 100.6324 (Dubiner) | 6.8834e-15 | 0.01016357140 | 8.07851e-6 | 0.00077683330 |

The rotated-square reference projectors differ by 6.6363e-13 and give the same measured drift to ~1e-16. The triangle's printed `reference_disagreement=6.8413e-15` is a **self-projection numerical residual**, not agreement of two independent bases. Even a conservative condition-scaled reference error is orders of magnitude below the observed drift. These are strong independent confirmations of N201 on the two named cases.

The long-double label printed by NumPy is `float128`, but the machine has x87 precision with unit roundoff 2^-64 (double 2^-53). Drift is much smaller at higher precision, consistent with rounding excitation. The reduction factors are not an exact universal linear-u theorem; operation order and rounding realization matter. No asymptotic conclusion in degree, node count, or across all geometries is inferred.
