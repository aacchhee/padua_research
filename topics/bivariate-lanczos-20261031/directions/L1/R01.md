### (a) Local relations and coefficient pattern

Ordering of the input code (`code/inputs/f6_givens_vs_hl_deficit.krylov`): within degree t the columns are (t,0), …, (t,t) ↔ x^t, …, y^t; column (t,0) = X·(t−1,0), column (t,j≥1) = Y·(t−1,j−1). So in this convention **Y is the "full" operator F** (applied to every column) and **X the "injecting" operator J** (one column per degree). HL BIT table (2.1) is the mirror image (H = x full, K = y injected), which is why plan.md says "only part of H_y is formed"; BJ 1985 (x_1-heavy order, multiplier x_{k_j} = first variable present) is of the HL BIT type.

- [EXACT] PD-T?bivariate-lanczos-claude-101 (`tracks/bivariate-lanczos/theory/window-coefficient-pattern-exact.md`, **PROVED**): the support of every F-column and J-column of H = QᵀAQ; all F-columns of S_{n−1} are formed (their exact support equals the child's exact window plus the child), only the J-columns (t,0) are formed, the other t J-columns per degree are implicit and fixed recursively by commutativity (explicit recursion, part 5); window sizes 2t−1 (J-product), 2t, 2t+1 (F-products), matching HL BIT Thm 2.3 in the mirrored order; the code's 'block' window (degrees t−2..t) is a superset. Minor routine lemma (part 6): the square part of HL SIMAX's N_k is unitarily similar to Z = H_x + iH_y (same subspace), so in exact arithmetic the frozen objects Z and N_k have identical Ritz values and residuals.
- [FP] PD-T?bivariate-lanczos-claude-102 (`theory/fp-local-relations-window-recurrence.md`, **PROVED**, routine first-order): β_k q̂_k = A_k q̂_{p(k)} − Q̂_{W_k}h_k − f_k with explicit constants for BJ (CGS) and HL (ORTH); matrix forms F Q̂_{n−1} = Q̂_n T_F + E_F with T_F the matrix of **computed** coefficients (exact-support pattern, boundary block B_F of size (n+1)×n, upper-Hessenberg pattern) and J q̂_{t,0} = Q̂_{t+1}τ_t + f only; for the implicit J-columns the analysis objects g_l, d_l (defect) with the exact **defect recursion**: the J-defect is forced only by local rounding and propagated by the F-recurrence.

Measured findings (all **MEASURED**, scope = the listed runs):

M1. **Product-aligned supports** (random square, tensor grid, Padua with cubature weights; also the square rotated by π/2, swapped, a product Beta-marginal set, and the square with non-product weights exp(2xy) in the probe): the J-defect stays at 1e-15–1e-14 until loss begins; the F-defect stays at 1e-15 throughout (as PD-T?…-102 predicts). On random squares the bj loss is predicted within the factor-3 band by **every** object (Z: ratios 0.73–2.5 on M = 1681, 1.1 on M = 6724; H_x 0.42–1.5; H_y 0.47–1.4; oblique 0.63–2.2); single-section and joint minimal residuals agree within a factor 2 (the first Ritz vectors to converge are localised at corner nodes, extreme in both coordinates; the alignment columns show the same vector minimises all three residuals). HL (ORTH) loss is a constant 3–8× below the bj loss and below u/ρ for all objects ("tracks", ratio 0.1–0.55).
M2. **Over-prediction by single sections**: on tensor 41² at n = 40, H_x and H_y have residuals 1.8e-15 and 1.4e-15 (S_40 contains every function of x, resp. y, on the 41 abscissae: exact one-coordinate invariant subspaces), u/ρ ≈ 0.1, while the loss is 1.8e-14 (bj) and 8.6e-15 (hl): single sections over-predict by ~10¹³. Z shows no convergence (1.4e-2) and no loss (uninformative, consistent).
M3. **Non-product supports** (every rotation by an angle not a multiple of π/2, L-shape random and grid, annulus, disk, triangle, sheared squares, rotated product set): loss grows exponentially from degree ≈ 8–12 for **both** bj and hl and reaches O(0.1–1) by degree 24–32, with Ritz residuals identical to the unrotated set (Z is rotation-covariant: ρ_Z agrees to all printed digits between a set and its rotation until loss). No object predicts (loss/(u/ρ) from 10³ to 10¹¹, tag "/under"). The J-defect grows with the loss (ratio loss/defect between 1 and ~10³ depending on the set, e.g. 3–6 for the rotated square, up to 4·10³ for the triangle at n = 16); the F-defect stays at 1e-15 until loss is O(1). The growth scales with the unit roundoff (rotated square, n = 16/20: loss 3.8e-11/1.2e-8 in double vs 6.8e-14/2.0e-11 in long double, ratio ≈ 560–600 for an ε-ratio of 2048): amplified rounding, not an algebraic error of the window. The triangle reaches loss 1 at n = 20 (M = 1681).
M4. **Complex HL SIMAX form**: our implementation of HL SIMAX Algorithm 1 with ORTH loses orthogonality like ~3ⁿ from degree ≈ 6 on **every** set, including tensor and random squares (random 1681: 2.8e-12 at n = 12, 1.5e-7 at n = 20, 0.93 at n = 40), with N*-defect growing at the same rate while the N-defect stays at 1e-15; full (non-truncated) orthogonalisation removes it (2.3e-15 at n = 16); long double scales with ε. N_k residuals agree with the real run's ρ_Z (PD-T?…-101 part 6) until the simax span degrades, but never predict the simax loss.
M5. Mechanism split (structural observation, explained in R02): in the real recurrence the truncation of every **F**-product is justified by F's own computed relations (F is symmetric and complete), while the truncation of the one **J**-product per degree relies on the implicit J-relations; in the complex form the truncation of every N-product relies on the implicit N*-relations. The measured instability appears exactly where an implicit relation's defect is not controlled.

Verdict on L1-Q1's dichotomy: **neither**. Within the regime where it holds, loss is Ritz-type and is predicted equally by single and joint residuals on random squares (no separation), single sections are falsified as the governing object by tensor grids, and outside product-aligned supports the dominant mechanism is the growth of the injecting operator's defect (commutator residual), which no single-operator or joint Ritz quantity contains.
