**3.4 Fixed scheme: well-definedness.** What I can prove (Theorem A, `givens-updating-rotation-count-backward-bound.md`): for any data and any order the scheme produces a well-defined floating-point rotation sequence (each rotation is formed from a stored pair (a,b), b != 0, and r = hypot(a,b) > 0) with backward error <= 16.5 u nu M(M-1) to an exactly orthogonal Q̃; this holds for 'largest' as well and is **not** an IEP backward-stability statement (it is met by doing nothing; reconstruction finding M1). What I could not prove is the statement that matters: in *exact arithmetic*, for an arbitrary order (including non-unisolvent prefixes) the fixed scheme maintains the zero-pattern invariants of the structured form (so the final H is order independent and equals the H of the problem), and consequently that the online detector is exact. Where it breaks: the column-rotation step and the dependence on the 'alternative' (redundant) columns of doubly-parented monomials, which are consistent only through the commutativity H_x H_y = H_y H_x of the *final* structured pair; at a singular prefix the redundant pivot is zero in exact arithmetic and the argument of the unisolvent case (pivots are quotients of leading minors) is unavailable. For all-prefix-unisolvent orders the invariants are the content of the 2010 paper's construction (not re-proved here). **Non-routine: escalated to the orchestrator.** The routine, PROVED substitute is `unisolvent-prefix-extension-lemma.md` (PD-T?bivariate-lanczos-claude-505): all-prefix-unisolvent orders exist (greedy Schur-complement completion; gepp is one).
