## 3. Results

**Theorem A (PD-T?bivariate-lanczos-claude-501, PROVED, routine first-order instance of rotation analysis).** With θ = √2 ε₁(2+ε₁) = 16.5u(1+O(u)), ν = (‖x‖_∞²+‖y‖_∞²)^{1/2} and Rθ ≤ ½: there is an exactly orthogonal Q̃, a product of the R rotations built from the pairs (a,b) formed in the run, with ‖(Ĥ_x − Q̃ᵀdiag(x)Q̃, Ĥ_y − Q̃ᵀdiag(y)Q̃)‖_F ≤ 2Rθν ≤ 16.5 u ν M(M−1)(1+O(u)) and ‖ŵ − Q̃ᵀv‖₂ ≤ 2Rε₁‖v‖₂. Relative to ‖(x,y)‖₂ (≈ √M for Padua nodes) this is O(u M^{3/2}); e.g. at M = 2016 about 2e-10, against measured forward errors of 1e-13 (Padua, scheme): the bound is loose by about 3 orders of magnitude. No assumption on unisolvence of prefixes, distinctness of nodes, weights or order: the bound is a *backward* statement about the computed Ĥ with respect to the unknown Q̃, which depends on the data, and it does not bound the forward error to the unique H (that needs the conditioning of the node set; not claimed).

Pivot map: the proof uses only that each rotation is formed from one stored pair (a,b), b != 0, so Theorem A holds for any pivot choice, including the data-dependent 'largest' rule (Remark in the theory file, PROVED by the same proof). Hence the 'largest' catastrophe is not a failure of backward stability of the rotation sequence; by inference (not tested: the C port does not accumulate Q̃) it is a failure to reach the structured H of the problem. Theorem A does not bound the forward error to H (OPEN, conditional on a structured perturbation bound).

**Worst-case bounds do not separate M from √M (candidate 503 killed as an explanation).** Theorem A gives M^{3/2}u relative; a statistical (random-signs) model of the same count would give √(R)·… but that is a model, not a bound. For the comparators the textbook normwise backward-error bounds for Householder QR (Higham ASNA Thm 19.4: ‖ΔA‖_F ≤ γ_{cmn}‖A‖_F) have the same polynomial-in-(M,N) character (LITERATURE, UNVERIFIED — not read in this round), so the worst-case exponents do not distinguish the methods.
