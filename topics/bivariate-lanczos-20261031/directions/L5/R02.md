## 3. Results

**Theorem A (PD-T?bivariate-lanczos-claude-501, PROVED, routine first-order instance of rotation analysis).** With θ = √2 ε₁(2+ε₁) = 16.5u(1+O(u)), ν = (‖x‖_∞²+‖y‖_∞²)^{1/2} and Rθ ≤ ½: there is an exactly orthogonal Q̃, a product of the R rotations built from the pairs (a,b) formed in the run, with ‖(Ĥ_x − Q̃ᵀdiag(x)Q̃, Ĥ_y − Q̃ᵀdiag(y)Q̃)‖_F ≤ 2Rθν ≤ 16.5 u ν M(M−1)(1+O(u)) and ‖ŵ − Q̃ᵀv‖₂ ≤ 2Rε₁‖v‖₂. Relative to ‖(x,y)‖₂ (≈ √M for Padua nodes) this is O(u M^{3/2}); e.g. at M = 2016 about 2e-10, against measured forward errors of 1e-13 (Padua, scheme): the bound is loose by about 3 orders of magnitude. No assumption on unisolvence of prefixes, distinctness of nodes, weights or order: the bound is a *backward* statement about the computed Ĥ with respect to the unknown Q̃, which depends on the data, and it does not bound the forward error to the unique H (that needs the conditioning of the node set; not claimed).

**What Theorem A is not (reconstruction finding M1; scope restated).** It is not a backward-stability statement for the inverse eigenvalue problem: its conclusion is also met by doing nothing (no rotations, Q̃ = I, Ĥ = diag(x, y), ŵ = v), and it constrains neither ŵ ≈ ‖v‖e₁ nor the zero pattern below the pivots, which are what make the output the solution of the structured problem. The proof uses only that each rotation is formed from one stored pair (a,b), b != 0, so the bound holds for any pivot choice, including 'largest'; consequently it carries no information about the 'largest' catastrophe. My earlier inference here ('a failure to reach the structured H ... with a different Q̃ whose first column is not that of the problem') was half wrong and is withdrawn: the reviewer measured with Q̂ᵀ accumulated (not rerun by me) that 'scheme' and 'largest' have the same backward residual and the same first-column residual on all-unisolvent orders, and that the structure defect (zero pattern below the pivots) tracks the forward error of 'largest' (n = 50 gepp: 8.4e-10 vs 6.8e-14); see PD-N?bivariate-lanczos-claude-506, N6. Theorem A does not bound the forward error to H (OPEN, conditional on a structured perturbation bound).

**Worst-case bounds do not explain the deficit (candidate 503; reason corrected after reconstruction finding L1).** The pre-registered kill criterion was 'the proved bound has the same M-exponent as the comparators'. That criterion did **not** fire literally: Theorem A gives M^{3/2}u relative, while the Householder QR bound (Higham ASNA Thm 19.4, ‖Δa_j‖₂ ≤ γ̃_{mn}‖a_j‖₂; LITERATURE, UNVERIFIED by me, citation checked by the reviewer against Connolly–Higham 2022) is of order MN u ≈ M²u; and Higham's Givens-QR bound (ASNA §19.6) is γ̃_{m+n−2}, linear, because rotations group into disjoint stages (UNVERIFIED). So the worst-case bounds rank Givens *better* than Householder, the opposite of the measured order, and the quantities are not like for like (a two-sided similarity versus the QR of the Chebyshev–Vandermonde matrix, whose H also needs QᵀXQ formed; no HL or CGS2 bound is cited). IDEA 503 is therefore rejected as an *explanation* of the measured deficit on that ground, not by the pre-registered criterion. The probabilistic analyses (Higham–Mary; Connolly–Higham 2022: Householder O(√(mn)u), Givens QR O((m+n)^{1/2}u)) are the natural frame for the statistical question (not read in detail by me). The observed gaps (size-level slopes 0.9–1.7 vs 0.4–0.8, R01 §3.1) are statistical, MEASURED, and not explained by any proved statement here. Open (finding L2): the 2M−3 stage structure of Algorithm 1 (rotations of equal j+i are disjoint, reviewer's brute-force check at M = 5, 12, 30) may allow M u instead of M^{3/2}u; in the two-sided setting disjoint rotations do not commute in floating point, so this is not immediate.

**Lockstep growth diagnostic (MEASURED; `logs/r02_dual.jsonl`, `r02_dual.log`).** Running double and long-double Givens-scheme in lockstep on the same data and comparing the active (H_x, H_y) blocks after i inserted points (10 checkpoints; Padua cubature weights, n = 15, 20, 30, 40, orders given, rand1, rand2, gepp):
- gepp (all prefixes numerically unisolvent, well conditioned): the relative difference shows an overall increasing trend with local decreases in i (three decreasing checkpoint steps each at n = 15 and 20, one at n = 30 (i = 162 → 214: 2.5685960e-14 → 2.3156126e-14), none at n = 40; `logs/analysis_r01d.txt`); the fitted log–log slope is 0.83–0.87 at n = 30, 40 and 0.65–0.70 at n = 15, 20 (a fit to ten checkpoints of a series that is not monotone; the comparison with the √i of incoherent and the i of coherent accumulation is descriptive only), final values 1.0e-14..7.2e-14.
- random orders: non-monotone, up to 1e-12..1e-11 mid-run at n = 40 (slopes −0.6..1.3 at n ≤ 40 depending on order), dropping to 1e-13 at i = M (rand1 n=40: 1.1e-11 at i = 0.9M to 1.8e-13 at M).
- given order (almost all prefixes singular): the active blocks differ by relative 0.24–0.6 at every checkpoint before the end, at n = 15, 20, 30, 40, but the *final* matrices agree to 7.9e-15, 9.9e-15, 2.3e-14, 4.1e-14. Intermediate states at singular prefixes are strongly precision-sensitive (double and long double, run on exactly the same input doubles, differ by 0.24–0.6); that the rotation angles there are built from rounding noise is a hypothesis, and whether the intermediate map is mathematically discontinuous in the data is not shown (it would need a proof). Yet in these runs the final H is accurate, while treating the intermediate H as an approximation to the intermediate exact object is not supported at singular prefixes (the observation concerns the runs listed, not a proved instability). This is the empirical motivation for Theorem A being stated for an *unknown* Q̃.
