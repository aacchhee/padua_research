### LIT?nn-mop-iep-codex-01 — exact conversions and their hypotheses

- Statement: §2.1 recursively constructs shifted step-lines in both
  directions; §2.2 Thm 2.3 converts their coefficients to NN coefficients.
  A main step-line alone leaves a parameter associated with the second
  measure; its actual first moment fixes it. §3.1 Thm 3.1 and §3.3
  Thm 3.3 reconstruct NN coefficients from marginal Jacobi recurrences,
  subject to nonzero diagonal differences. §3.3 uses site-shifted transfer
  compatibility, not a same-site commuting multiplication pair.
- Hypotheses/locators: one variable, r measures, requisite normality and
  nonzero divisors; §§2.1–2.2 pp. 4–12, §3.1 pp. 14–17, §3.3 pp. 19–21,
  equations (3.11)–(3.14).
- Relation/limits: corrects literal C4 “two fixed shifted lines.” Establishes
  an exact baseline, without a finite-terminal or stability guarantee.

### LIT?nn-mop-iep-codex-02 — normality, NN coefficients, flatness

- Statement: normality is equivalent to nonsingularity of the mixed moment
  matrix. Thm 2.2 gives determinant expressions for NN coefficients.
  Thm 3.1 derives the r=2 coefficient compatibility equations from
  \(R_2(n+1,m)R_1(n,m)=R_1(n,m+1)R_2(n,m)\).

- Relation/limits: direct predecessor to C1/C2. Positivity of two measures
  is not a substitute for mixed normality. No new local priority attaches

### LIT?nn-mop-iep-codex-03 — a single self-adjoint operator on an unfolded tree

- Statement: NN recurrences generate Jacobi operators on rooted trees;
  different monotone paths lift to different vertices. Positive lower
  recurrence coefficients permit diagonal symmetrisation. The principal
  framework assumes perfectness, strict positivity and boundedness of
  specified coefficients; Angelesco systems provide examples.
- Hypotheses/locators: §1.1 excludes measures supported on finite sets;
  §1.3 equation (1.17); §3.1 finite-tree construction and symmetrisation.
- Relation/limits: “finite tree” is a path unfolding, not evidence that
  arbitrary finite-support measures meet these assumptions. One tree
  operator is not the common-basis bivariate pair. The same-support input
  does not inherit Angelesco positivity of every mixed coefficient.

### LIT?nn-mop-iep-codex-06 — compatibility converses and boundary reconstruction

- Statement: for the prescribed invertible 3×3 transfer-matrix forms,
  zero curvature is equivalent to the existence of a perfect two-function
  Hermite–Padé system. Its coefficient equations define a boundary-value
  problem with the marginal recurrence data. Prop. 4.5 proves the converse
  by recovering the marginal systems and transporting the fundamental
  solution. §5 discusses reconstruction, including recovery via moments.
- Hypotheses/locators: Thm 1.1/1.1′; Prop. 4.5 pp. 13–14; Prop. 5.1
  p. 16. Perfectness and invertibility are essential.
- Relation/limits: generic inverse/zero-curvature interpretation is prior
  art. It is not an automatic positive finite-measure representation or
  a floating-point stability result.

### LIT?nn-mop-iep-codex-09 — finite perfect systems already have CC/inverse theory

- Statement: Definition 2.8 calls a finite system perfect when every index
  in its support-size rectangle is normal. Thm 3.6 extends FHV's CC
  algorithm to these systems, with boundary lower coefficients zero.
  Thm 3.19 supplies a Favard-type characterization for complex functionals
  using compatibility, initial/final zeros and nondegeneracy; its proof
  constructs marginal functionals and inducts on total degree.
- Relation/limits: directly defeats a broad absence claim about finite
  NN inverse theory. Same-support measures need not be rectangle-perfect:
  mixed rank ≤N prevents normality beyond total degree N. Hence the theorem
  is not an all-input theorem for the local common-support triangle.
  Recurrence data also cannot determine arbitrary mass scalings of the
  original weights; no such additional inverse uniqueness is claimed here.
