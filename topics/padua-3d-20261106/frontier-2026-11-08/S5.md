## 2. Literature: the problem is a recognised open problem

- **Gunzburger–Teckentrup, arXiv 1407.3291 (2014), PRIMARY.**
  - Conjecture (2.6): optimal λ*_{n,d} ≤ c(d)(log(n+1))^d on [−1,1]^d, motivated by Padua and Xu points.
  - They state that the growth "is not known analytically".
  - Table 1 gives numerically optimised 3-D cube values: n = 1..5 → **2.00, 2.95, 4.05, 5.09, 6.40**. They say the fit "confirms" the conjecture.
  - The repository topic never cites this paper; it appears only in a programme-ideas source list.
- **Bos–De Marchi–Vianello, arXiv 1502.04114 (IMAJNA 2017), PRIMARY.**
  - "Since the resulting curve is not self-intersecting, we cannot obtain total-degree polynomial interpolation."
  - The Lebesgue constant of approximate Fekete points (AFP) from the rank-1 lattice "seems to increase quadratically with respect to the degree, at least in the given degree range" (n = 1..30, compared against n² and N). This confirms the LIT-392 row from primary text.
- **Bloom–Bos–Calvi–Levenberg, arXiv 1111.6418, PRIMARY.**
  - §7: "it is not clear what might be the analogues of Padua points in higher dimensions."
  - Intertwining Leja / Re-Leja sequences gives only polynomial growth (H3); Re-Leja is O(n³ log n) univariately (their Thm 19).
  - Their numbered list of ten problems does not include 3-D Padua as such. Problem (5) is explicit Fekete points for some K ⊂ C^d, d > 1.
- **Caliari–De Marchi–Vianello 2008 (Hyperinterpolation in the cube), SNIPPET.** "Extension of Xu-like or Padua-like interpolation to dimension 3 (cube) seems to be a very difficult task". They also note that no minimal product-Chebyshev cubature is known in the cube.
- **De Marchi survey (CMA 2022), SNIPPET.** Higher-dimensional extension is "still the biggest open problem".
- **DEKL (already in the repository).** Lissajous–Chebyshev nodes achieve log^d for non-total-degree spaces Π_Γ. So minimal-node log³ interpolation exists in 3-D for some polynomial spaces, but not for total degree.
- **Recent work.** Two targeted searches (not exhaustive) found no 2018–2026 claim of a 3-D total-degree minimal-node polylog construction. Hecht et al., arXiv 2504.17899, studies downward-closed spaces and gives no explicit bound.
- **Known computable O(N).** Discrete Fekete or local-maxvol (1+δ) extraction from an admissible mesh with constant C gives Λ ≤ (1+δ)·C·N in polynomial time (LIT-382 plus maxvol). So the repository's O(N³) R-Leja bound is *dominated by known theory*. The paper note must not present it as the best explicit polynomial bound. A real "second-best" result would have to be **o(N)**.

\* n16 uses the 40-step U2 procedure (969 sites). n14 is the 680-site U2 transfer (cost gate failed). n2–12 are U2 at the 200-step cap. QR n16 is 161.5 (7.1·log³17); localdet n16 is 93.6.

- **Least-squares fits, n2–12.** Power law: Λ ≈ 1.25·n^1.14 (n4–12: n^1.38). c·log³(n+1)+d gives RMS 1.86, c·log⁴ gives RMS 1.25, c·log⁵ gives RMS 0.79. Local slopes rise from 0.8 to 2.3 by n12, and to 5.3 at n14→16.
- **The rising slope tracks the optimiser, not intrinsic growth.**
  - The step cap is fixed, while the number of parameters (3N) grows 13-fold between n4 and n12.
  - At n2 and n4 the repo is only 7–9% above GT.
  - Extrapolating GT's own n ≤ 5 fit gives about 15 at n12 and about 20 at n16. The repo's values are 1.7× and 3.6× higher.
  - So the repository's numbers are **not evidence against polylog**. They also do not support the "~quadratic" AFP picture; U2 and localdet already beat greedy QR by about 2× at n16.
- **Numerics cannot discriminate the laws.**
  - GT's n ≤ 5 data fit 0.797·log³(n+1)+1.83 (max residual 0.10) and 1.094n+0.82 (max residual 0.11) equally well.
  - The two curves stay within 10% of each other through n = 30 (34.2 vs 33.9), which is already N ≈ 5456.
  - **No feasible measurement campaign can decide log³ against linear growth.** Only a theorem or an explicit construction can. This removes the scientific value of further optimisation and pruning for the main question.

## 4. Structural heuristic: the Padua mechanism is twice overdetermined in 3-D

A Padua-type set has N nodes, positive weights and degree-(2n−1) product-Chebyshev exactness. That gives 4N real unknowns against dim Π_{2n−1} = binom(2n+2, 3) moment equations.

| n | unknowns 4N | equations |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 80 | 56 |
| 4 | 140 | 120 |
| 5 | 224 | 220 |
| 6 | 336 | 364 |

- Asymptotically the ratio of equations to unknowns is 2^d/(d+1): **1 in 1-D** (Gauss), **4/3 in 2-D** (where Padua's Lissajous structure absorbs about N excess equations), and **2 in 3-D** (about 4N excess). Symmetry reductions leave this ratio essentially unchanged.
- Lattice structures are the only known way to make moments vanish automatically, and they are excluded asymptotically by packing (n ≥ 110) and at n = 3..5 by the exact folded minima.
- This is my own dimension-count heuristic, not a proof. It predicts:
  - generic Padua-type sets probably **exist for n = 3, 4** (n = 5 is borderline);
  - they **need a new non-lattice algebraic coincidence for n ≥ 6**. That is equivalent in strength to asymptotically minimal 3-cube Chebyshev cubature, which has been open since at least 2008.
- **Unisolvence is generic here.** With exactness 2n−1, the Gram matrix is block-diagonal, so the set is singular only if some degree-n orthogonal polynomial vanishes on X.
- **Quality warning.** The n = 2 exact Padua-type sets (order 19) measure Λ ≈ 8.13, against GT's 2.95 and U2's 3.16. In 3-D the cubature mechanism did not give near-optimal low-degree sets.

## 6. Recommendation: NARROW, then FREEZE

- **Freeze now:** every open-ended construction, optimisation and pruning stream (T7–T10, U1–U3, pruning P*). Release the unused ceilings and record resume conditions instead of spending them.
  - The main resume condition is a candidate non-lattice algebraic structure that makes about 4N moment conditions automatic, or a technique that gives o(N) for any explicit family.
- **Run at most about 6 rounds of narrowed work:**
  1. **Paper note (3–5 rounds, mandatory).** Start with the fulltext novelty gate against Cools–Lyness/Noskov/Poppe–Cools, DMVX, Dencker–Erb and GT. Add GT's 3-D optimum table as the external benchmark. Correct the "best explicit bound" framing to O(N) computable (LIT-382 plus maxvol). Add a PAPER_MAP brief.
  2. **Bet B (≤ 3 rounds, strict kill as above).** Its outcome slots into the note either way: existence at n3/4 plus overdetermination at n ≥ 6, or a finite non-existence observation.
  3. Optional **bet C**, only if B finishes early, with a 3-round hard cap.
- **Then:** the Claude audit04 that is still pending (usable plus pruning plus the narrowed work), followed by PR preparation, final IDs and registry, with AC deciding the merge.
- **Expected value.** Almost all the remaining value is in packaging what exists (A) plus one cheap finite answer (B). Continuing at scale has low expected value, because measurements cannot discriminate and every constructive class has died on quality.
- **Main uncertainty.** An unknown algebraic structure could make 3-D Padua-type sets exist for all n, much as Padua points in 2-D defeat the 4/3 overdetermination. Its value would be very high, but the repository has no lead on it. That justifies a recorded resume condition, not budgeted rounds.
