## Can these constructions be moved to total degree on [−1,1]^3? No; the obstructions are explicit

- **(a) Interpolation transport.** It needs Π_n∘Φ = Π_n for all n, which forces Φ to be affine (elementary). The A_d domain Δ* (cusps, curved boundary) is not affinely a cube.
- **(b) Weyl-group machinery (item 6).** It is graded by m-degree. On S₃-symmetric cube polynomials, total degree n means Σ jα_j ≤ n with weights (1,2,3) on (σ₁,σ₂,σ₃).
  - C₃/B₃ co-marks give (1,2,2). DER, elementary: e^α has leading monomial (α₁+α₂+α₃, α₂+α₃, α₃), and the change of basis is triangular in dominance order. Hence **the symmetric part of Li–Sun–Xu's Π*_n is exactly the C₃ m-degree ≤ n space.** This explains the n³/4 count.
  - C₂ co-marks (1,2) equal the 2-D total-degree weights. That is the 2-D coincidence behind the square constructions, and it fails for every d ≥ 3, since the C_d co-marks are (1,2,…,2).
- **(c) Quotient transport in Xu's 2012 style** (Sobolev invariance plus Chevalley invariants) needs basic invariants of equal degree k, so that G-invariant Π_{km} = Π_m∘Φ. In O(3) only A₁³ frames qualify; inside the cube's group these are {e₁,e₂,e₃} (trivial T₂ map) and {e_k, e_i ± e_j} (A₁ × Xu's 2-D map), since at most two diagonal normals are orthogonal (DER). D₂ ≅ A₁×A₁ (ℓ¹ ≅ ℓ^∞ in R²) has no 3-D analogue: D₃ = A₃ has degrees (2,3,4).
- **(d) Unifying picture (DER).** Unfolded frequency bodies: Li–Xu A_d gives the hexagon / rhombic dodecahedron; the Berens–Schmid–Xu tensor case gives the cube; C₃ m-degree gives the rhombic dodecahedron. All three tile. **Cube total degree gives the octahedron, which neither tiles nor is spectral (item 9).** Every known Gaussian or Padua-type family has a tiling body. This leads to I1.

**Literature status of "minimal-node log³ on the 3-cube":** no positive result found. Proved neighbours: Li–Sun–Xu Cor. 4.12 (superspace, ~1.5N nodes); Li–Xu (minimal, but on Δ*(A₃)); and, trivially (DER), minimal sets on σ([−1,1]^3) from the sorted Chebyshev–Lobatto grid (binom(n+3,3) points, Λ ≤ Λ_tensor).

## Candidate ideas

### I1. Spectral-limit obstruction: near-critical positive Chebyshev cubature does not exist in d ≥ 3 (top)

**Mechanism (DER, proof sketch).**
- Let X ⊂ [−1,1]^3 carry a positive rule exact on Π_{2n−2} for the product Chebyshev measure.
- Lift by sign orbits to X̃ ⊂ T³ with M ≤ 8|X| nodes, weights w > 0, exact for all e^{ik·θ} with 0 < |k|₁ ≤ 2n−2.
- Let A = {|k|₁ ≤ n−1} and E_w = [√w_x e^{ik·x}]. Then E_w*E_w = I exactly.
- P = E_wE_w* is a rank-|A| projection, with exactly: P_xx = w_x|A| ≤ 1; Σ(1 − P_xx) = M − |A|; Σ_{x'≠x} |P_xx'|² = P_xx(1 − P_xx); and Bessel, Σ_x w_x |D_A(x−y)|² = |A| for all y.
- If |X| ≤ (1+o(1)) dim Π_n, then M/|A| → 1, since both are (4/3)n³ + O(n²). Typical nodes then have w ≈ 1/|A|, are pairwise near-orthogonal, and are 1/n-separated.
- A window limit of n·X̃ gives Λ ⊂ R³ with 1̂_O(λ−λ') = 0 and Σ_λ |1̂_O(λ−s)|² ≡ const. That makes Λ a spectrum of the octahedron, which contradicts item 9.

**Claim (CONDITIONAL on an audit of the limit step).** There are δ > 0 and n₀ such that every positive Chebyshev rule on [−1,1]^3 exact on Π_{2n−2} has at least (1+δ)n³/6 nodes for n ≥ n₀. The analogue holds for every d ≥ 3 with n^d/d!.
- Hence no Padua-type sets for n ≥ n₀, for **any** node set (phased, unions, non-lattice) and **any positive weights**.
- It also answers Li–Sun–Xu's n³/6 question negatively for positive rules.

**MEAS (consistency check).** On the 2-D Padua torus lift, the identities hold exactly at n = 6, 10, 16: Gram error ≤ 3e−14 and Σ(1 − P_xx) = M − |A| = 4n − 1. The diamond tiles, so there is no contradiction in 2-D. Script: `/tmp/claude-0/-workspace-padua-research/10b30ff5-df76-597f-8b43-0ded306fb443/scratchpad/s2code/padua_check.py`.

**Why it escapes the killed routes.** It extends T9-03 and the n ≥ 110 coset theorem, which cover only unshifted lattice cosets with Haar weights, to all node sets and positive weights. It closes three of the "Escapes" listed in `handoffs/04-work.md`: phases, non-Haar weights and unions.

### I2. Weighted, local version: no regular weight on the cube admits Gaussian, Gauss–Lobatto or Padua-type cubature asymptotically

**Mechanism.** The identities of I1 hold for any measure: P_xx = w_x K(x,x) ≤ 1 is the Christoffel bound, Σ(1 − P_xx) = N − dim Π_{n−1}, and the Bessel identity holds. Bulk universality (Kroó–Lubinsky type) gives the local limit kernel PW(octahedron) at interior points of the cube, so a local window limit yields the same contradiction.

**Why it matters.** It directly answers the key question: "find a weight with Gaussian cubature and relate it to the cube" fails for total degree on the cube. Li–Xu weights work only because Δ*(A_d) has a tiling local body.

**Closest entries.** LIT-374/192 (Xu 1994: "mostly negative" belief for d > 2, not a theorem), LIT-389, IDEA-207.

**Test.** Prove it for product Jacobi weights by hand first; check against Xu 2012 (2-D, diamond: consistent). **Promise 3, difficulty 3–4.** Critique: the universality hypotheses for cube total-degree kernels may not be in the literature; it inherits I1's caveats.
