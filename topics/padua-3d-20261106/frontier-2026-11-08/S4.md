## Ideas

### I1 (top): spectral transference, so no near-Gaussian positive cubature and no Padua-type sets for large n

**Mechanism.** Take a positive Chebyshev rule (x_i, w_i), exact to degree 2n−1, with N nodes. Let D = dim Π_{n−1} and K = K_{n−1}.
- Exactness on K(·,x_i)² and K(x,x) ∈ Π_{2n−2} gives exact identities: w_iK(x_i,x_i) = 1−δ_i, with δ_i = Σ_{j≠i} w_jK_ij²/K_ii ≥ 0, and **Σδ_i = N−D**.
- Equivalently, W^{1/2}V_{n−1}V_{n−1}^TW^{1/2} is a rank-D projection with diagonal 1−δ_i.
- For a Padua-type set, N−D ≈ n²/2. So all but O(n²) nodes have nearly orthogonal kernels, which also forces separation of order 1/n.
- Rescale at a generic interior point. The total-degree kernel tends to the octahedron's Paley–Wiener kernel. Weak limits give exponentials that are mutually orthogonal on O.
- Positivity plus Beurling–Selberg window counts give density exactly vol(O). Orthogonal at critical density means complete, so O would be spectral, contradicting Lev–Matolcsi.

**Claimed conclusion.**
- There is c > 0 with N ≥ (1+c)·dim Π_{n−1} for n ≥ n₀, for any weight with continuous positive density, and likewise for d-cubes with d ≥ 3, disks and balls.
- Hence no Padua-type sets exist for large n.
- Non-Haar weights, phases and unions cannot help.

**Why it escapes the killed routes.** It extends the unshifted-coset Betke–Henk obstruction (r08-negative-t9-unshifted-minimal-cubature) to arbitrary nodes with positive weights, and answers the brief's "Padua-type at some n ≥ 3?" asymptotically.

**Closest entries.** T9/T10 packing, the T5 Möller/commutator bounds, LIT-010, IDEA-029.

**Separation criterion.** Killed by any positive rule family with N/D → 1 on a domain whose local spectrum does not tile.

**Cheapest decisive tests.**
- Write the transference lemma and check it two ways: it must admit 2-D Padua (the diamond tiles), and it must forbid such families on the disk. Search the literature for disk or triangle families with N/D → 1.
- Check Σδ_i = N−D on the topic's lattice rules.
- Run a novelty gate.

**Effective constant.** A Delsarte/Cohn–Elkies LP for the density of orthogonal exponential sets on O would make c explicit. The lattice value is 1/18.

**Promise 4, difficulty 3.**

**Self-critique.**
- c is ineffective, so no finite n is decided.
- Universality, window counting and completeness each need careful proofs.
- Positivity is essential.
- It kills only the cubature mechanism, not polylog interpolation in general.
- It may be folklore in spectral-set theory.

### I2: the √N barrier, L² certificates and a localization diagnostic

**Mechanism.** Λ_X ≤ √N·√K_max/σ_min(V), where V is the orthonormal Vandermonde and K_max = Σ_{|k|₁≤n} 2^{nnz(k)} ≈ 8N. The weighted form is Bos's G-efficiency. Optimised sets sit near √N on both S² and the cube, which is what delocalized Lagrange functions give. Polylog needs Lagrange functions that decay like a Dirichlet kernel.

**Test.** For the n=4..16 sets, compute σ_min, max Σℓ_i², the decay profile of |ℓ_i|, and a mesh-certified Λ (source 9).
- Bounded max Σℓ_i² together with Λ ≈ √N means a delocalized optimiser; then try a localization-weighted objective.
- **Kill:** Λ ≪ √N with delocalized ℓ_i.

**Closest entries.** IDEA-027 and the direct-norm runs.

**Promise 3 as a diagnostic, difficulty 2.** Capped at √N.

### I3: discrete complete interpolating sequences and the octahedron Riesz-basis question

**Target.** N-point families with uniform two-sided L² MZ would converge weakly to a Riesz basis of exponentials for L²(O). This is open, and even the triangle case is open.

**Consequence.** Bounded-conditioning O(√N) constructions are at least that hard. The Walnut and Debernardi–Lev shifted-lattice-plus-perturbation constructions are a blueprint for structured phased-coset unions; T7-R06-U had no such structure. Triangular faces are where those methods break.

**Test.** Prove the weak-limit lemma in 2-D with Padua (LIT-488).

**Promise 2–3, difficulty 4.**

**Self-critique.** Polylog Λ does not imply uniform MZ, so this constrains only one route.

### I4: tile sandwich, and why 1.5N appears twice

**Observation.** LSX and the topic's parity grid both use ≈1.5N nodes, and vol(rhombic dodecahedron)/vol(O) = 3/2.

**Conjecture.** Every parallelohedron containing O has volume ≥ (3/2)vol(O). Quick checks: a truncated octahedron gives 3 and a prism gives 3. If true, 1.5N is optimal for interpolation in a tiling space containing Π_n, which marks the limit of lattice and tiling methods.

**Test.** Numerical optimisation over the five parallelohedron types.

**Also.** Add an LSX record (source 1).

**Promise 3 for the literature part, difficulty 2.** Explanatory only, since extraction from Γ_n is killed (T10-02).

### I5: Euler–Jacobi and liaison view of Padua

**2-D.** Pad_n is one parity half of the complete intersection C_{n+1}×C_{n+2}. Euler–Jacobi gives Σ_Z h/J = 0 for deg h ≤ 2n. Since sign(1/J) is the checkerboard and |J|⁻¹ ∝ the Lobatto weights, each half inherits degree-(2n−1) cubature.

**3-D.** A complete intersection containing a unisolvent X has generator degrees ≥ n+1, so |Z| ≥ (n+1)³ ≈ 6N. X is then about 1/d! of Z, while the Jacobian sign gives only 1/2. Binomial generators give cosets, which are killed.

**Prediction.** A 3-D Padua-type set needs non-binomial quasi-orthogonal H-bases, consistent with I1.

**Promise 2, difficulty 2.** It may duplicate Harris–Simanek (LIT-001).
