## T1 reconstruction

reflection differences give 2*l_j*e_j in its annihilator. Hence m_j divides
2*l_j, eliminating nonzero residues on odd orders and leaving only binary
half-period residues on even orders. Finite character orthogonality gives
double annihilation. The no-unit condition is necessary and sufficient for
retaining the *actual* projection orders; this rules out hidden axis-order
reduction. Every permitted code gives a reflection-stable subgroup.

For A=a+H, reflection invariance is equivalent to 2*a_j*e_j in H.
The resulting integrality and active-coordinate parity restrictions on
2*m_j*a_j are necessary and sufficient. The syndrome translation is
surjective; inactive odd orders and both half phases are covered. Cosine
folding preserves parity on active even-order axes. Inactive half-shifted
odd orders have one fixed reflection point, while even orders have none;
the written folded counts, including m=1, are correct. In d3 there are
exactly six labelled unit-free binary subspaces: the zero space, three
pair lines, the triple line and the even-weight plane.

Reflection stability of the whole coset implies that of its difference
group, so the obstruction theorem legitimately invokes the classification.
Injectivity forces at least n+1 levels in every coordinate. For zero, pair
and triple relation types the stated node lower bounds already exceed
binom(n+3,3) for n>=2. In the two-relation type, parity differences yield
nonzero pairwise Chebyshev aliases of degree max(q_j,q_k). Thus at most
one denominator can be <=n; after sorting q >= (n,n+1,n+1). Minimizing
the two complementary parity products gives exactly the stated even/odd

quantifier uses this reduction and monotonicity, not an order cutoff.

and (n,n+1). Its only nonzero points of l1 norm <=2n are (+/-2n,0),
