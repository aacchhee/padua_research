## T6-01. Projection comparison and the interpolation corollary (theory/t6-universal-lower-bound.md)

**Reconstructed statement.** For d ≥ 1, n ≥ 0 and any bounded real linear projection P of C([−1,1]^d) onto total-degree Π_n^d, ‖P‖ ≥ 3^{−d}‖D_n^d‖_{L¹(μ)}, where D_n^d is the Dirichlet kernel of the l¹ ball B_n^d and μ is the probability Haar measure. For d=3, n ≥ 2 and X ⊂ [−1,1]³ a Π_n³-unisolvent set (necessarily of binom(n+3,3) points):

Λ(X) = ‖I_X‖ ≥ max{1, H_{⌊n/2⌋}³/(648π³)} ≥ log³(⌊n/2⌋+1)/(648π³).

The hypothesis n ≥ 2 is needed only by the Fourier lemma (n ≥ d−1). For n ≤ 1 the bound Λ ≥ 1 is trivial.

**Verdict: VERIFIED. Every step and every inequality direction is correct.**

1. J is an isometric bijection onto the coordinate-even functions, and JΠ_n = span ∏cos(α_jθ_j) with |α| ≤ n.
2. E is contractive, Q = JPJ⁻¹E has ‖Q‖ ≤ ‖P‖, and Q fixes V_n.
3. Conjugation-averaging keeps only c_{k,k}. For k ∈ B_n, Ee_k = ∏cos(k_jθ_j) is fixed by Q, so Re_k = 2^{−s(k)}e_k. For k ∉ B_n the spectrum of Qe_k lies in B_n, so Re_k = 0. I confirmed this numerically with a genuine interpolation projection (command 6).
4. 2I − C_j has multiplier 2 when k_j ≠ 0 and 1 when k_j = 0, and ‖2I − C_j‖ ≤ 2 + 1 = 3. The norm bound for M = ∏(2I − C_j) is therefore **proven**: ‖M‖ ≤ 27 = 3³ by submultiplicativity. MR = S_n holds on trigonometric polynomials and then everywhere by density and boundedness.
5. ‖S_n‖ = ‖D‖₁, using the real regularized-sign test f_ε.
6. ‖I_X‖ = Λ(X) ≥ 1.

The constant: 648 = 27 · 24, where 27 = 3^d is the correction norm and 24 = d·2^d at d=3 comes from the Fourier lemma (there is also a π³). The general-d form 1/(d(6π)^d) is consistent.
