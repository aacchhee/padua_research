## Claim A: classification (PD-T?padua-3d-codex-T1-01), `theory/t1-reflection-classification.md`

**Reconstructed statement.** Let H be a finite subgroup of T^d with R_j H = H for **every** j. Let m_j be the projection orders and E = {j : m_j even}.
- The annihilator is L = M + {(m_j/2) b_j e_j : b in B}, for a unique unit-free code B ≤ F_2^E. Conversely, every unit-free B gives a reflection-invariant H with exactly these orders.
- A = a + H is invariant under every R_j iff 2a_j e_j ∈ H, which holds iff r_j = 2 m_j a_j is an integer and r_j is even on the active coordinates.
- Canonical form: parity syndrome C ∈ F_2^act / B^⊥ and free half-shifts ε_j on the inactive coordinates. This gives 2^(#inactive + dim B) invariant cosets per H.
- In d = 3 there are six labelled codes: 0, three pair lines, the triple line and the even-weight plane.

**Verdict: SUPPORTED.**
- I re-derived the finite duality (|H^⊥| = |P|/|H|, double annihilator) and the step 2 l_j e_j ∈ L ⇒ m_j | 2 l_j.
- I re-derived the necessity of the unit-free condition, the converse and the translation criterion.
- Fold counts: e(q) = ⌊q/2⌋+1 and o(q) = ⌈q/2⌉. On inactive axes, ⌊m/2⌋+1, or for ε = 1, m/2 (m even) and (m+1)/2 (m odd). This includes m = 1 and the fixed points t = 0, 1/2, which are the coordinates ±1.
- Coincident cosine images are handled correctly. X equals the set of sign-group orbits of A, and active parity is fold-invariant, so the count is a product subject to C.

## Claim B: obstruction (PD-N?padua-3d-codex-T1-01), `theory/t1-minimal-obstruction.md`

**Reconstructed statement.** Take n ≥ 2, and a finite coset A = a + H ⊂ T^3 invariant under R_1, R_2 and R_3. If X = c(A) has |X| = binom(n+3,3), then evaluation on Π_n is not injective.

The proof first applies the short-axis argument: every axis needs at least n+1 levels, which forces q_j ≥ n on active axes. It then bounds |X| for each code:

| B | lower bound on \|X\| |
|---|---|
| 0 | (n+1)^3 |
| pair line | (n+1)·2e(n)o(n) ≥ n(n+1)(n+2)/2 |
| triple line | ((n+1)^3 − δ)/2 ≥ n(n+1)(n+2)/2 |
| even-weight plane | the pair aliases T_{q_j}(x_j) ∓ T_{q_k}(x_k) force q ≥ (n, n+1, n+1), which gives M_n |

Here M_n = (n+1)(n+2)^2/4 for even n and (n+1)^2(n+3)/4 for odd n.

**Verdict: SUPPORTED.**
- Every inequality, the parity minimisation that gives M_n, and both ratio tests (n > 0 for even n, n > 1 for odd n) were re-derived by hand.
- No subgroup or translation is missed: the brute force below finds zero mismatches.
- The "up to permutation" reduction is valid because Π_n and the cube are symmetric under coordinate permutation.

**Two-dimensional Padua escape.** Verified.

- The Gram matrix is therefore diagonal and positive, and the count is (n+1)(n+2)/2.

**Degree-one exception.** Verified. The points are (1,±1,0) and (−1,0,±1), and the affine determinant is exactly 8.
