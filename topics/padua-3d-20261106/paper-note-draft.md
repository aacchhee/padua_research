# Positive-cubature and finite-coset obstructions for three-dimensional Padua analogues

Research-note draft, 2026-11-12. No historical priority is claimed.

## Abstract

Let Π_n be ordinary total-degree polynomials on the cube. We study analogues of the bivariate Padua package: exactly dimΠ_n nodes, unisolvent interpolation, positive product-Chebyshev cubature through degree2n−1, and polylogarithmic Lebesgue growth. An analytic argument gives a non-effective fixed relative excess over dimΠ_(n−1) for every positive product-Chebyshev cubature rule exact on Π_(2n−1) with all nodes in the closed cube, once n is sufficiently large, excluding the full package eventually. Its load-bearing external input is Greenfeld–Lev's spectral-polytope facet theorem; the local work proves the near-critical-frame, stationary-limit and completeness steps. Separate results exclude reflection-invariant finite cosets at minimal interpolation cardinality, prove exponential growth for monotone-height Padua stacks, and classify the ten-node finite-coset case. A certified twenty-node positive degree-five rule, Π₃-unisolvent and not contained in the cosine image of any single finite torus coset, exhibits the finite package at n=3, where the unshifted-lattice class (which supplies it at n=1,2) fails. Minimal-cardinality interpolation without positive cubature remains open at the desired quality.

## 1. Target and known comparisons

Throughout, C=[−1,1]³,

    dμ(x)=π^(−3)∏_(j=1)^3(1−x_j²)^(−1/2)dx,
    N_n=dimΠ_n=binom(n+3,3).

For a Π_n-unisolvent set X of N_n distinct nodes, Λ(X)=sup_(x∈C)Σ_(ξ∈X)|ℓ_ξ(x)| is the norm of its interpolation projector on C(C). The strong target additionally asks for strictly positive weights exact on Π_(2n−1) and Λ=O(log³(n+2)). Positivity and cubature are extra requirements; results about them are not lower bounds for every interpolation scheme.

The basic parity-grid cubature and Fourier/hyperinterpolation mechanism are established prior work. [De Marchi–Vianello–Xu, Thm2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0805.3529v1) gives positive degree2n−1 cubature with about n³/4 nodes. [Li–Sun–Xu, Cor4.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0808.0180v2) proves a log³ interpolation bound on that scale in a larger frequency-selected polynomial space, not ordinary Π_n at N_n nodes. Their n³/4+O(n²) cubature assertion is a conjecture. [Li–Xu](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.1079v1) has all-dimensional interpolation/cubature families on nonlinear A_d orbit domains; their transformed domain and degree dictionary do not identify them with this cube problem.

Two general baselines calibrate interpolation claims. Compact Fekete maximization gives Λ≤N_n: replacing one row of a maximizing Vandermonde proves |ℓ_ξ|≤1. This is existence, not a cheap closed formula. The explicit nested RLeja lower-set construction has the verified bound

    Λ≤8 binom(n+9,9)=O(n⁹)=O(N_n³),

using [Chkifa, Cor4.3/eq4.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.01731v2). Its value is explicit nesting; its order and mechanism are known. [Dolbeault–Chkifa, Cor1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.07435v2) supplies other general interpolation bounds, with optimal-design computation an explicit caveat. [Krieg–Pozharska–M.Ullrich–T.Ullrich, Thm1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.02220v2) gives O(√N) sampling projections using2N evaluations in arbitrary N-dimensional spaces; its worst-case sharpness is not a √N lower barrier for cube polynomials.

## 2. Eventual fixed excess for positive cubature

**Theorem A.** There are γ>0 and n₀ such that every positive rule for μ with all nodes in the closed cube C=[−1,1]³, exact on Π_(2n−1), with n≥n₀ and N distinct support nodes, satisfies

    N≥(1+γ)binom(n+2,3).

Consequently for every sufficiently large n, no such rule (positive, with all nodes in C, exact on Π_(2n−1)) with N=N_n exists, since N_n/binom(n+2,3)=1+3/n.

**Proof status:** PROVED with explicit LITERATURE dependency. The full proof depends on three local proved lemmas and primary-checked [Greenfeld–Lev, Thm1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.08854v2). Neither γ nor n₀ is made effective; no sharp1/4 leading constant is obtained.

Here is the proof structure. Exactness on products of degree≤n−1 polynomials makes the weighted evaluation matrix an isometry. Its row projection has leverage q_i≤1, and Σ_i(1−q_i)=N−dimΠ_(n−1). Reflecting angular representatives by coordinate signs, with stabilizers counted exactly, gives positive Haar cubature on the torus. Near-critical polynomial cardinality squeezes the torus count to the cardinality m_n of the integer l1 ball of radius n−1.

Discarding o(m_n) low-leverage atoms leaves normalized kernel correlations tending to zero. After scaling by n/(2π), the Fourier kernels converge uniformly on compact sets to

    F(u)=|O|^(−1)∫_O exp(2πi ξ·u)dξ,
    O={ξ∈R³:||ξ||₁≤1},   |O|=4/3.

Since F(0)=1, retained configurations are uniformly separated. Random translation over a period cell and compactness produce a stationary law of orthogonal exponential sets Λ for L²(O). Uniform local count bounds preserve the exact mean counting measure |O|dx. These are the conclusions of the stationary-limit lemma, rather than an assumption about a deterministic density.

Completeness needs a further argument. Plancherel gives ∫|F|²=1/|O|; the mean measure therefore gives EΣ_(λ∈Λ)|F(y−λ)|²=1. Bessel bounds this sum by1, so equality holds almost surely for every rational y on one probability-one event. Equality in Hilbert projection and Fourier uniqueness make the exponentials complete. The complete-law lemma does not equate critical deterministic density with completeness.

Greenfeld–Lev's theorem requires every facet of a spectral convex polytope to be centrally symmetric. O has triangular facets, so it cannot have this complete spectrum. Negating Theorem A would supply rules with n→∞ and N/dimΠ_(n−1)→1, giving the contradiction. The diamond in dimension two has a lattice spectrum and the actual Padua torus lifts converge to it; the proof therefore preserves the bivariate control.

Theorem A is not stated in the literature checked here; no priority is claimed. It answers negatively, for positive weights with all nodes in C and with non-effective constants, the question of Li–Sun–Xu [arXiv:0808.0180v2, pp3,16] whether degree-(2n−1) product-Chebyshev rules with n³/6+O(n²) nodes exist on the cube. Their definition allows nodes anywhere in R³ and real nonzero weights [p1]; the signed-weight and exterior-node cases and their n³/4+O(n²) conjecture remain open. The proof combines the Christoffel defect identity and Chebyshev torus unfolding with a kernel blow-up as in Antezana–Marzo–Ortega-Cerdà [arXiv:2101.08064v1, Thm14/proof, pp15–16], density-to-completeness compactness as in Kolountzakis [arXiv:1606.02452, Thm1], and the spectral obstruction of Greenfeld–Lev [arXiv:1602.08854v2, Thm1.1]. Kuperberg [arXiv:math/0405366v2, §6, pp11–14; §7, p14] also gives positive-cubature local-density precedents and the continuous Fourier-cubature density question. The local distinction is the passage from arbitrary near-critical positive Chebyshev frames with nodes in C to a stationary orthogonal law with preserved critical mean intensity, followed by completeness.

Effective fixed relative excess over classical Fisher-type bounds was already known in other settings through Yudin-type linear programming: Yudin1997 for spherical designs (known here through Lyubich p2), and Lyubich [arXiv:math/0703569v1, Thm4.1, §5(5.9)–(5.10), Prop5.1] for positive projective cubature. The Lyubich comparison has primary-statement coverage; Yudin is cited indirectly. Xu [J. Complexity19(2003),392–402], at search/abstract coverage only, reports an improved lower bound for positive interior Chebyshev-disk rules. These results do not supply cube constants. The cube/Chebyshev setting and the non-effective spectral route distinguish Theorem A; no sphere/disk transfer of its positive-frame mechanism is proved or claimed here.

A routine fixed-polynomial transfer also works: if p>0 on C is fixed and ν=cμ/p, multiplying the weights of a ν-rule with all nodes in C by p/c gives a μ-rule losing only deg p in exactness. Thus an eventual fixed excess follows for this bounded subclass. It retains the source dependency. General full-support doubling weights remain OPEN: moving-center kernel universality and retained bulk count have not been established.

## 3. Restricted interpolation obstructions and finite classification

**Theorem B — one reflection-invariant coset.** Let H≤(R/Z)³ be finite and A=a+H invariant under each coordinate reflection. For n≥2, its cosine image cannot simultaneously have N_n nodes and injective Π_n evaluation. This local PROVED theorem uses the binary-code classification of reflection-stable cosets, short coordinate annihilators and exact parity counts; it requires no weights or cubature. It does not cover noninvariant cosets, coset unions or arbitrary nodes. Its d=2 specialization explicitly permits Padua points.

**Theorem C — monotone-height stack.** Place a degree-(n−k) Padua set in the plane z=cos(kπ/n), k=0,…,n, with the stated local convention. This N_n-node set is unisolvent, but its continuous cube Lebesgue constant satisfies

    liminf_(n→∞) n^(−1)logΛ ≥ 3ζ(3)/(2π²)>0.

The local analytic proof evaluates the weighted height-Vandermonde logarithmic asymptotic and compares determinants across height permutations. Recursive/Radon–Berzolari unisolvence and the determinant mechanism are standard; the particular asymptotic estimate is the scoped local calculation. Reordered stacks are not excluded. No numerical growth fit proves this theorem.

**Theorem D — ten-node finite cosets.** Up to phase translation by H and independent coordinate sign changes of the cosine image, every Π₂-unisolvent ten-node finite-coset set is represented by four order16 subgroups or32 order19 subgroups, at zero phase. There is no positive-dimensional unisolvent phase stratum. The computer-assisted classification uses an analytic reduction to orders10..80, a complete HNF/phase enumeration and exact rank predicates, with independent full replay and independently implemented census. This is a finite-coset result, not a classification of arbitrary ten-node sets or an all-degree family.

Chebyshev lattice generation, shifting, HNF search and folding are older frameworks; [Poppe–Cools](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~josefdick/MCQMC_Proceedings/MCQMC_Proceedings_2010_Preprints/PoppeCools-final.pdf) is a direct search predecessor. [Dencker–Erb](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.04564v2) proves discrete orthogonality and interpolation on Lissajous–Chebyshev nodes in selected spaces. The read statements do not give Theorems B–D, but incomplete primary coverage prevents an exhaustive priority claim. [Carnicer–Sauer, Thm3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.01850) supplies a modern primary Radon–Berzolari/cardinal-formula comparison.

## 4. What lattice and finite computations establish

For full-support unshifted cosine images carrying positive degree2n−1 cubature, the dual integer lattice has minimum l1 norm≥2n. The regular octahedron's lattice-packing density18/19 (Minkowski 1904, as credited and tabulated by Betke–Henk) gives detL≥38n³/27 and hence |X|≥19n³/108. Thus this class cannot have N_n nodes when n≥110. This proved application with explicit LITERATURE dependency uses primary-checked [Betke–Henk](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9909172v1). It supplies an explicit class-specific threshold complementary to Theorem A's non-effective result for arbitrary nodes in C.

The packing reduction and determinant candidates are inherited. [Cools–Lyness2001](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-01-01326-6), §2 and Tables1/3, already gives the shortest-dual-vector reduction and matching candidate values for n1..15. Its K-optimality is weaker than unrestricted lattice optimality. [Kuperberg, Prop3.1/Thm3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0405366) likewise states the lattice-design/dual-packing mechanism. Lyness, *Notes on lattice rules* (2003), at search-excerpt coverage, additionally reports unrestricted optimality for enhanced degree δ≤4 and δ≡0 mod6 (here δ=2n, the minimal nonzero dual l1 norm). Thus the minima at n=1,2,3,6,9,12,15 (2,12,38,304,1026,2432,4750) and their optimality are reportedly already known; local certificates are independent verification, with no novelty claimed for those cases. The fulltext and Cools’s *A Belgian View on Lattice Rules* remain unread. Other local certificates may strengthen certification relative to the checked sources, with priority unestablished.

The elementary double-Lee-sphere anticode bound detL≥(2/3)n(2n²+1), a Lee-metric anticode count in the setting of [Horak–AlBdaiwi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.3475v3) proved locally without anticode optimality and attaining the minima2,12,38 at n1..3, followed by exact unrestricted HNF restart, removes the packing dependency for the four minima n4..7:92,178,304,486. This does not remove the dependency above n7 or identify global cosine-fold minima. The broader determinant list, global folded minima and rank-constrained fold minima are different optimization objectives.

Oversampling remains useful. The parity-grid Π_n projector uses (3/2+o(1))N_n nodes and has O(log³(n+2)) norm by the local transfer from [Kolomoitsev–Lomako, Thm4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.00608v1); the integer-translated octahedron has a fixed triangulation and the normalized Haar L1 convention matches. This is PROVED with explicit LITERATURE dependency. Wang–Wang–Wang (2014) report O(log^d n) hyperinterpolation norm on the d-cube, answering the conjecture of Caliari–De Marchi–Vianello (2008). This attribution uses secondary/abstract coverage; their exact operator and hypotheses remain unverified here. The log³ corollary is therefore an alternative derivation, not a new bound. The octahedral Dirichlet-kernel order itself is classical: [Dencker–Erb–Kolomoitsev–Lomako, Thm2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.03451) states it two-sidedly for the octahedron and applies such bounds to Lissajous–Chebyshev interpolation. A local O(log⁴) proof is separate. Neither bound transfers to a square N_n-node submatrix without the missing inverse-restriction control.

Three final finite programmes calibrate these distinctions:

- **Nonlattice cubic rule:** twenty distinct interior nodes with strictly positive weights are exact on Π₅ and Π₃-unisolvent. A fixed rational coordinate pair excludes containment in the cosine image of any single finite torus coset. The finite theorem is PROVED, computer-assisted, and uses an exact directed-dyadic root enclosure, rank transfer and a rational nonlattice witness. Finite minimal-cubature certificates in dimensions at least three are earlier work (Noskov–Schmid, Hirao–Sawa, Schmid, known here through secondary reports); their primary texts and classical degree-five cube-rule compendia remain unread. No first-existence or first-certification claim follows. Earlier cubic half-shift sets already supply twenty-node unisolvence; the positive degree-five package is the added finite requirement. Its sampled Λ≈30.0121 remains MEASURED, with no certified continuum bound or all-degree continuation.
- **Algebraic real calibration:** known Chung–Yao natural-lattice nodes admit rational rectangular and symmetric determinantal pencils, N_n distinct real affine nodes and Π_n cardinal polynomials for every n. The elementary realization shows that broad real-node existence is not an obstruction for those families. Hilbert–Burch matrices of planar point sets are classical; the bivariate recurrence-residual pencil construction and its linear syzygies are also inherited, so pencil existence is not presented as new. The finite Padua n2..4 exact-arithmetic calibration remains MEASURED within its stated scope. A bounded experiment found best sampled Λ≈5478.15/546899.58 at n3/4 after eight symmetric-matrix searches. These poor local paths from a badly conditioned known geometry neither reject the broad matrix family nor prove a 3D recurrence transfer. Full 3D quality remains OPEN.
- **Certified finite norm:** the retained optimized dyadic n12/N455 payload has the rigorous continuum enclosure **25.961333≤Λ≤29.062900**, obtained by intersecting nonnested m2/m3 mesh enclosures. The local inverse-residual calculation and primary-checked [Białas-Cież–Kenne–Sommariva–Vianello mesh theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18656v1) provide the bound. This is PROVED with explicit LITERATURE dependency on that mesh theorem; the external proof is not locally reconstructed. The certified QR baselines n4..16 use separate inputs. Floating normalized-Gram eigenvalues remain MEASURED and establish no uniform Riesz theorem.

External numerical comparisons must use optimized benchmarks without calling them certified optima. [Gunzburger–Teckentrup, Table1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.3291v1) reports d=3 values2.00,2.95,4.05,5.09,6.40 for n1..5; these include a stronger n4 value than the earlier value5.54 obtained in this work. A finite exact cubature theorem can therefore have much weaker interpolation quality than a numerical benchmark with different guarantees.

## 5. Remaining questions

Exact-N all-degree Π_n interpolation with O(log³(n+2)) growth remains OPEN without positivity, coset or height-order restrictions. General full-support doubling weights also remain OPEN. Theorem A supplies no effective γ or n₀, no sharp n³/4 lower coefficient and no signed-weight obstruction. The finite results supply neither an asymptotic quality law nor an all-degree construction.
