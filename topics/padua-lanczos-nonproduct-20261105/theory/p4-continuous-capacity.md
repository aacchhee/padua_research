## Statement

Let mu be an atomless compactly supported probability measure on R^2 with
U(n), and let H=L2(mu). Fix the same graded monomial order and admissible
coordinate parents as in p4-weighted-normal-quotient.md. Form the monic
polynomials b_k, coefficients alpha_kl, positive squared norms d_k and
positive raw-product scales sigma_h in H. Sources f_h are arbitrary real
functions in H. At every step inject epsilon f_h before exact projection
against all preceding perturbed columns; the start b_0=1 is exact.

For a completed degree s<=n and declared nonempty source subset J of
nonconstant columns through s, use

    ||f||src^2=sum_(h in J)||f_h||H^2/sigma_h^2,
    V_k(z;f)=sum_(h in J) g_kh(z) f_h(z),
    C_s(z)_(k,h)=g_kh(z)*sigma_h/sqrt(d_k),  k<N_s, h in J.

Here g is the frozen scalar response of PD-T136 with the current weighted
alpha; g_kh=0 for k<h. Let P_s project in H onto Pi_s, and R_s=I-P_s.
The perturbed polynomial-span projector P_s(epsilon;f) satisfies, for each
fixed s,f,

    ||R_s P_s(epsilon;f)||
       =|epsilon| ||R_s[V_k(f)/sqrt(d_k)]_k|| + O(epsilon^2).

Matrix columns now lie in H and their norm is the operator norm R^{N_s}->H.
The corresponding aggregate-source first-order spectral capacity obeys
the exact formula

    kappa_mu(s;J)
       =esssup_(z under mu) ||C_s(z)||_2
       =max_(z in supp(mu)) ||C_s(z)||_2.                    (1)

No smooth density is needed; atomlessness is essential to the proof. This
is a model identity for fixed degree, not an all-degree growth law or an
N3 recursion certificate.

For comparison, on a finite positive measure with represented Euclidean
source coordinates and C_i=C_s(z_i),

    max_i sqrt(1-P_s,ii)*||C_i||_2
       <= kappa_finite(s;J) <= max_i ||C_i||_2.              (2)

The finite capacity can be zero even when every C_i is nonzero. Formula
(1) must not be applied to a finite cubature source space.

## Definitions

Atomless means every singleton has zero measure. For finite Borel measures
on R^2 this implies that every positive-measure set contains subsets of
arbitrarily small strictly positive measure: subdivide the plane into
successively finer squares; if all positive subsets had a common positive
mass floor, a nested sequence of such squares with masses bounded below
would yield an atom. The source space is the Hilbert direct sum H^J with
the declared relative-raw-product scaling. Output norm is spectral, and
the supremum ranges over all sources, not a probabilistic law.

## Assumptions

Compact support ensures bounded coordinate multiplication on H. U(n)
gives positive exact pivots and finite-rank polynomial projections. Choices
are frozen, including ties. Every source is fixed while epsilon tends to
zero. No source is assumed polynomial, continuous, an actual rounding
error, or generated by an operation graph.

## Proof or evidence

All operations in the full-projection recurrence are bounded linear
operations, inner products and divisions by quantities nonzero at the
base run. For finitely many columns they are smooth H-valued functions
of epsilon near zero for each fixed f. Multiplication by x or y is
bounded because support is compact. The weighted monic differentiation
proof applies in H: derivative minus frozen response is in the exact
polynomial space through that column's degree, hence annihilated by R_s.

For completeness set Q=[b_k/sqrt(d_k)] and
D_epsilon=[b_k(epsilon)/sqrt(d_k)]. Q is an isometry R^{N_s}->H.
Q*D_epsilon=I+O(epsilon) is invertible, so the perturbed span is the graph
of R_s D_epsilon(Q*D_epsilon)^{-1} over ran(Q). Its norm is
|epsilon| ||R_s[V_k/sqrt(d_k)]||+O(epsilon^2). A graph operator Z has
largest principal-angle sine ||Z||/sqrt(1+||Z||^2), by maximizing
||Za||/sqrt(||a||^2+||Za||^2). This gives the derivative statement in H.

Write f_h=sigma_h u_h. Fix a unit output coefficient vector a in R^{N_s}
and define the bounded vector-valued function

    c_a(z)=C_s(z)^T a in R^J.

The output column combination is R_s M_a u, where

    (M_a u)(z)=sum_h c_a(z)_h u_h(z).

The source norm is ||u||_(H^J). Pointwise Cauchy–Schwarz proves
||M_a||<=esssup||c_a||; the same is an upper bound for ||R_s M_a||.
Write K_a=esssup||c_a||. If K_a=0, equality is immediate. Otherwise fix
delta>0 small enough that K_a-delta>0. On a positive-measure set E the
norm of c_a exceeds K_a-delta. Atomlessness gives positive-measure
subsets E_j of E with mu(E_j)->0. Define

    u_h^(j)(z)=1_(E_j)(z)*c_a(z)_h/
                       [||c_a(z)||*sqrt(mu(E_j))].

These source vectors have norm one and their scalar outputs are

    y_j(z)=1_(E_j)(z)*||c_a(z)||/sqrt(mu(E_j)).

Hence K_a-delta<=||y_j||<=K_a. Choose any orthonormal polynomial basis
q_1,...,q_(N_s) of Pi_s. By Cauchy–Schwarz on E_j,

    ||P_s y_j||^2
       <=||y_j||^2 sum_l integral_(E_j)|q_l|^2 dmu -> 0.

The limit follows from absolute continuity of the integrals of the
finitely many L1 functions |q_l|^2. Therefore ||R_s y_j|| tends at least
to K_a-delta; letting delta->0 proves

    ||R_s M_a||=K_a.                                      (3)

Taking suprema over the independent unit choices u and a can be done in
either order, so the capacity is sup_(||a||=1)esssup_z||C_s(z)^T a||.
For a countable dense subset of the finite-dimensional unit sphere,
pointwise continuity in a identifies its supremum with ||C_s(z)||_2.
Essential suprema commute with this countable supremum: the union of
countably many exceptional null sets remains null. Thus (3) yields the
first equality of (1). Entries of C_s are polynomials times fixed
constants, so ||C_s(z)|| is continuous on compact support. Every relative
neighborhood of a support point has positive mu measure; consequently a
maximum cannot exceed the essential supremum. This proves the second
equality of (1).

For finite data the unprojected output matrix has row (C_i u_i)^T.
Its Frobenius norm is at most max_i||C_i||_2 times the Euclidean source
norm, and normal projection is a contraction, proving (2)'s upper. For
one fixed node i choose a unit source vector supported on that node,
with its h-coordinates a right singular vector of C_i for its largest
singular value. The output after projection has rank one:

    (R_s e_i)(C_i u_i)^T.

Its spectral norm is sqrt(R_s,ii)||C_i||_2. Maximizing over i gives the
lower in (2). If M=N_s, U(s) makes P_s=I and finite capacity is zero.
The localization step fails because a positive finite atom cannot be
subdivided into arbitrary small positive-measure sets. This proves the
claimed distinction.

## Scope

The formula maximizes over arbitrary L2 errors. Concentrated sources can
be discontinuous and arbitrarily large pointwise while retaining a fixed
L2 budget. It therefore cannot be asserted for an L-infinity source
budget, polynomial-only injections, relative scalar-operation errors or
actual IEEE errors. The remainder need not be uniform in a concentrating
source sequence or in degree. No finite-epsilon lower is inferred by
exchanging those limits.
