## Independent proof skeleton and arithmetic checks to execute

Let n>=1 and char(K) not divide 2n(n+1). Work in an algebraic closure, choose primitive roots u of order 2n and v of order 2(n+1), and set X_i=u^i+u^-i, Y_j=v^j+v^-j. The even checkerboard i+j even is Pad_n in Dickson coordinates; normalized coordinates divide both by 2. Trace equality identifies only inverse roots because their common trace is the coefficient of T in T²-XT+1. Thus each axis list is distinct, and counting the even checkerboard gives N=(n+1)(n+2)/2. Both projections contain every axis label. Over F_q all points are rational iff both primitive traces are rational, iff u^q=u or u^-1 and v^q=v or v^-1, equivalently q≡±1 mod 2n and q≡±1 mod 2(n+1), with signs independent. This includes n=1 and q=3.

For the normalized Chebyshev values t_a(i)=(u^(ai)+u^(-ai))/2 (t_0=1), endpoint half weights e_i (1/2 at i=0,n, otherwise 1) give axis Fourier sums zero unless the frequency is a multiple of 2n. Twisted sums with (-1)^i are zero unless the frequency is congruent to n modulo 2n. The checkerboard sum is half the sum of untwisted and jointly twisted tensor sums. For two total-degree <=n modes (a,b),(c,d), a+c+b+d<=2n, so simultaneous twisted frequencies would require a+c>=n and b+d>=n+1, an impossibility. Consequently the normalized checkerboard weights w_ij=2e_i e_j/[n(n+1)] yield a diagonal Gram matrix in t_a(X/2)t_b(Y/2): the norm is 2^(-1[a>0]-1[b>0]), except the mode (n,0), whose norm is 1. This proof works equally for the odd checkerboard: its twisted term changes sign and remains zero.

Replace each t_a for a>0 by the monic Dickson polynomial C_a=2t_a(X/2); retain C_0=1. The product of Gram entries is 2^(n(n+1)+1), since the total number of positive indices among a+b<=n is n(n+1), and the exceptional (n,0) contributes one further factor 2. The total endpoint incidence count on either checkerboard is 2n+3. Therefore product w=2^(N-2n-3)/[n(n+1)]^N. The triangular monic change of basis has determinant one, so det(V_monomial)^2=2^((n²+3n+6)/2)*[n(n+1)]^N. Nonzero weights and diagonal entries prove unisolvence. In normalized x=X/2,y=Y/2 coordinates the squared determinant has the additional factor 2^(-2n(n+1)(n+2)/3), the squared column rescaling. This is not an integral-model theorem at bad primes.

For q odd, n=(q-1)/2, normalized x is a split-torus trace iff chi(x²-1)∈{0,1}; normalized y is a norm-one torus trace iff chi(y²-1)∈{0,-1}. For x≠-1 the parity sign is chi(2(x+1)); at x=-1 it is chi(-1). For y≠-1 it is chi(2(y+1)); at y=-1 it is -chi(-1). The even checkerboard consists exactly of the allowed traces whose corrected signs agree. Proof of the nonsplit sign: for w²=v, r=w+w^-1 has r²=2(y+1); writing s=v^((q+1)/2) gives w^q=s*w^-1 and r^q=s*r, hence chi(r²)=s for r≠0. The split statement follows from 2(x+1)=(u+1)²/u. Endpoint values follow directly from the label i=n or j=n+1. At q=3 (n=1) this produces normalized points (1,1),(1,-1),(-1,0), determinant magnitude 4 over Z and nonzero modulo 3. Endpoints cannot be suppressed by assigning chi(0)=0.
