### LIT-470 (LIT?padua-literature-claude-04) — location of the maximum of 1-D Lebesgue functions (four classical cases)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase:
  - (a) Trigonometric interpolation at 2n+1 or 2n equispaced nodes: the Lebesgue function is periodic with the node spacing. On each node interval it is one trigonometric polynomial with fixed signs, even about the midpoint, and a two-hump shape is excluded by counting extrema. So the maximum on every interval is at its midpoint, which gives explicit Lebesgue constants.
  - (b) Algebraic interpolation at the Chebyshev zeros: the Lebesgue function is bounded by the transformed trigonometric one and equals it on the two end intervals. So the global maximum is at x = ±1.
  - (c) Interpolation at the Chebyshev extrema: for odd n the extremal value is reached on the middle interval; for even n there is a small positive correction.
  - (d) Numerical table for n = 10: local maxima decrease from the ends toward the middle for the zeros, and increase toward the middle for the extrema.
- Hypotheses and notation: one variable; equispaced trigonometric nodes; Chebyshev zeros cos((k+½)π/n); Chebyshev extrema cos(kπ/n).
- Page / theorem / equation: introduction p. 105, which describes the method (sign analysis, then symmetry to locate the maximum); Satz 1 with its proof and (1.10), pp. 106–107; Satz 2, pp. 107–108; Satz 3 with (3.6)–(3.7), pp. 108–109; Satz 4 with (4.7)–(4.14), pp. 109–110; Table 2, p. 111.
