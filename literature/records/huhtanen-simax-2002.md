### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-122 — N is generically a polynomial in its Hermitian part (Theorems 2.1, 2.2, 2.5, 2.7)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: For a normal N = H + iK (H, K the Hermitian and skew-Hermitian parts, which commute), the set of normal matrices whose Hermitian part is nonderogatory is open and dense (Theorem 2.1, cited from [18]). If #σ(N) = #σ(H) (the real parts of the eigenvalues are distinct, a generic condition: Proposition 2.3), then N = H + i p(H) for a polynomial p of degree at most #σ(N)−1 (Theorem 2.2, Lagrange interpolation of the imaginary parts as a function of the real parts). For invertible generic N, N⁻¹ = p(H) with deg p ≤ deg(N)−1 (Theorem 2.5). For arbitrary normal N, a rotation e^{iθ}N has this property for θ in an open dense subset of [0, 2π) (Theorem 2.7, cited from [18]).
- Hypotheses and notation: exact arithmetic; N normal; H = (N+N*)/2; deg(N) the degree of the minimal polynomial.
- Page / theorem / equation: Theorem 2.1 p. 1095; Proposition 2.3 and Theorem 2.2 p. 1095; Theorem 2.5 p. 1096; Theorem 2.7 p. 1097.
- Reading evidence and limitations: Proofs of Theorems 2.2 and 2.5 are short interpolation arguments and were read; Theorems 2.1 and 2.7 depend on [18]. Not independently checked.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-123 — Algorithm 1: three-term Lanczos for H with start vector Nb/‖Nb‖ and vectors q_k = N⁻¹ q̂_k

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Because N commutes with H, min_{p∈P_{j−1}} ‖N p(H) b − b‖ = min ‖p(H) N b − b‖, a best-approximation problem in the Krylov space K_j(H; N b). It is solved by the Hermitian Lanczos three-term recurrence (3.6) started at q̂_0 = N b/‖N b‖; to avoid applying N⁻¹ the algorithm propagates q_k = N⁻¹ q̂_k, uses N q_k = q̂_k for the inner products, and gives approximate solutions x_k = x_{k−1} + α_k q_k with a three-term recurrence and the storage of classical Lanczos. For Hermitian N it coincides with GMRES/MINRES; Proposition 3.1 shows the equivalence for N = e^{iθ}H + λI with θ ∉ {π/2, 3π/2}. Complexity (Remark 2): three additional products N q_{k−2}, N q_{k−1}, N q_k per step beyond classical Lanczos.
- Hypotheses and notation: N normal, invertible, generic in the sense of Proposition 2.3; exact arithmetic for all optimality statements.
- Page / theorem / equation: Algorithm 1, p. 1100; (3.2)–(3.16), pp. 1097–1100; Remark 2, p. 1100; Proposition 3.1, p. 1101.
- Reading evidence and limitations: Read in full.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Single-operator Lanczos; relevant only as a placement reference (L4): this is Lanczos for one Hermitian operator H with a starting vector, with no second operator injected.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-124 — Remark 3: non-orthonormality of q_k and pointer to Rozložník–Strakoš

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Because of the formal multiplication by N⁻¹, the computed vectors q_k are not orthonormal, whereas the vectors q̂_k = N q_k are. Numerical stability properties of the so-called A*A-variant of GMRES have been considered by Rozložník and Strakoš [27]; this variant corresponds to Algorithm 1, and "their analysis can be repeated with the method proposed", with the additional fact that Algorithm 1 relies on a three-term recurrence to be taken into account.
- Hypotheses and notation: floating-point implications are only indicated; no theorem, no bound.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-125 — Rotations and restarts (Algorithm 2) and numerical behaviour

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: For nongeneric N (not a polynomial in H), Algorithm 2 replaces H by H_θ (Hermitian part of e^{iθ}N), restarts with the current approximation as initial guess and a new θ, and frequent restarts with random rotations "seem" to give GMRES-like convergence (Remarks 4–5, Remark 6 asks whether such a strategy can be guaranteed). Numerical experiments (Examples 2–5, N up to 600×600 with Matlab, relative residuals shown in Figures 2–7) compare Algorithm 1 with GMRES, BiCGSTAB and QMR on a few spectra.
- Hypotheses and notation: floating-point Matlab computations; only residual histories are reported.
- Page / theorem / equation: Definition 2.8 p. 1097; Algorithm 2 and Remarks 4–5, p. 1101; Examples 2–5, pp. 1101–1106; Remark 6, p. 1106.
