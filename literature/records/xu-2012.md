### LIT-725 — Lebesgue-constant statements for Gaussian-type and Xu-type minimal-cubature interpolation

- Statement in faithful paraphrase:
  - Cor. 5.2 (Gaussian-type case, symmetric polynomials on the parabola–two-lines domain, nodes (x_j + x_k, x_j x_k)): ‖L_n‖_∞ ≤ (‖I_n‖_{C[−1,1]})², the square of the one-dimensional Lebesgue constant. The node set and the interpolation space differ from the Xu points.
  - §6.2: for the minimal (Xu-type) case the result "cannot be deduced" from the one-variable constant "as we did in Corollary 5.2", since four sums remain. The case α = β = −1/2 was introduced in Xu 1996 (LIT-314), and its order was "determined in [3] based on the explicit expression in [20]", i.e. by BdMV (LIT-723) from Xu 1996.
  - Thm 6.1 / (6.2): the minimal-rule nodes are (s_{j,k}, t_{j,k}) and three images, with s = cos((θ_j − θ_k)/2) and t = cos((θ_j + θ_k)/2) for zeros x_k = cos θ_k of P_n^{(α,β)}, a rotated product grid.
  - Lemma 6.2: the Lebesgue constant is ¼ of the maximum of a four-subset sum. Lemma 6.5: max Λ_n^{(i,j)} = O(log n) at max{α,β} = −1/2. Thm 6.6: ‖L_n‖ = O((log n)²) at max{α,β} = −1/2 (proof by substituting x₁ = cos((θ₁−θ₂)/2), x₂ = cos((θ₁+θ₂)/2)).
- Hypotheses and notation: Jacobi-type weights; for α = β = −1/2 and θ_k = (2k−1)π/(2n) the nodes are the even-degree (2n) Xu points (cross-check by the checker, not stated in the paper). The one-dimensional building block has n = N/2 nodes there, so the decomposition differs from the X1 lift (PD-T690), which uses 2N equispaced trigonometric nodes.
- Page / theorem / equation: as listed (arXiv numbering).
