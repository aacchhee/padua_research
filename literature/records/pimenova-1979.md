### LIT?padua-literature-claude-03 — pointwise asymptotics of the Lebesgue function of two-variable trigonometric interpolation (rhombic frequencies)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: This is trigonometric interpolation on the uniform (2n+1)² grid, step h = 2π/(2n+1), with rhombic frequencies |k| + |l| ≤ n.
  - Theorem 1, eq. (2), p. 52 (page image): L̃ⁿ(x,y) = (2/π²){|sin(n(x+y)/2) sin(n(x−y)/2)| + |cos(n(x+y)/2) cos(n(x−y)/2)|} ln² n + O(ln n). The grid is x_μ = μh, y_ν = νh, h = 2π/(2n+1), with sums over μ, ν = 1, …, 2n+1. (The text above (2) prints the index range as 0, …, n.) This is Kuznetsova 1995 eq. (1) at m = n. On p. 52, (1) quotes Daugavet's rhombic Lebesgue constant as (16/π⁴) ln² n + O(ln n). (Corrected 2026-11-05: the 2026-11-03 OCR paraphrase gave the arguments as (n+½)(x ± y).)
  - Eq. (3), p. 53 (page image): for the square (tensor) frequency set, L̃□ = (4/π²)|sin((n+½)x)|·|sin((n+½)y)| ln² n + O(ln n). So the two pointwise asymptotics differ in the interpolation case, unlike the Fourier-sum Lebesgue constants, which coincide (Daugavet 1970).
- Hypotheses and notation: m = n; odd periodic grid; no Padua or cosine symmetrisation.
- Page / theorem / equation: Theorem 1, p. 52; eq. (3), p. 53; Theorems 2–3, pp. 53–54.
- Reading evidence and limitations: Theorem 1 and eq. (3) confirmed on page images (2026-11-05). Kuznetsova 1995 (p. 260) states that her eq. (1) contains this case, and the two formulas agree at m = n.
- Relation to campaign claims: the earliest located pointwise asymptotic of a two-variable interpolation Lebesgue function. It is background for the priority wording of PD-T156–159; see [P1 comparison](../../topics/padua-literature-20261103/p1-comparison.md).
