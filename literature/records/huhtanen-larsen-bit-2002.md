### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-101 — Algorithm: two-matrix Lanczos-type orthogonalization with a fixed multiplication order

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Given commuting real symmetric H, K with spec(H+iK) = S = {(x_j,y_j)} and a unit start vector q̂_0 whose squared components in the joint eigenbasis are the weights m_j, a basis of R^n is generated by multiplying earlier vectors by H or K in the order of table (2.1) and orthogonalizing each product against a window of earlier vectors. Cycle k (k ≥ 1) has k+1 vectors: first the first vector of cycle k−1 is multiplied by K, then every vector of cycle k−1 is multiplied by H. After k cycles there are (k+1)(k+2)/2 vectors. A new vector is normalized, or set to zero if its norm is zero. Multiplications by H and K are identified with multiplications by x and y.
- Hypotheses and notation: H, K ∈ R^{n×n} symmetric and commuting; q̂_0 of unit length; k_0 = (k−2)(k−1)/2, k_1 = (k−1)k/2, k_2 = k(k+1)/2; the listing orthogonalizes the K-product against indices k_0−1 … k_2−1 and each H-product q̂_l against indices l−k … l+k.
- Page / theorem / equation: pp. 394–396 (identification and table (2.1) on p. 395; Algorithm 1 listing pp. 395–396).
- Reading evidence and limitations: Read in full. Reading observation (mine, not an author statement): the "Algorithm 1 using ORTH" listing on p. 401 interchanges the roles of H and K relative to the listing on pp. 395–396 and to table (2.1) (the p. 401 version multiplies by H first and by K for the remaining vectors). Theorem 2.1 and table (2.1) are consistent with K first; which printing is intended is UNVERIFIED (possible misprint). Also, by my count the p. 395 listing starts the first-vector orthogonalization at index k_0−1, one earlier than the 2k−1 vectors counted in the proof on p. 398; the companion SIMAX paper starts at k_0.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): This is the "HL variant" named in plan.md L1-Q1. The campaign's operator pair (X, Y) corresponds to (H, K) only up to which operator multiplies first; the campaign should pin the ordering convention from its own code, not from this paper.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-102 — Theorem 2.1 (polynomial identification and leading terms)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: With l(k,s) = k(k+1)/2 + s and 0 ≤ s ≤ k, the vector q̂_{l(k,s)} equals p_{s,k−s}(H,K) q̂_0 for a bivariate polynomial p_{s,k−s} with leading term c·x^s y^{k−s} (c ∈ R, possibly zero) in graded lexicographic order (x^{j1}y^{l1} > x^{j2}y^{l2} if j1+l1 > j2+l2, or equal total degree and j1 > j2). Consequently span over j+l ≤ k of {x^j y^l} equals span {p_{j,l−j}} on S for every k.
- Hypotheses and notation: H, K symmetric and commuting, q̂_0 ∈ R^n arbitrary; exact arithmetic.
- Page / theorem / equation: Theorem 2.1, p. 396; consequence stated on p. 396 (Section 2.2).
- Reading evidence and limitations: The proof is only "modify the proof of [14, Theorem 3.1]" (p. 396); the proof was not read here. The paper notes that avoiding the monomial basis is "crucial for numerical stability" and cites Parlett and Golub–Welsch as support (p. 396); no result is proved for the bivariate case.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Supplies the graded-basis identification assumed in plan.md (total-degree graded orthonormal basis). Dependence on [14] for the proof must stay explicit.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-103 — Corollary 2.2 (vanishing polynomial forces zero vectors)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: If some polynomial p with leading term c x^j y^l vanishes on S, then for every k ≥ j + l the vectors q̂_{k(k+1)/2 + s} are zero for all s with j ≤ s ≤ k − l. Hence once a zero vector appears, the vectors that must be zero in later cycles are known in advance.
- Hypotheses and notation: as in Theorem 2.1; exact arithmetic.
- Page / theorem / equation: Corollary 2.2, p. 396 (the "known in advance" remark follows it on p. 396).
- Reading evidence and limitations: Stated without proof beyond the pointer to Theorem 2.1; not independently checked.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Describes the exact structural-zero pattern (rank deficiency of the graded sections) that the plan's L1/L3 comparisons assume for non-unisolvent sets; relevant to the "non-unisolvent prefix" mechanism in L5-Q1 only through the pivot becoming zero in exact arithmetic.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-105 — Inner product (2.2), orthogonal polynomials and Example 2.1

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: The Euclidean inner product of p(H,K)q̂_0 and r(H,K)q̂_0 equals the discrete inner product Σ_j m_j p(x_j,y_j) r(x_j,y_j) with m_j = |⟨q̂_0,q_j⟩|²; hence the generated orthonormal vectors correspond to bivariate polynomials orthogonal for the discrete measure. The recurrence coefficients (α, β) from Algorithm 1 give the polynomial recurrences (displayed for the first four polynomials). Example 2.1: 8 equispaced nodes on the unit circle with uniform weights give p_{0,0}=1, p_{0,1}=√2 y, p_{1,0}=√2 x, …, and p_{2,0}=p_{2,1}=p_{3,0}≡0 because the nodes lie on a conic.
- Hypotheses and notation: diagonal H = diag(x), K = diag(y) are allowed (the text says a simple choice of H, K is diagonal).
- Page / theorem / equation: eq. (2.2), p. 397; Example 2.1, p. 397.
- Reading evidence and limitations: Read; the example polynomials were not recomputed.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Same object as plan.md's X, Y, w, q_0 = √w/‖√w‖.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-106 — Theorem 2.3 (recurrence length at most 2k+1)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Let H, K ∈ R^{n×n} be symmetric and commuting with spec(H+iK) = S. Then, for any q̂_0 ∈ R^n, the length of the recurrence for computing q̂_{k(k+1)/2 + s} is at most 2k+1 for s = 0, …, k. In the proof, q̂_{k(k−1)/2} (the K-product) needs orthogonalization only against 2k−1 vectors of cycles k−2 and k−1, and each H-product in cycle k against at most (k−1−(s−2))_+ + k + s = 2k+1 vectors; the argument uses Theorem 2.1 to show that products of low-degree vectors are orthogonal to vectors of lower leading terms. The authors remark that they get 2k+1, "somewhat surprisingly", where the closely related algorithm of [14, 15] has 2k. The recurrence length therefore grows like the square root of the iteration count.
- Hypotheses and notation: exact arithmetic; H, K commuting symmetric; q̂_0 arbitrary (no genericity assumption stated).
- Page / theorem / equation: Theorem 2.3, p. 397; proof p. 398.
- Reading evidence and limitations: The statement and proof sketch were read; the proof was not independently re-derived. The companion SIMAX paper (record `huhtanen-larsen-simax-2002`, Theorem 3.7) states length 2k for the (N, N*) version and says one could reduce to 2k−1, so the two papers do not give the same constant. The reported locator "Thm 2.3, ≤ 2k+1 vectors at degree k, p. 397" is confirmed.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): This is the exact-arithmetic window statement used in plan.md. It is a LITERATURE statement only; no finite-precision version is given anywhere in the paper.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-107 — Cost and storage statements

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: With d = (k+1)(k+2)/2 vectors after cycle k, each iteration saves and orthogonalizes against of order √(8d) vectors (printed as a square root of 8d), in contrast to Arnoldi, which orthogonalizes against all d. The authors say this is the slowest growth of recurrence length for bivariate orthogonal polynomials they are aware of, also for planar regions; any concrete algorithm based on the existence results of [24] (Xu 1993) would have recurrence length at least 3k−3 in the k-th cycle. The method is described as "extremely fast", needing only a few matrix-vector multiplications, and very large sample sets are said to be usable because only a small fraction of the basis polynomials must be saved. If the nodes lie on a low-degree algebraic curve, storage can stay O(n) as in Hermitian Lanczos.
- Hypotheses and notation: exact-arithmetic counting argument.
- Page / theorem / equation: p. 398 (window and "3k−3" comparison), p. 402 ("extremely fast"), p. 404 (storage O(n)).
- Reading evidence and limitations: No total operation count is given in the paper (for example, no O(M N^{3/2}) flop count); the plan's total cost is therefore my derivation from the window length, not a published statement (UNVERIFIED as a published claim).
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Do not cite HL for the total cost O(M N^{3/2}); cite only the per-iteration vector count.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-108 — Window stops growing when S lies on a low-degree curve

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Combining Corollary 2.2 and Theorem 2.3: if S lies on an algebraic curve of degree k, the growth of the recurrence length beyond 2k+1 ceases in later cycles; this is easy to implement to make the algorithm optimal in recurrence length and storage. If zero vectors are discarded, the stored set stays fixed.
- Hypotheses and notation: exact arithmetic; the curve degree is known or detected.
- Page / theorem / equation: p. 398 (end of Section 2.2); p. 404 (storage remark).
- Reading evidence and limitations: Read; no proof beyond the combination of the two results.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Relevant to Padua nodes and to node sets on curves where truncation of the window is allowed; the plan's benchmarks (Padua, checkerboard) should state whether zero columns are discarded.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-109 — Extension to more variables

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: The method extends to arbitrary many commuting symmetric matrices (Example 2.2: three matrices H_1, H_2, H_3 with a multiplication table analogous to (2.1)); the recurrence length again grows slower than linearly, although "somewhat disappointingly" the improvement is much less dramatic than in the bivariate case, unless the nodes lie on a low-degree algebraic surface (for example the sphere, giving O(√n) length as in the bivariate case).

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-110 — Finite-precision implementation: zero detection with ORTH (MGS plus one reorthogonalization)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: In finite precision the orthogonalization will not return exact zero vectors, so a numerical criterion is needed to decide whether the vector being orthogonalized lies numerically in the span of the window vectors. The authors use a variant of Reichel–Gragg's Algorithm 686 ORTH: modified Gram–Schmidt, with a second pass if the norm drops below 0.707 of the original, and a vector declared to lie numerically in the span if its final norm is below max(0.707 ν_1, tol·ν_0) with tol = 2 l u (l the vector length, u the unit round-off). The design rests on Kahan's "twice is enough" result (cited from Parlett, p. 113). Algorithm 1 is rewritten with ORTH.
- Hypotheses and notation: floating-point arithmetic with unit round-off u; ORTH is applied to the window only.
- Page / theorem / equation: Section 3, pp. 399–401 (ORTH pseudocode p. 400; Algorithm 1 using ORTH p. 401).
- Reading evidence and limitations: The printed tolerance symbol was garbled in the text extraction; "2 l u" is as I read it and should be rechecked against the page image if it is used quantitatively (UNVERIFIED). No error analysis of the combined algorithm is given.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Defines the "HL variant" with local MGS + one reorthogonalization that the plan compares with CGS2; this is the only floating-point implementation detail in the paper. It reorthogonalizes only against the window, not the whole basis.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-111 — Remark on loss of orthogonality (finite-precision behaviour)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: The behaviour of Algorithm 1 in finite precision "seems to be similar" to that of the Hermitian Lanczos algorithm: loss of orthogonality may occur among vectors from different cycles when bivariate polynomials of very high degree are computed; in the low-degree case this is not a problem. None of the examples of Section 4.1 were affected: when the points were on a prescribed conic or cubic, the polynomial coefficients were recovered to essentially full machine accuracy.
- Hypotheses and notation: informal observation based on the authors' examples; no theorem, no quantification of "very high degree".
- Page / theorem / equation: p. 401 (end of Section 3); coefficient recovery statements p. 401 and p. 402 (after Example 4.2).
- Reading evidence and limitations: This statement is an observation, not a conjecture, and contains no mention of Ritz values (the Ritz-value connection appears in the companion SIMAX paper, record `huhtanen-larsen-simax-2002`, statement 119). No data on orthogonality loss are reported in this paper.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): The earliest statement in the corpus that loss of orthogonality is expected for the bivariate recurrence; it gives no measurable benchmark for L1.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-112 — Curve fitting characterization (4.1) and invariance (Proposition 4.1)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: For each leading term x^j y^l, Algorithm 1 yields the polynomial x^j y^l − p̂ whose induced norm is minimal over all p with lower leading term (eq. (4.1)), a bivariate analogue of the Lanczos minimization (4.2). By Corollary 2.2 the minimum is zero iff some polynomial of that degree has a zero set containing the nodes; otherwise the leading term with the smallest minimum is chosen to fit the curve. Proposition 4.1: the curves satisfying (4.1) are rotation- and translation-invariant (proof by change of variables for translations, "similar arguments" for rotations). Examples 4.1 and 4.2 show exact and approximate conic and cubic fits.
- Hypotheses and notation: norm induced by (2.2); real nodes.
- Page / theorem / equation: eq. (4.1), p. 401; Proposition 4.1, p. 403; Examples 4.1–4.2, pp. 402–403.
- Reading evidence and limitations: Read in full; the proof of rotation invariance is not given.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Supports the interpretation of vanishing-polynomial detection as a rank question used in L5 (non-unisolvent prefixes); not otherwise central.

### LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-113 — Least-squares approximation and interpolation; reported numerical results

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Fourier coefficients a_l = ⟨p_l, f⟩ (eq. (4.3)) give the least-squares approximant (4.4), computed with the same short recurrence. At D = n−1 an interpolant is obtained that is unique in the generated subspace and of minimal degree in de Boor's sense. Example 4.3 (interpolation/approximation of exp(−x²−y²) on n random points in [−1,1]², uniform weights): 45 points give an interpolant of degree 8 with maximal error 6.4×10⁻² on the square; large errors near the edge of the square keep the maximal error on [−1,1]² above 3×10⁻³ (Fig. 4.5, left panel), whereas on [−1/2,1/2]² the best fit occurred for 91 points and D = 87 with maximal error 1.4×10⁻⁶ (as printed in the text on p. 406).
- Hypotheses and notation: random nodes; double-precision Matlab; no comparison method.
- Page / theorem / equation: Section 4.2, pp. 403–406; numbers on pp. 405–406.
- Reading evidence and limitations: Figures were not inspected; the numbers are as printed in the text. No condition numbers or orthogonality diagnostics are reported.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): A MEASURED-type report in the source on small unstructured node sets only (n ≤ 91); it gives no floating-point evidence at the scale M up to 10⁴ of the plan.
