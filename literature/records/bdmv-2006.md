## Extracted published statements

### LIT-723 — upper bound for the Lebesgue constant of the Xu points

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Theorem 1, eq. (10): for even n, Λ^Xu_n ≤ 8((2/π) log n + 5)² + 4.
- Hypotheses and notation: Xu points of even degree n (nodes (3)–(4), N = n(n+2)/2) and Xu's interpolation space as in LIT-314; "for simplicity's sake" the paper treats even n only (§2, p. 136). Odd n appears only in the introduction (p. 135).
- Page / theorem / equation: Theorem 1, eq. (10), p. 137.
- Reading evidence and limitations: the journal text layer is clean at (10). The leading coefficient on log² n is 8·(4/π²) = 32/π², against the true 4/π² (PD-T693).
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): the first published bound of order log² n for Xu points. It is not sharp and contains no vertex statement. PD-T693 proves Λ^Xu_n = L_n², hence the sharp inequality for every n ≥ 1.

### LIT-724 — proof method of Theorem 1, and what the paper does not contain

- Statement in faithful paraphrase:
  - Lemma 1, eq. (11), p. 137: the kernel D_n(α,β) is ¼ times a sum of two products of one-dimensional Dirichlet-type factors in (α+β)/2 and (α−β)/2. With α = θ₁+φ₁ and β = θ₂+φ₂ these are the rotated variables (θ₁±θ₂)/2 shifted by the node.
  - Eq. (12), p. 137: decomposition of K*_n into four sign terms plus the T_n correction. Lemmas 2–3, eq. (13), pp. 137–138: crude bounds for the (n±1) products and for D_n + D_{n+1}.
  - Eqs. (16)–(18), pp. 138–139: the node sum is split into the two types A and B. The reindexing k = i+j, m = i−j (p. 139) maps the node indices into a superset of a product range, and the sum is enlarged to a product of two one-dimensional sums.
  - Lemmas 4–5, eqs. (19)–(20), p. 140: each one-dimensional factor is at most (2/π) log n + 4 (resp. + 5). The proof closes on pp. 140–141.
  - The paper contains no identity (every step is an inequality), no identification of the one-dimensional factors with the Lebesgue constant of the Chebyshev zeros, no lower bound (the introduction, p. 135, only quotes one-dimensional, ball and tensor-product facts), and no statement on where the maximum lies. The acknowledgment (p. 141) thanks Yuan Xu for suggestions on the proof.
- Hypotheses and notation: as for LIT-723.
- Page / theorem / equation: as listed.
- Reading evidence and limitations: a grep of the full text for vert, corner, maxim, attain, sharp, exact and optimal found no statement about the location of the maximum; the "attained" hits concern the one-dimensional and tensor-product minimal growth.
- Relation to campaign claims: PD-T690 (X1-01) is a sharper form of this method (an exact fiber-sum lift instead of enlarged sums), so its mechanism is "partially known" and is not claimed as new; the exact identity and the maximizers were not found in the documented search.
