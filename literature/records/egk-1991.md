### LIT-175 (LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-133) — Representation and the updating problem for Jacobi matrices

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Orthonormal polynomials for the discrete inner product with nodes x_j and weights w_j² are represented by the Jacobi matrix J_n (symmetric, tridiagonal, unreduced, eigenvalues the nodes), the zeroth moment σ (= the norm of the weight vector, with the first components of the normalized eigenvectors of J giving the weights, eq. (3.5)) and, for least-squares fits, the Fourier coefficients d_n. The updating problem (Section 4.1) is: given σ, J_n, d_n and a new triple {x, w, y}, find the data for the enlarged inner product; the solution exists for ñ ≤ n and the size can increase to n+1 iff x is not already an eigenvalue of J_n. The one-point solution is the trivial start, so any fit can be built point by point without storing the data.
- Hypotheses and notation: real nodes; exact arithmetic; N data points, order n ≤ N.
- Page / theorem / equation: Sections 3–4.1, pp. 330–332 (eq. (3.5) p. 331; Section 4.1 p. 332).
- Reading evidence and limitations: Read.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): The univariate prototype of point-by-point updating that the 2010 Givens IEP (record `vanbarel-chesnokov-2010`, cites this paper as ref. [12]) generalizes; the VB–C paper does not state that it extends this paper's rotation scheme.

### LIT-176 (LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-134) — Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.1 (rotation update; only a leading submatrix is needed)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Updating by one point is achieved by an orthogonal similarity J_{N+1} = Q [J_N 0; 0 x] Qᵀ, where Q is uniquely determined by requiring tridiagonality and by its first row (eq. (4.4)); σ_{N+1}² = σ_N² + w² (4.2); the Fourier coefficients are updated by Q (4.3). Algorithmically Q = R_N … R_1 is a product of rotations between row j and row N+1 (eq. (4.7)); the first rotation sets (4.4) and the others chase the extra row/column to restore tridiagonal form, each step touching only the last row/column and O(1) entries (Figure of the chase on pp. 333–334). Corollary 4.1: the leading n × n submatrix of J_{N+1} and the first n entries of d_{N+1} depend only on the leading n × n submatrix of J_N and the first n entries of d_N and on the triple {x, w, y}, so partial matrices can be updated.
- Hypotheses and notation: real nodes; exact arithmetic; the rotations are plane rotations (condition number 1).
- Page / theorem / equation: Theorem 4.1, pp. 332–333; eq. (4.7) and Corollary 4.1, pp. 333–334; partial-matrix discussion Section 4.6, p. 338.
- Reading evidence and limitations: Statements read; proofs not re-derived.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Gives the exact-arithmetic update; the bivariate case is not covered by these theorems.

### LIT-177 (LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-135) — Lanczos-type (lower triangular, LTL) update is unstable; rotations are stable

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: The lower-triangular Lanczos (LTL) method of Section 4.3 is described as equivalent to the modified Chebyshev algorithm of Gautschi [4] and as suffering the instabilities mentioned there, particularly for uniformly spaced knots. Since the rotation method "provides stable answers", the authors conclude it is not likely that the underlying map itself is ill conditioned (as suggested in [4]), only that the LTL method is unsuitable. The determinant method (Section 4.4) and the special-form method (Section 4.5) are Lanczos-type variants with the same forward recurrence character.
- Hypotheses and notation: floating-point remark; no theorem.
- Page / theorem / equation: p. 335 (Section 4.3), p. 337 (Section 4.5).
- Reading evidence and limitations: Qualitative; "stable" is not defined or analyzed formally in these pages.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): A statement that a Lanczos/moment-type route is less stable than rotations in the univariate Jacobi setting; relevant context for L5 (Givens vs Lanczos-type accuracy), not a bivariate result.

### LIT-178 (LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-136) — Downdating, hyper-rotations and conditions for existence (Theorems 5.1, 5.2)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Downdating by a complex weight replaces w² by −w², which keeps the matrices real but uses hyper-rotations, whose condition number can be arbitrarily large, so "the inherent instability of downdating" shows up as ill-conditioning of these transformations (p. 340). Theorem 5.1: downdating can also be done by reversing the rotations (eigenvector method); the similarity is the product of the same rotations, determined from the last two elements of the eigenvector of J_{N+1} belonging to x. Theorem 5.2: for a partial matrix J_n, with q the solution of the first n+1 equations of (J_n − xI)q = 0 normalized so that its first entry is w/σ_{N+1}, downdating by {x, w} is possible up to the largest size ñ for which the leading ñ-vector of q has norm below a threshold (printed formula garbled; the prose says a real η exists when ‖q‖ < 1); if w ≥ σ_{N+1} no downdating is possible. Essentially downdating is possible when the weight is sufficiently small, otherwise the degree must be reduced. The stability of the reversed-rotation process depends on the accuracy of the first rotation R_n, which is limited by the condition of a forward substitution (p. 342).
- Hypotheses and notation: real arithmetic with hyper-rotations or reversed plane rotations; exact-arithmetic theorems.
- Page / theorem / equation: Section 5.2, p. 340; Theorem 5.1, pp. 340–341; Theorem 5.2, p. 341; stability remark, p. 342.
- Reading evidence and limitations: Read; the displayed conditions in Theorem 5.2 are garbled in the text layer (paraphrased from prose).
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): Downdating is outside the campaign's scope; recorded because the paper's stability discussion is about this operation.

### LIT-180 (LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-138) — Numerical test design and the reported accuracy decay under sliding-window up/downdating

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Test: moving least-squares fit to noisy data x_j = −1 + 2(j−1)/(N−1) + δ, w_j = 1/√N + δ, y_j = 1.5 + sin(4x_j) + δ (|δ| < 10⁻⁴, uniform); a window of M points is slid by updating with a new triple and downdating the oldest; after every n_s = 5 steps the result is compared with a reference solution obtained by updating from scratch with RHR; the accuracy is reported as the number of correct decimal digits of the α_i, β_i of J_n and of d_n (and of the fits q_j). Reported results (Figures 1–5 discussed in the text): with N = 50, M = 10, n = 5 (RHR) the accuracy decays both as the window slides along the data set and as the degree of the fit q_j increases (a statement the authors make in the text next to Figures 1–2); relative errors in the diagonal entries of J and in d become unduly large at the fourth stage where, by symmetry, zero quantities are being computed (Fig. 2(b)); the decay is consistent with the bound on the condition of the product of hyper-rotations; M = 20 shows an insignificant decrease in accuracy. The propagation of round-off from stage to stage is said to resemble shooting-type methods, so the test is "quite severe".
- Hypotheses and notation: double precision (not stated explicitly in the pages read); methods RHR, REV, TLD, TLS.
- Page / theorem / equation: Section 7 (design), pp. 348–349 (garbled formulas); discussion pp. 349–352 (Figures 1–5 on pp. 349–351).
- Reading evidence and limitations: Digit counts are read from figures in the paper, not from the text, and the plots were not inspected, so no numbers are claimed beyond the prose.
- Relation to campaign claims (no inherited proof status): MEASURED-type source evidence about sliding-window (up plus down) accuracy in one univariate example; it does not concern pure updating error versus M.

### LIT-182 (LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-140) — Accuracy loss of updating (Gauss and equispaced points) and downdating (40 points)

- Statement in faithful paraphrase: In "other exploratory tests", for updating with point distributions from classical Gauss quadratures on finite intervals, the TLS and RHR methods agree within about 0.5 decimals and lose fewer than about three decimal places for as many as 1,000 points; for equally spaced points both lose about three decimals after 500 points; TLS, a little more accurate than RHR on this data, can suffer extreme exponent growth and fail unless care is taken, whereas RHR is immune. For downdating, 40 classical Gauss points suffice for both methods to lose three digits, and both lose 16 digits on 40 equally spaced points, while the top part of the resulting Jacobi matrix is still determined to almost full accuracy.
- Hypotheses and notation: exploratory (data not shown); real nodes; univariate.
