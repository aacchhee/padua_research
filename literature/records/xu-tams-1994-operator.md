### LIT-237 (LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-233) — Setting, three-term relation and rank conditions; Favard-type theorems (Thm 2.1, 2.2)

- Verification: **Full text read via the AMS PDF (tool digest); several displays (2.5), (2.6), the C_k rank condition and an index range in Theorem 3.1 were garbled in the extraction and are NOT transcribed. `LITERATURE` attribution only.**
- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Polynomial vectors P_k = (P_k^1..P_k^{r_k})^T collect polynomials of exact total degree k, r_k = dim Pi_k^d - dim Pi_{k-1}^d (p. 194, eq. 2.2). Three-term relation: x_i P_k = A_{k,i} P_{k+1} + B_{k,i} P_k + A_{k-1,i}^T P_{k-1}, A_{k,i} of size r_k x r_{k+1}, B_{k,i} symmetric (Theorem 2.2(a), p. 195; Corollary 2.3, p. 196). Rank condition (Theorem 2.2(b)): the stacked matrix A_k = [A_{k,1}; ...; A_{k,d}] of size d r_k x r_{k+1} has rank r_{k+1}; consequently matrices D_{k,i} exist with the sum over i of D_{k,i} A_{k,i} equal to the identity (eq. 2.7, p. 196). Theorem 2.1 (p. 195, taken from Xu's earlier paper [17]): for a sequence P_k with P_k nonzero of total degree k, existence of a linear functional inducing a quasi-inner product for which {P_k} is an orthogonal basis is equivalent to the three-term relation together with the rank condition; commutativity is not a hypothesis and the functional need not be positive definite. Theorem 2.2 (pp. 195-196): the orthonormal version (P_0 = 1): existence of an inner product making {P_k} orthonormal is equivalent to the three-term relation with rank A_k = r_{k+1}; again commutativity is not assumed, and the proof shows the Gram matrices H_k = L(P_k P_k^T) equal the identity by induction. Positive definiteness of the inner product gives an orthonormal basis Q_n = S_n^{-1} P_n of the polynomial space. Theorem 2.2 does not say whether the functional has an integral representation (p. 196).
- Hypotheses and notation: Dimension d (any); real polynomials; P_{-1} = 0, P_0 = 1.

### LIT-239 (LIT?bivariate-lanczos-claude-235) — Measure representation, determinacy and the spectral theorem (Theorems 3.1, 4.1, 4.6)

- Verification: **Full text via digest. `LITERATURE` attribution only.**
- Statement in faithful paraphrase: Theorem 3.1 (p. 197): the following are equivalent: (i) there is a determinate measure with compact support in R^d making {P_n} orthonormal; (ii) the three-term relation with the rank condition holds and the coefficient blocks satisfy uniform bounds on spectral norms in k (the index range in (ii) is garbled in the extraction). For d = 1 it is classical; Kowalski [9] gave an integral representation without nonnegativity or determinacy. Theorem 4.1 (p. 198): bounded, mutually commuting self-adjoint operators with a cyclic vector are unitarily equivalent to multiplication operators on L^2(R^d, mu) with compactly supported mu (commutation via spectral measures, eq. 4.1; for bounded operators commutation is T_1 T_2 = T_2 T_1). Theorem 4.6 (p. 201): bounded coefficient sequences yield a compactly supported measure mu under which the T_i are unitarily equivalent to multiplication operators; Lemma 4.7: the P_n are orthonormal with respect to mu with the same coefficients. The proof of 3.1 uses Berg's criterion for determinacy (pp. 201-202). The paper contains no zero or cubature results; these are announced for subsequent papers (p. 197).
