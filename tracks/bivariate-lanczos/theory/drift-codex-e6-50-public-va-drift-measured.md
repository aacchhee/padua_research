## Statement

**MEASURED, finite execution scope.** In the recorded Python/NumPy/SciPy environment, actual unmodified `vandermonde_arnoldi_CGS` from polyrat 0.2.2, upstream commit `6903d7af9995c9d88e661fb229626696e00fdfba`, was executed on every member of the unchanged twelve-case `L2.BENCH` suite at its original maximal degree and on the frozen once-rounded rational T24/Q24 grids at degrees 12 and 16. Two upstream coordinate orientations and own L2 full-CGS2 dev, mirror, alt and adapt were evaluated on each case.

1. On all sixteen case instances, upstream input `[y,x]` produces columns entrywise equal to own L2 dev in this execution. Inspection and an exhaustive parent check through degree 47 map upstream `[y,x]` to campaign dev, including within-degree order. Upstream `[x,y]` maps to mirror with the campaign within-degree order reversed. Source inspection is an implementation fact, not an inherited published stability theorem.
2. On frozen rotpi4 at degree 24 and triangle at degree 20, upstream `[y,x]` has computed reference span gap respectively `0.004105469352253462` and `0.010163571396223751`, while the maximum Gram error `max|QᵀQ-I|` is at most `6.89e-15`; that value is the (0,0) normalization entry alone, and the off-diagonal maximum is at most `2.8e-16` (see Metrics; response-01). Reference normalized evaluation-matrix condition numbers are respectively approximately `3.78e3` and `1.01e2`. Rot0.3 and both rational grids also have resolved drift relative to their reported reference diagnostic scales. The complete values and own-selection comparisons are linked below.
3. A unit weighted-norm target `f=Uv` selected post hoc from the reference polynomial span (v the leading right singular vector of `(I-P_upstream_yx)U`) has relative training residual equal to those gaps **by construction** (`||(I-WWᵀ)f||` is that singular value identically); the equality is not an independent finding, and the informative quantity is the off-sample error. **The original test set is the 128 explicit deterministic midpoints `(P[:128]+P[-128:])/2` of the node pairs `(P[i],P[-128+i])`. For the two hard cases the nodes are iid uniform in the support, and the midpoints are then concentrated towards the centre, but the law differs by geometry: for the iid native square (rotpi4, in native coordinates before the rotation) the midpoint has a product of two triangular densities, whereas for the iid triangle `T={x,y>=-1,x+y<=0}` it has the overlap-area density `area(T∩(2z-T))`, which is piecewise quadratic and not triangular (for example `4t²` at `z=(-1+t,-1+t)`, `0<t<=1/2`). For the deterministic T24/Q24 grids and the other deterministic benchmark node sets the midpoint sets have no iid sampling law. The test errors reported on the midpoint set are specific to that set.** On it, actual upstream recurrence evaluation gives relative errors approximately `1.014e-3` (rotpi4) and `1.569e-3` (triangle). MEASURED (response-01, `evidence/response-01-e6-offsample.json`): the same fits evaluated on 4000 uniform interior points (seed 2026110101) give `1.49e-2` and `2.45e-2` (about 15 times the midpoint values), and on a boundary-inclusive sample (about 2000 points on the edges, at the vertices and at inward distance `1e-6..1e-1`) `7.12e-3` and `2.30e-2`. The error is dominated by points near the vertices and is heavy-tailed: over ten disjoint 400-point blocks of the uniform sample the rotpi4 error ranges from `7.0e-4` to `2.1e-2` (triangle `2.2e-3` to `6.7e-2`). The fixed analytic target `exp(x+y)` has relative training and midpoint test errors at most `2.2e-15` on rot0.3, rotpi4 and triangle in the original runs; in the response-01 runs on rotpi4 and triangle its test error is at most `2.6e-14` on the uniform sample and `1.3e-13` on the boundary-inclusive sample (reference-span least-squares fit on the same samples: at most `7.3e-15` and `4.0e-14`; the upstream error is at most about 3.5 times the reference-span error). `exp(x+y)` is a ridge function aligned with the rotpi4 frame (there `x+y=sqrt(2)u` for the native square coordinate `u`, so it is univariate in the reference frame), and the selection chronology of this target relative to the results is not documented in LOG or provenance. One generic non-ridge entire target `exp(x)cos(2y)+sin(3xy)`, fixed in `e6_offsample.py` before any result was computed, is approximation-limited at these degrees (relative error `1.3e-12` to `1.0e-10` on rotpi4 and `5.2e-11` to `8.5e-9` on triangle, from midpoint to boundary samples) and the upstream prediction differs from the reference-span least-squares prediction by at most `5.0e-14` (uniform sample) and `3.7e-13` (boundary sample) relative to the target norm: no loss attributable to the span gap is resolved for it.

All statements are numerical observations for these pinned inputs and this execution environment. The adversarial target is represented numerically in a well-conditioned reference span, not by an exact symbolic polynomial certificate. No universal failure of polynomial fitting, all selections, or all reorthogonalization methods is asserted.

## Definitions

For positive sample weights w, the reference evaluation matrix contains `sqrt(w)` times all total-degree-at-most-n product-Chebyshev or Dubiner basis evaluations, column normalized before Householder QR. Inverse-rotated coordinates are used for the rotated-square Chebyshev references; triangle uses Dubiner. U denotes its QR orthonormal factor. W denotes the Householder orthonormal factor of the upstream computed basis Q. The measured gap is `||(I-WWᵀ)U||₂`, and orthogonality is `max|QᵀQ-I|`. This single number is dominated by the (0,0) entry (normalization of the first column), which is identical for every method and for the reference factor; the off-diagonal maximum is reported separately (response-01: rotpi4 `2.0e-16` for `[y,x]`, `2.5e-16` for `[x,y]`; triangle `2.8e-16`, `2.1e-16`; diagonal excluding (0,0) at most `8.9e-16`; the (0,0) entry is `6.883e-15` in all cases).

The common adversarial target for each case is `f=Uv`, where v is a leading right singular vector of `(I-P_upstream_yx)U`. Its weighted training norm is one; the saved v is applied to every compared method on that case. Training residual is `||(I-WWᵀ)f||₂/||f||₂`. Actual recurrence predictions use upstream `vandermonde_arnoldi_eval` and `np.linalg.lstsq` coefficients; target test values use the reference QR triangular solve. Test relative error divides the Euclidean error norm by the Euclidean norm of target values.

T24 is the rational grid `(i/12-1,j/12-1)` for integers i,j≥0 with i+j≤24, M = 325. Q24 rotates the 25×25 rational square grid by cosine 3/5 and sine 4/5, M = 625. Each coordinate is formed exactly using Fraction, then rounded once to binary64. The experiment therefore concerns that rounded measure. Test points are `(P[:128]+P[-128:])/2` (the midpoint test set: explicit deterministic midpoints of node pairs; centre-concentrated for the iid hard cases with the geometry-specific densities stated in Statement item 3, and without any sampling law for the deterministic T24/Q24 grids; test errors reported on it are specific to it). Response-01 adds a uniform interior sample and a boundary-inclusive sample; see Metrics.

## Assumptions

- Frozen existing seed, positive weights, points and maximal degree for every old L2 benchmark; n12 and n16 for the two additional grids.
- Binary64 upstream and own numerical routines in the manifested environment; `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`. The tested environment pins `numpy==2.3.5`, and ≥ 2.4 must be excluded: `_update_rule` in upstream polyrat at the pinned commit uses `int(np.argwhere(...))` (`polyrat/arnoldi.py:12`), which only warns under NumPy 2.3.5 but raises `TypeError: only 0-dimensional arrays can be converted to Python scalars` under NumPy 2.5.x (response-01) and, in the audit-02 E6 isolated probes, also under NumPy 2.4.0 (yanked) and the non-yanked 2.4.1 with the upstream source unchanged. A `<2.5` restriction is therefore insufficient; versions below 2.3.5 were not tested. E6 commands need `PYTHONPATH=/workspace/padua-drift-runs/python-deps` (NumPy 2.3.5) in this workspace.
- Actual upstream code is imported unchanged through the documented namespace-package adapter. This bypasses unrelated unavailable rational-solver package imports, and is not a full polyrat installation test.
- Reference conditioning and numerical rank are evaluated explicitly. `u*kappa` is a diagnostic error scale, not a rigorous forward-error enclosure. Raw outputs with a failed reference screen do not certify a distance to the exact polynomial span.
- Midpoint tests on triangle and rotated square stay in the respective convex domain. Tests on nonconvex L-shape and annulus can leave the sampling domain; their errors do not receive the same in-domain interpretation.

## Scope

This result confirms earlier own-code drift in an actual retrieved upstream implementation and distinguishes worst reference-polynomial approximation loss from accurate fitting of the tested smooth functions (`exp(x+y)`, one generic entire target). Test errors for the worst-polynomial target depend strongly on the test distribution (midpoint, uniform and boundary-inclusive samples differ by a factor of about 15 and are heavy-tailed). It does not identify polyrat with ZN's unavailable implementation. Column order and coordinate orientation remain part of every implementation comparison. The cubic-checkerboard smooth test's enormous off-sample error is a separate severe-conditioning observation; its failed reference rank prevents attributing it to a trusted exact-span gap.
