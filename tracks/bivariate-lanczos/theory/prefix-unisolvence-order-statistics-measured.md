## Statement

All parts [FP/numerical-rank, MEASURED] within the scope below.

(A) *Prefix statistics.* For Padua first-family sets Pad_n, n = 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 50 (M = N = 66, 136, 231, 496, 861, 1326), prefix K of an order = first K nodes versus the first K graded monomials; 'numerically singular' = numerical estimate of σ_min < 1e-11 for the leading K×K block of the row-permuted orthogonal product-Chebyshev matrix Q_c (a direct floating-point SVD, or the floating-point lower/upper estimates described under Definitions; no rounding-error enclosure; observed: every numerically singular prefix has an estimate ≤ 1e-15, nothing in the interval 1e-15..1e-11 at the scanned prefixes; n_amb = 0 for all 54 orders (9 orders × 6 sizes)). (i) The structured orders `given` and `sortx` are singular at almost all prefixes: 58/66, 126/136 (125 sortx), 218/231, 478/496, 838/861 (`sortx` 838), 1298/1326 prefixes (the combinatorial line criterion — K nodes on c vertical lines with c(c+1)/2 < K, an exact statement about the nodes and separate from the numerical estimates — identifies ≥ 99% of them; see `prefix.py`). (ii) Random orders (rand1–3, 18 orders at n = 10..50): all prefixes numerically unisolvent in 16 of 18 order/size cases (3 orders × 6 sizes), with exceptions n = 15 rand2 (4 singular prefixes, K = 127..130) and n = 40 rand2 (1 singular prefix, K = 2), and the smallest numerical σ_min estimate among the refined/scanned prefixes of an order falls from ≈ 1e-3 (n = 10) to 2e-7..6e-7 (n = 30), 7e-8..4e-7 (n = 40) and 3e-9..3.5e-8 (n = 50). Fragility of the binary classification (finding M4): n = 40 rand2 is 'non-unisolvent' only because its K = 2 prefix has two nodes with the same x coordinate, yet its 'largest' ratio (9.6e2, 1.5e3) equals that of the unisolvent rand1 and rand3; the classification tolerance used in the joins is a numerical estimate sigma_min < 1e-11 for 'singular', not the pre-registered all-prefixes >= 1e-8 (finding L5; 14 of 16 catastrophes on all-unisolvent orders also pass the pre-registered 1e-8 rule, and the 8 gepp/rpiv cases at n = 50 do not depend on any tolerance). (iii) The constructed orders gepp (Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting on Q_c) and rpiv1–3 (randomised threshold pivoting, θ = 0.1) have all prefixes numerically unisolvent (no estimate below 1e-11) with smallest numerical σ_min estimates among the refined/scanned prefixes 1.3e-1, 7.8e-2, 6.2e-2, 2.9e-2, 1.5e-2, 7.5e-3 (gepp) and ≥ 4.7e-4 (rpiv, n ≤ 50). Independent floating-point check (Codex audit-01, `audit-01/L5/kill-rerun.jsonl`): all-prefix LU lower estimates for the developer's GEPP order of 6.33e-5 (n = 30) and 1.1564e-5 (n = 50; max-entry factorization residual 4.62e-15, so even the coarse correction N·residual is only 6.13e-12); these are lower estimates consistent with the values above, not exact or interval certificates, and the audit does not certify all prefixes of the n = 60 REPRO order.

(B) *Online detector.* 'Prefix K (K ≤ n=10,14,20 sets) is numerically singular ⇔ min over the monomials j ≤ K of the primary pivot |V(j)[j, p(j)]| (and |w_1| for j = 1) of the 'scheme' Givens state after K points is < 1e-10' holds without exception on 20 order/size cases (n = 10: given, sortx, rand1–3, gepp, rpiv1; n = 14: same; n = 20: given, sortx, rand1, rand2, gepp, rpiv1; `logs/r03_detector.log`): all 58 / 58, 110 / 110, 218 / 218 numerically singular prefixes flagged, all numerically singular prefixes of the random orders flagged (n = 14: 1 and 2), no prefix classified numerically unisolvent flagged. The *newest* pivot alone (monomial K) is **not** a detector (n = 10 given: 6 of 58 singular prefixes have it < 1e-10; n = 14: 10 of 110). The min-pivot is also correlated with the numerical σ_min estimate on unisolvent prefixes (Spearman 0.3..0.9, order dependent) — a conditioning proxy, not an estimate.

(C) *Effect on the 'scheme' rule.* On the 40 (n, order, weight) cases with all prefixes numerically unisolvent and n ≥ 30 (3 random + gepp + 3 rpiv at n = 30, 40, 50 minus the singular rand2 at n = 40, cubature and U(0.1,1) weights), Spearman correlation of log10(scheme error) with log10(smallest numerical prefix σ_min estimate) is −0.51 (p = 9e-4); at n = 50 alone −0.71 (14 cases: scheme error 1.1e-13..1.3e-13 for gepp/rpiv with σ_min ≥ 4.7e-4 versus 3.1e-13, 3.7e-13, 1.6e-12 (cub) and 3.4e-12 (U) for the random orders with numerical σ_min estimates 3e-9..3.5e-8). The REPRO outlier (`topics/…/repro/REPRO.md`: Padua n = 60, uniform weights, random order from `default_rng(2)`): scheme 2.0e-12 and largest 2.1e-4 (error versus the Householder H) were reproduced bit-for-bit in magnitude by `diag_repro_x60.py`; its prefix scan (1008 s, floating point) found no numerically singular prefix and a smallest numerical σ_min estimate among the refined prefixes of 9.7e-10 (158 prefixes with numerical upper estimate < 1e-6, 2 below 1e-8): the smallest value among the refined/scanned prefixes in this study (not an exact or interval certificate for the other prefixes). Three further random n = 60 orders gave scheme errors 2.0e-11, 5.1e-13, 1.6e-12 (prefix scans not run) and the gepp order 2.0e-13 (`logs/r03_x60.json`, `logs/r03_x60_controls.jsonl`; R03.md): the REPRO value lies within the range of these four random draws (5e-13..2e-11; the sigma_min of the three controls is not measured, reconstruction finding L6). 

(D) *Precision sensitivity of intermediate states on streams with numerically singular prefixes (observation; mathematical discontinuity is not shown).* Running double and long-double Givens in lockstep on the same input doubles (`exp_r02_dual.py`, `logs/r02_dual.jsonl`; the two precisions see exactly the same data, so this is not a limiting sequence of perturbed exact data), the active (H_x, H_y) blocks differ by relative Frobenius 0.24–0.6 at every checkpoint before the end for the `given` order at n = 15, 20, 30, 40 while the *final* matrices agree to 7.9e-15, 9.9e-15, 2.3e-14, 4.1e-14 (same as the error against the reference). For the unisolvent `gepp` order the lockstep difference shows an overall increasing trend with local decreases in the number i of inserted points (three decreasing checkpoint steps each at n = 15 and n = 20, one at n = 30 (i = 162 → 214: 2.5685960e-14 → 2.3156126e-14), none at n = 40; `logs/analysis_r01d.txt`) with fitted log–log slope 0.83–0.87 (gepp, n = 30, 40) and 0.65–0.70 (gepp, n = 15, 20); for random orders the difference reaches 1e-12..1e-11 in mid-run (n = 40) before dropping to 1e-13 at i = M.

## Definitions

Q_c: the M×M orthogonal matrix diag(√w_cub)·V·D with V the product-Chebyshev basis T_a(x)T_b(y) in graded lex order (x-degree descending), w_cub the Padua cubature weights, D the diagonal making Q_c orthonormal (only (n,0) has norm² 2). Prefix block B_K = Q_c[perm[:K], :K]. Numerical σ_min estimate (earlier wording: 'certified σ_min'): either a direct floating-point SVD of B_K (lo = hi, no error enclosure), or the lower/upper estimates σ_S ≥ σ_min(B_K) ≥ 1/((1+b)² max(b, 1/σ_S)) from a bordered-inverse chain. The two inequalities are reasonable statements in exact arithmetic for the exact matrix, but `prefix.py` evaluates b, σ_S and the block inverses with ordinary floating-point inverses, products and SVDs without enclosing their errors, so the output are numerical lower/upper estimates, not exact values or interval enclosures (validated against exhaustive floating-point SVD on 18 order/size cases at n = 6, 10, 14: 0 classification mismatches), refined by SVD where the estimates straddle the tolerance and at the ≤ 150 prefixes with the smallest upper estimate. 'Smallest σ_min' of an order always means the smallest value among the refined/scanned prefixes. The identifiers `smin_exact` and the words 'certified'/'exact' in the code docstrings of `prefix.py`, `exp_r01b.py`, `analyze_r01b.py`, `diag_*.py` are unchanged historical wording and mean 'direct floating-point SVD' and 'numerical bound'. The combinatorial line criterion is the only exact singularity argument in the scan. Singular/unisolvent for the weights: both are weight-independent (positive row scaling).

## Assumptions

The entries of Q_c are computed in IEEE double. The earlier assumption 'entries of Q_c with errors ≲ 3u, so certified σ_min ≥ 1e-11 implies nonsingular for K ≤ 2016 (‖E‖₂ ≲ K·3u ≈ 1e-12)' is **withdrawn as a bound** (audit-01 L5 M2): it was not demonstrated. Codex audit-01's 80-digit spot checks of the largest-error entry against exact Padua angles, cubature weights and analytic normalisation give 17.11u (n = 30), 13.69u (n = 50) and 22.60u (n = 62) (5.58u at n = 10; against evaluation on the same stored double nodes and weights 3.32u at n = 10 and 3.62u at n = 62; `audit-01/L5/diagnostics.jsonl`; themselves numerical). A crude ‖E‖₂ ≤ K·max|E_ij| with the n = 62 value is about 5e-12 at K = 2016, the same order as the 1e-11 tolerance. Therefore all classifications below are floating-point classifications at the stated tolerance; none is an enclosure of the underlying real matrix, and the 'clean gap' (no estimate between 1e-15 and 1e-11) is an empirical observation supporting the classification, not a proof. A rigorous route would bound both the matrix-construction and the linear-algebra errors (with residual corrections) or use exact/interval arithmetic. No high-precision (mpmath) cross-check of the classification was made (the pre-registered cross-check at M = 66, 136, 231 was replaced by the exhaustive-SVD validation and the clean gap above; stated as a deviation).
