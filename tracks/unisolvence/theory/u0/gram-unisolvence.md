## Statement

Let `n>=1` and let `K` have characteristic zero or characteristic `p` not dividing `2n(n+1)`. In an algebraic closure choose roots `zeta,eta` of exact orders `2n,2(n+1)`. Set `x_j=(zeta^j+zeta^-j)/2` for `0<=j<=n`, `y_k=(eta^k+eta^-k)/2` for `0<=k<=n+1`, and `S={(x_j,y_k):j+k even}`. Over the field `E` generated by their coordinates, `S` has `N=(n+1)(n+2)/2` distinct points and is unisolvent for `P_n(E)`.

More precisely, let `V_(j,k),(a,b)=T_a(x_j)T_b(y_k)`, `a+b<=n`, with `T_0=1,T_1=t,T_(r+1)=2tT_r-T_(r-1)`. Put `e_i^(m)=1/2` at `i=0,m` and `1` otherwise, and `w_jk=2e_j^(n)e_k^(n+1)/(n(n+1))`. Then `V^t W V=D`, where `D_(a,b)=c_a c_b`, `c_0=1,c_r=1/2 (r>0)`, except `D_(n,0)=1`.

The weighted moment of `T_a(x)T_b(y)` equals `delta_(a,0)delta_(b,0)` for `a+b<=2n+1`, except at `(2n,0)` and `(n,n+1)`, where it equals `1`.

## Definitions and assumptions

Unisolvent means evaluation `P_n(E)->E^S` is an isomorphism. We assume actual roots of the stated orders, rather than the source implementation's narrower availability inside `F_(p^2)`. Rationality over a particular `F_q` is a separate claim in [rationality.md](rationality.md).

## Proof

For a primitive `2m`-th root `rho`, write `c_j=(rho^j+rho^-j)/2`. The recurrence gives `T_r(c_j)=(rho^(rj)+rho^(-rj))/2`. An endpoint-half sum is half the full inverse-symmetric cyclic sum, so

`A_m(r):=(1/m) sum_(j=0)^m e_j^(m) T_r(c_j) = [2m divides r]`.

Indeed `sum_(j=0)^(2m-1) rho^(rj)` is `2m` when `2m|r` and zero otherwise, by the geometric-series formula. All divisions used are permitted. Also `(-1)^j T_r(c_j)=T_(r+m)(c_j)`, hence

`(1/m) sum e_j^(m) (-1)^j T_r(c_j) = [r congruent m modulo 2m]`.

Writing the checkerboard indicator as `(1+(-1)^(j+k))/2`, its weighted moment is therefore exactly

`M(a,b)=[2n|a][2(n+1)|b] + [a congruent n mod 2n][b congruent n+1 mod 2(n+1)]`.

For nonnegative `a,b` with `a+b<=2n+1`, the first term survives only at `(0,0),(2n,0)` and the second only at `(n,n+1)`. This proves the moment statement.

For two columns indexed by `(a,b),(c,d)` of total degrees at most `n`, apply `T_rT_s=(T_(r+s)+T_|r-s|)/2` in each coordinate. All resulting moment indices have sum at most `2n`; the ordinary `(0,0)` contribution gives `c_a c_b` on the diagonal and zero off it. The only additional moment is `(2n,0)`, which can occur only for `(a,b)=(c,d)=(n,0)`. In that case `T_n(x_j)^2=1`, giving diagonal entry `1`. Thus the stated Gram identity holds.

If `c_j=c_l`, then `rho^j,rho^l` solve the same quadratic `t^2-2c_j t+1`; hence `rho^j=rho^l` or `rho^j=rho^-l`. With `j,l` between `0,m`, either equality forces `j=l`. Thus each axis has distinct nodes. The parity checkerboard in consecutive sizes `n+1,n+2` has exactly half their product, since one size is even. Therefore `|S|=N`. Every weight and every diagonal entry of `D` is nonzero. Consequently `det(V)^2 det(W)=det(D)!=0`. The Chebyshev products form a basis of `P_n` because their leading coefficients are nonzero powers of `2`. This proves unisolvence.
