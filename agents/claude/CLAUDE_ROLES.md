# Claude development and audit roles

Read `AGENTS.md` and `agents/WORKFLOW.md` at the working branch head before acting, and record the SHA. State whether you are orchestrator, developer, or independent auditor. Claude audits Codex development and Codex audits Claude development; independent subagent checks supplement that handoff. Follow the reply format, Git restrictions, evidence rules, and WORK/AUDIT/PR procedure. Only the orchestrator performs Git operations; AC alone merges.

## Development

Use Sonnet 5.5 high for code and technical development, Sonnet 5.5 medium for mechanical work, and Opus 5.5 high or xhigh for mathematics. Record the model and effort. Delegate bounded tasks with explicit inputs, expected artifacts, acceptance criteria, and disjoint ownership; do not delegate Git authority.

1. Read definitions, the requested scope, relevant theory, and source dependencies. Before ideas, scan all `state/IDEAS.md` headings and aliases, search mechanism terms and synonyms, and read full shortlisted entries and obstruction tables. Log that gate; a renamed known mechanism does not pass it.
2. Generate candidates with `templates/IDEA.md` and retain every candidate, including failed, equivalent, deferred, and rejected ones. Give each a closest predecessor, a mathematical separation criterion, and a kill test. A negative result must state exactly what was attempted and the assumptions, scope, evidence, and remaining alternatives.
3. Test the distinguishing criterion before investing in a proof. For surviving candidates write exact claims, assumptions, proof obligations, counterexample searches, and verification plans. Preserve abandoned proof routes and their specific obstructions.
4. Develop and verify with actual execution records. Pin SHA and complete fetched-file lists for runs without a clone. Record commands, inputs, environment, outputs, and seeds. Put unexecuted work under Runs needed. Use independent reruns for consequential computation; never call output inspection a rerun. A computer proof requires a proved finite reduction, exact arithmetic, committed checker and evidence records, and independent rerun.
5. Write durable theory using `templates/THEORY.md`. Split substantive PROVED claims into their own files/IDs and keep PROVED; retain the unresolved original target, dependencies, split mappings and wording history. Only minor routine proved steps may stay with the main statement, under the workflow's classification and aggregation rules. Use epistemic statuses `PROVED`, `PARTIAL`, `CONDITIONAL`, `MEASURED` or `OPEN`; PARTIAL requires PROVED and OPEN mathematical parts only. Record REJECTED separately as a disposition with a reason; a `PD-N` file carries the status of its negative statement. Label cited facts `LITERATURE`, identifying the source and what was independently checked, without importing a proof by implication. Maintain idea and topic records, including all failed candidates.
6. Request audit from another agent using the exact SHA/hashes, claims, dependency list, evidence manifest, and reproduction commands. Address findings explicitly. At PR preparation reconcile IDs and shared registries against current state. The orchestrator commits, pushes the topic branch, and opens the PR; a draft may disclose pending audit. Do not merge.

## Independent audit

The auditor chooses the model and depth: Sonnet 5.5 high or Opus 5.5 high for protocol/technical audit; Opus 5.5 high or xhigh for mathematics; Opus 5.5 xhigh for adversarial analysis or a novel mechanism. Record the choice and its reason. The auditor must be a different agent from the developer.

Read the full `state/IDEAS.md` whenever scope includes ideas, novelty, mechanisms, or negative claims, then inspect relevant full entries, theory, and obstruction tables. If there is no novelty content, state that scope and why the full read is omitted. Reconstruct the exact claim independently. Challenge novelty separation, kill tests, omitted candidates, assumptions, quantifiers, edge cases, proof dependencies, and the scope of negative conclusions.

Inspect code and independently execute consequential checks. Record what actually ran and what remains untested. Check claimed computer proofs against the proved reduction, exactness, committed checker/evidence, and independent rerun requirements. Check output manifests, hashes, file limits, storage retrieval, status labels, and source attribution. A numerical match does not prove a universal claim.

Return an AUDIT record with model/effort, candidate SHA/hashes, read coverage, actual rerun commands/results, findings with locations and evidence, and remaining uncertainty. Require development responses and review material changes; do not conflate fixing a proof with independently approving it. Subagent auditors do not alter Git state. Readiness concerns the stated scope only and never authorizes a merge.
